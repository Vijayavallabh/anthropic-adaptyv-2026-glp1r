# Conformational-state-selective / two-domain / biased-agonist de novo design SOTA (for de novo GLP1R agonist, Adaptyv Challenge 3)

Scope: latest (2024–Oct 2026) de novo methods for (a) conformation-state-selective / active-state-biased binders, (b) partial/biased agonists, (c) two-domain "address+message" agonist constructs, and (d) any new de novo GPCR agonism precedent. Tiers: **[PR]** peer-reviewed; **[pre]** preprint/workshop; **[HYP]** my hypothesis/reasoning; **[in-silico]** computational-only (no wet validation). Every tool is flagged for license + install. BINDING vs ACTIVATION is distinguished throughout.

Project constraints kept in view: ≤20 de novo zero-shot designs, 10–250 aa, natural AA, **LINEAR or DISULFIDE only** (no head-to-tail/backbone cyclization), no grafting of GLP-1/exendin/semaglutide, no native agonist–receptor complex as template/motif/backbone, submitted-design tools must be **permissive-license (MIT/Apache/BSD/CC-BY)** — AF3 official weights, Protenix-v2, ESM-3, ESMC-600M are OUT (AF2/AF-Multimer params = CC-BY-4.0 are IN).

## Q1. Methods that design binders SELECTIVE for one conformational state (active-state / negative-design / multistate), and GPCR evidence

### Takeaway
A dedicated, generator-agnostic conformation-selective design method now exists (**AlloGen**, ICML 2026 — holo vs apo calmodulin), and the state-switching framework **SwitchCraft** (ICML 2026) adds explicit positive/negative-allostery primitives, but neither is wet-validated on a GPCR. For GPCRs the practical levers are: (i) design directly against an **active-state receptor structure** (Baker's own stated rationale — "active-state stabilization alone would be sufficient to generate agonists" — worked at class A), and (ii) de novo **active-state-selective GPCR binders already exist** (Baker "G-protein/Gα mimetics"; and biologically, active-state nanobodies Nb80/Nb6B9). No published method does negative design against the *inactive 7TM pocket* to bias agonism (see Q3).

### Cited Findings
- **AlloGen: Conformation-Selective Binder Design with Differential State Scoring** (Hanqun Cao, Aastha Pal, …, Pranam Chatterjee), ICML 2026 workshop "Generative and Agentic AI for Biology." Decouples backbone generation from a learned, differentiable **state-selectivity scorer Q_θ** (SE(3)-invariant interface graph transformer), trained in two phases (interface geometry first, then conformational discrimination). The scorer is **generator-agnostic** and can act as a passive re-ranker OR an active gradient guide "without retraining." Validated on **calmodulin: designed peptides bind the holo state with no detectable apo binding.** GPCRs are named only as an example target class (alongside kinases/nuclear receptors); **no GPCR experiment**; no code/license on the page; validation detail (wet vs in-silico) not disclosed — [ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/70800); [OpenReview id OOfdPJlcN1](https://openreview.net/forum?id=OOfdPJlcN1); [emergentmind summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/allogen)
- **SwitchCraft: A Programmatic Framework for Designing State-Switching Proteins** (Bowen Jing, Mihir Bafna, et al.), arXiv:2605.31236 (29 May 2026), ICML 2026 poster. Backpropagates through structure-prediction models to design proteins that switch between functional states. Design primitives include **positive/negative allostery**: scaffolding a motif is *enhanced* (positive) or *disrupted* (negative) by an effector. Allostery benchmark: 5 ligands × 24 RFdiffusion motifs, 100 seqs each → 11 motifs with ≥1 success; negative-allostery success = motif intact in one state, disrupted in the other (Δmotif-RMSD > 1 Å). **In-silico only (no wet validation); not a receptor agonist; not GPCR** — [arXiv:2605.31236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31236); [ICML poster 63230](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/63230)
- **Baker GPCR paper (active-state rationale).** For class A agonists they designed against **active-state receptor structures** ("MRGPRX1 designs used three active-state structures, PDB 8DWC/8DWG/8DWH"), "reasoning that active-state stabilization alone would be sufficient to generate agonists." The Discussion explicitly states **agonists are hard because they require conformational selectivity** — [bioRxiv 2025.03.23.644666 full text](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.23.644666v1.full) (now [Nature 2026, s41586-026-10656-8](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10656-8))
- **De novo active-state-selective GPCR binders exist (Baker "Gα mimetics").** A Baker-lab webinar describes de novo designed **G-alpha mimetics that are thermostable, selectively bind the ACTIVE state of GPCRs, and bind a wide range of GPCRs** — i.e., state-selective GPCR binding is already a solved de novo problem on the *intracellular* face — [Twist webinar listing](https://www.twistbioscience.com/resources/webinar/de-novo-design-g-protein-mimetics)
- **Flexible-target / conformation-specific RFdiffusion** (Nature 2025, 10.1038/s41586-025-09248-9, IPD-hosted). A fine-tuned RFdiffusion for binders to **conformationally variable targets** (incl. intrinsically disordered peptides), reporting **picomolar-affinity binders to helical peptide targets**, and uses **partial diffusion** to refine inputs; authors state the ability to "design binders to conformationally variable targets" should be broadly useful — [IPD PDF (s41586-025-09248-9)](https://www.ipd.uw.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/s41586-025-09248-9-compressed.pdf) *(PDF would not text-parse this session; claims taken from IPD hosting + search abstract — verify exact title/affinity before quoting)*
- **Biological precedent — active-state-selective nanobodies.** Nb80 binds agonist-occupied β2AR but not inverse-agonist-bound (yeast-display: strong with full agonist BI-167107, minimal with carazolol); it partly traps the active state even without ligand. Affinity-matured **Nb6B9** (yeast-display conformation-specific selection) gave a 2.8 Å active-state β2AR structure; Nb80 also active-state-stabilizes β1AR without changing antagonist affinity. These are **selection-derived, intracellular-face binders (G-protein mimics), NOT agonists** — [Steyaert/Kobilka patent texts, USPTO 9863959/10054598/10436796](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10436796); [Ring/FredHutch PDF](https://research.fredhutch.org/content/dam/stripe/ring/publications/15-2013.pdf); [β1AR native-MS probe study 2026, 10.1021/acs.analchem.6c04571](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/native-mass-spectrometry-reveals-ligand-specific-conformational-heterogeneity-of-the-%CE%B21-adrenergic-receptor-using-nanobody-probes/)
- **Multistate/negative-design foundations.** A generic multistate-design program (PLoS ONE 2011) tested negative design and found accuracy improves as undesired interactions cover more conformations, while noting **negative design is harder than positive design**; MSD-FASTER (Allen & Mayo) states explicit negative design targets structural/binding/catalytic specificity — [PLoS ONE 2011 (PMC3130737)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3130737/); [MSD-FASTER (Caltech)](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/zsj9r-zmd85)
- **Chamaileon: Cross-Context Binder Design** (arXiv 2026) — frames multistate design as keeping a binder compatible across a target's conformational landscape. *Search-surfaced only; NOT independently verified this session* — [arXiv:2607.23518](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.23518)

### Inferences
- **The eligible, directly usable lever for GLP1R is "design against an active-state 7TM receptor structure"** using a permissive generator (RFdiffusion/BoltzGen), optionally with partial diffusion of your *own* backbone. This is allowed because targeting the active-state *receptor* surface is NOT the same as using the native *agonist–receptor complex* as a template/motif/backbone — the latter is banned, the former is not. [HYP: eligibility reading]
- AlloGen's **re-rank-or-gradient-guide scorer is the single most on-target published idea** for "prefer active, reject inactive," and is generator-agnostic so it could sit on top of an eligible permissive generator — but it is unvalidated on GPCRs, has no released code/license yet, and would have to be reimplemented. [HYP]
- GPCR state-selective *binding* is demonstrably achievable (Gα mimetics, Nb80); the unsolved step is converting state-selective binding at the *extracellular/orthosteric* face into *activation*. [inference]

### Gaps
- No wet-validated de novo method that explicitly negative-designs against an inactive GPCR 7TM pocket to bias agonism (AlloGen is calmodulin; SwitchCraft is in-silico).
- Could not parse the flexible-target RFdiffusion PDF directly — exact title/authors/affinity numbers should be re-confirmed from the DOI.
- AlloGen's validation modality (wet-lab vs in-silico) and license are unknown.

## Q2. De novo TWO-DOMAIN "address+message" agonist with controlled geometric register

### Takeaway
**No de novo-designed two-domain (ECD-address + 7TM-message) GPCR agonist with a controlled register has been demonstrated** — this is genuinely unprecedented. The message–address *concept* is classical (Schwyzer peptides; Portoghese bivalent opioids) and register is controlled there via rigid linker geometry/length; the only de novo protein precedents that control an inter-module geometry are **cytokine-receptor agonists tuned by dimerization geometry** (G-CSFR) and **split/two-component cytokine mimics** — mechanisms that cluster receptor chains, NOT thread a message into a pocket. Notably, EvoBind reached class-B agonism **without** a two-domain architecture (one compact cyclic peptide in the orthosteric pocket).

### Cited Findings
- **Message–address concept + register control (classical, small-molecule/peptide).** Portoghese's bivalent-ligand work maps Schwyzer's concept: the **"message" binds the orthosteric site; the "address" adds affinity/selectivity**; register is tuned by **linker geometry and rigidity** (ortho/meta/para, cis/trans, number of accessible conformers). Bivalent-ligand patents give spacer rules: ~3–12 Å (preferred) for two sites on one receptor, ~30–70 Å for sites on separate proteins — [Portoghese message-address (UMN)](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/bivalent-ligands-and-the-message-address-concept-in-the-design-of/); [β2AR bivalent-agonist patent US7105701](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7105701)
- **De novo cytokine agonist with geometry as the control variable (G-CSFR).** [PR] Designers **varied receptor-binding affinity AND dimerization geometry** to produce hyper-thermostable agonists with **altered signaling specificities** (bias toward granulopoietic differentiation over proliferation), able to outcompete native G-CSF — i.e., inter-module geometry sets signaling output — [Mol Ther 2025, 10.1016/j.ymthe.2025.08.031 (PMC12628175)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12628175/)
- **Two-component / split de novo cytokine mimic.** A de novo IL-2 mimetic was split into **two components that are inactive alone and regain activity when co-localized** — an explicit two-module agonism design (co-localization register) — [UW thesis](https://digital.lib.washington.edu/researchworks/items/7661a0c4-fde3-4b0d-9a65-cb9b65c978f4/full)
- **Foundational de novo cytokine agonist (Neo-2/15).** [PR] De novo IL-2/IL-15 mimic binds IL-2Rβγc but has **no α-subunit site** — agonism by recreating the functional receptor-engaging epitope de novo (not grafted), i.e., single-module surface mimicry that drives receptor assembly — [Silva et al., Nature 2019 (bakerlab PDF)](https://www.bakerlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Silva2018_IL2-15.pdf)
- **EvoBind achieved class-B agonism WITHOUT two domains.** The 18-mer cyclic peptide is predicted to sit **deep in the GCGR/GLP1R orthosteric pocket** and induce an active-state-like conformation — a single compact element, not an address+message pair — [bioRxiv 2025.06.06.658268](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.06.658268) (see Q4).
- **Depth/insertion controlled only by scaffold selection (Baker).** For CXCR4 they selected pentamer scaffolds giving "the **deepest insertion** within the binding pocket" based on distance to hotspot residues — the nearest thing to explicit depth control, but still single-module — [bioRxiv 2025.03.23.644666](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.23.644666v1.full)

### Inferences
- **Geometric register in de novo protein design is today controlled one of three ways:** (i) a single continuous diffused backbone that is forced to contact two subsites at once (hotspot/epitope specification, not a true two-module construct); (ii) rigid fusion/linker engineering between two pre-made modules (the cytokine route); (iii) co-localization/clustering geometry (dimerization, split constructs). None of these has been shown to place a *message* at a defined depth under a cap like GLP1R's Trp33 while an *address* holds the ECD. [HYP]
- A de novo two-domain GLP1R agonist (de novo ECD-binding helix/mini-domain rigidly fused to a de novo message element threading the 7TM pocket) would be **novel method territory**; the hardest unsolved sub-problem is holding the message at the correct register/depth while the address docks the ECD — the cytokine literature shows geometry *tuning* is feasible in principle, but only for clustering, not pocket-threading. [HYP]
- Eligibility note: a two-*domain* construct is still allowed if it is one chain ≤250 aa, linear or disulfide-bonded, and neither module is a GLP-1/exendin fragment. [HYP]

### Gaps
- No published de novo GPCR agonist built as address+message with an engineered register — absence confirmed across searches, not a positive statement that it is impossible.
- No data on what linker/register tolerance a de novo GLP1R message element would need (the ~3–12 Å bivalent-ligand rule is small-molecule, not transferable to a protein spanning ECD→TM, which is tens of Å).

## Q3. Is "negative design" (bind active-state AND NOT fit the inactive-state pocket) demonstrated as a route to agonism?

### Takeaway
**Not demonstrated as a route to GPCR agonism.** The closest wet-adjacent result is AlloGen's holo-selective/apo-rejecting calmodulin peptides (conformational selectivity, not receptor activation). SwitchCraft formalizes negative allostery as a design primitive but is in-silico and not an agonist. Baker's successful class-A agonism used **positive** design against the active state ("active-state stabilization alone sufficient"), not explicit negative design against the inactive pocket.

### Cited Findings
- **AlloGen** — explicitly "identifies binders that prefer the desired state while rejecting alternative conformations"; calmodulin peptides **bind holo, no detectable apo binding**. This IS conformation-selective (a form of implicit negative design against the off-state), but on calmodulin, **not a receptor-activation readout** — [ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/70800)
- **SwitchCraft** — negative-allostery primitive: effector *disrupts* the motif; success = motif intact in state A, disrupted in state B (Δ RMSD > 1 Å). In-silico; not agonism — [arXiv:2605.31236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31236)
- **Baker: agonism via positive active-state design, not negative design.** Class-A agonists designed by stabilizing active-state structures (8DWC/8DWG/8DWH); Discussion frames agonism as *requiring conformational selectivity* but the executed strategy is positive selection for the active state, with no inactive-state negative-design term reported — [bioRxiv 2025.03.23.644666](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.23.644666v1.full)
- **Multistate/negative-design is established but hard.** "Negative design has proven considerably more challenging than positive design" — [PLoS ONE 2011 (PMC3130737)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3130737/)

### Inferences
- A concrete, eligible instantiation for GLP1R: generate/score candidates for **high predicted complementarity to the active-state 7TM pocket** AND **penalize fit to the inactive-state pocket** (dual co-fold deltas against active vs inactive receptor templates; the active-state-minus-inactive-state score is the AlloGen "differential state scoring" idea). This is implementable with permissive co-folders as a *selection filter* even if no generator does it natively. [HYP]
- Because current GPCR structure prediction poorly distinguishes agonist efficacy (EvoBind's 15-mer bound but didn't activate GCGR; see Q4), a negative-design/differential-state *score* is a hypothesis-generator, not a reliable efficacy oracle. [HYP, supported by EvoBind pIDDT-vs-activity mismatch below]

### Gaps
- No demonstration that "bind active, not inactive" negative design yields a functional agonist (any receptor class, wet-lab).

## Q4. Newest (mid-2025 → Oct 2026) de novo / engineered AGONISM of any GPCR, especially class B

### Takeaway
**The single most important precedent for this project**: Bryant lab's **EvoBind dual GCGR/GLP1R cyclic peptide agonists** — the first blind de novo **functional agonists** of a **class B** GPCR (cAMP-confirmed; 18-mer EC50 ≈ 32 nM at GLP1R, approaching liraglutide's 2.4 nM), and **G-protein biased** (no β-arrestin). Baker's Nature 2026 paper remains **class A agonists only** (MRGPRX1; some versions also NK1R/CCR5) with **class B = antagonists** (GLP1R/GIPR/GCGR). A 2026 conference poster claims de novo **RXFP1** (class A/LGR) agonists via active-state stabilization. No other new class-B de novo protein agonist found.

### Cited Findings
- **[pre] EvoBind dual cyclic peptide agonists of GCGR + GLP1R** (Qiuzhen Li, Elisee Wiita, Thomas Helleday, Patrick Bryant), bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.06.06.658268, posted **8 June 2025**, CC-BY-NC-4.0. Method: **EvoBind** (AF2 + AF-Multimer), **sequence-only, blind, no templates/site info** — only the GCGR sequence (from PDB 8JIQ) as input; cyclic peptides via cyclic offset; 33 lengths (8–40 aa) × 5 seeds × 5000 iters = 825,000 steps; top-10% by loss, pLDDT ≥ 85 → 20,387 candidates; AF-Multimer adversarial filter; 3 synthesized (15/18/31-mer). **ACTIVATION confirmed by cAMP**: 18-mer EC50 = **32 nM at GLP1R** and **542 nM at GCGR**; all three activate GLP1R (15-mer 11.8 µM, 31-mer 5.0 µM); 15-mer does NOT activate GCGR. Liraglutide GLP1R EC50 = 2.4 nM. **Biased agonism: none of the designs recruited β-arrestin** (liraglutide EC50 = 20.66 nM) → G-protein-biased. Designs predicted to bind **deep in the orthosteric pocket**, inducing active-state-like conformation (RMSD ~2 Å vs 8JIQ); **"sequences show no similarity to known agonists."** Key honesty caveat: the 15-mer **binds GCGR but is inactive**, and the 15-mer activates GLP1R despite not being designed for it — authors state **static pIDDT/structure "insufficient to explain differences in receptor activation"** — [bioRxiv 2025.06.06.658268](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.06.658268)
- **[PR] Baker "De novo design of miniproteins targeting GPCRs"** (Nature 2026, 10.1038/s41586-026-10656-8; preprint bioRxiv 2025.03.23.644666, Mar 2025). **MRGPRX1 agonists (class A)** + **CXCR4, GLP1R, GIPR, GCGR, CGRPR ANTAGONISTS**; some abstract versions also list **NK1R and CCR5 agonists** (all class A). Cryo-EM confirmed 5 receptor-bound designs incl. **CGRPR antagonist and MRGPRX1 agonist**. GLP1R binders are **ECD-targeted antagonists**: dGl1_024 (27 nM), mGl1_008 (5.3 nM), ~2% hit (2/96 expressed) — [Nature 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10656-8); [IPD PDF](https://www.ipd.uw.edu/publication-pdfs/432/db1453519cebe452e987a99f60ceb945/De%20novo%20design%20of%20miniproteins%20targeting%20GPCRs.pdf); PDB 9MNI (CGRPR), 22XC (CXCR4); [GEN coverage](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/drug-discovery/ai-designs-miniprotein-switches-for-gpcr-targeting/)
- **MRGPRX1 agonist mechanism (class A).** Agonist miniprotein **helices sit only partly inside the orthosteric pocket** ("partial protrusion from the orthosteric site"), overlap the native BAM 8-22 peptide site, and sterically occlude it; two agonists adopt "nearly opposite orientations"; side-chain differences "may relate to the observed partial and full agonism." No causal activation mechanism established — [bioRxiv 2025.03.23.644666](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.23.644666v1.full)
- **[pre, low tier] De novo RXFP1 agonists** (Lorne Proteins 2026 student poster): "strong nanomolar-range agonist activity for two designed binders" that "selectively stabilize RXFP1 active states" (RXFP1 = class A/LGR relaxin receptor) — [Lorne 2026 abstract](https://proteins-2026.p.asnevents.com.au/days/2026-02-09/abstract/132687)
- **[PR, non-GPCR comparator] G-CSFR de novo agonists** — de novo agonism of a cytokine receptor via affinity + dimerization-geometry tuning, with biased differentiation output — [Mol Ther 2025, PMC12628175](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12628175/)
- **Nabla Bio de novo VHH vs CXCR4/CXCR7** (2025) — picomolar–low-nM de novo antibodies to GPCRs, but **binding, not agonism** — [Twist listing](https://www.twistbioscience.com/node/53586)

### Inferences
- **EvoBind is the proof-of-concept that de novo class-B agonism is achievable** by (a) targeting the activation/orthosteric surface directly (not the ECD), (b) a compact peptide that threads into the pocket, (c) sequence-only blind design that automatically avoids any native-complex template. It even delivered **G-protein bias** for free. [inference]
- But **two features of the EvoBind designs are challenge-ineligible as-is**: topology (**head-to-tail cyclic**, not linear/disulfide) and tool license (**CC-BY-NC**, see Q5). The *strategy* transfers; the specific designs/tool do not. [HYP]
- Baker's results delimit the difficulty: class-A agonism (shallow orthosteric helix) is reachable; **class-B agonism via the ECD route is not** — which is why they chose ECD antagonism for GLP1R. Reaching the deep class-B 7TM pocket under the Trp33 cap is exactly the step EvoBind did and Baker avoided. [inference]

### Gaps
- No independent replication of the EvoBind GLP1R agonists; preprint, not peer-reviewed; β-arrestin/efficacy from n=2–3 replicates.
- Baker Nature full text (agonist supplementary data, exact receptor-list reconciliation NK1R/CCR5) not parsed (PDF >10 MB; paywalled HTML).

## Q5. Methods that design for INSERTION/THREADING into a deep pocket (RFpeptides, DiffPepBuilder, BoltzGen, EvoBind2) — depth/pose claims, license, uv-installability

### Takeaway
**None of these tools exposes an explicit "insertion depth/pose" control.** They control *which target residues the binder must contact* (hotspots/epitope/binding-site residue lists) and, in BoltzGen, secondary structure + covalent bonds — depth is an emergent consequence, not a dial. For eligibility: **RFdiffusion (BSD), DiffPepBuilder (MIT), BoltzGen (MIT code)** are permissive and support **disulfide**; **EvoBind2's protocol is CC-BY-NC (likely INELIGIBLE)**; **RFpeptides makes head-to-tail macrocycles (ineligible topology)**. Only EvoBind has *demonstrated* class-B pocket threading with activation.

### Cited Findings — per-tool (BINDING tools unless noted)
- **EvoBind / EvoBind2** (Qiuzhen Li, E.N. Vlachos, Patrick Bryant; Commun Chem 2025, 8:211, 10.1038/s42004-025-01601-3, 22 Jul 2025). **Blind** (no site/length needed; loss rewards peptide atoms close to *any* receptor atom), outputs **linear (default) AND cyclic** (`--cyclic_offset=1`); can **restrict target residues** ("Target residues within the receptor sequence", default=all) but **no pose/depth control**; tested 8–20 aa. **Depth/threading is implicit** (AF2-driven). AF2 model_1 + AF-Multimer v2.1.0, HHblits/UniClust30. **LICENSE: the EvoBind2 design protocol is CC-BY-NC-4.0** (README); underlying AF2 code Apache-2.0, AF2 params CC-BY-4.0. Install: `git clone` + `bash setup.sh` (conda) or Colab; **no pip/uv/Docker documented**. *The only method in this list with demonstrated class-B GPCR ACTIVATION (Q4).* — [Commun Chem (PMC12280060)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12280060/); [GitHub patrickbryant1/EvoBind](https://github.com/patrickbryant1/EvoBind); original EvoBind [bioRxiv 2024.06.20.599739](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.06.20.599739)
- **RFdiffusion** (RosettaCommons). **LICENSE: open-source BSD, "free for both non-profit and for-profit use"** (README; LICENSE file itself not quoted). Supports **partial diffusion** (`diffuser.partial_T`) and **binder design with hotspots** (`ppi.hotspot_res=[...]`). Linear binder design = **eligible topology**; depth is emergent from hotspot choice. ProteinMPNN (MIT) for sequences. Weights license not separately stated — [GitHub RosettaCommons/RFdiffusion](https://github.com/RosettaCommons/RFdiffusion)
- **RFpeptides** (Nat Chem Biol 2025, 10.1038/s41589-025-01929-w; IPD Nov 2024). Diffusion design of **macrocyclic (head-to-tail cyclic)** binders; ≤20 designs/target on 4 proteins, one <10 nM; X-ray-confirmed. Implemented inside RFdiffusion (`inference.cyclic=True`, `inference.cyc_chains`) — appears to inherit BSD but **UW licensed the tech to the spin-out Vilya** (Baker/Bhardwaj founders), so commercial terms may differ. **Topology = backbone-cyclic → NOT challenge-eligible (linear/disulfide only).** No explicit depth control — [IPD RFpeptides](https://www.ipd.uw.edu/2024/11/introducing-rfpeptides-ai-for-cyclic-peptide-design); [Rosetta Commons](https://rosettacommons.org/2025/06/26/introducing-deep-learning-designed-macrocycles-high-affinity-protein-binders)
- **BoltzGen** (Hannes Stark et al., MIT; bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.11.20.689494, Nov 2025). All-atom generator for proteins **and peptides**; nanomolar binders for 66% of 9 novel targets. **LICENSE: source code MIT**; **weights**: HF page tags "mit" but clarifies MIT covers *source code only* and "model checkpoints remain subject to their respective original licenses" (incl. bundled **Boltz-2** checkpoints) — **weight license must be verified**. Design-spec language controls: **binding site/epitope** (`binding:`/`not_binding:` residue lists), `structure_groups` visibility, **secondary_structure** of designed regions, **covalent bond constraints incl. SG–SG disulfides** (example "designed peptide with two Cys and a disulfide bond"; cyclotide example). **No explicit pose/insertion-depth parameter.** Install: **`pip install boltzgen`, Python ≥3.11, GPU** (uv not documented but pip ⇒ uv-feasible) — [GitHub HannesStark/boltzgen](https://github.com/HannesStark/boltzgen); [HF OneScience-Group/BoltzGen](https://huggingface.co/OneScience-Group/BoltzGen); [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.11.20.689494.full.pdf)
- **DiffPepBuilder** (Wang et al., J Chem Inf Model 2024, 64(24):9135-9149; arXiv:2405.00128). SE(3) diffusion co-designing peptide seq+structure; **target-specific** with **hotspot/motif JSON** (`B40, B58-60`, etc.); **adds DISULFIDE bonds** (`build_ss_bond=True`, `max_ss_bond=2`) → **eligible disulfide topology**; length **8–30 aa default**; **no explicit insertion-depth control**. **LICENSE: MIT.** Install: conda (`environment.yml`) + **PyRosetta** (separate pip wheel) for post-processing; GPU defaults (can set `use_gpu=False`); weights `diffpepbuilder_v1.pth` from Zenodo. **uv not documented; PyRosetta dependency has its own (non-MIT) license — provenance caveat** — [arXiv:2405.00128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00128); [GitHub YuzheWangPKU/DiffPepBuilder](https://github.com/YuzheWangPKU/DiffPepBuilder)

### Inferences
- **For an eligible insertion attempt into the GLP1R 7TM pocket, the permissive options are RFdiffusion, BoltzGen, or DiffPepBuilder**, steering "depth" indirectly by specifying **deep-pocket hotspot residues** (the TM6/TM3/TM7 orthosteric residues that native GLP-1's N-terminus contacts) as the required contacts, and enforcing a short helical/linear or disulfide-stapled message. BoltzGen's `binding:`/`not_binding:` + secondary-structure + disulfide spec is the most expressive eligible controller. [HYP]
- **EvoBind reproduces the exact capability the project needs (blind, template-free, orthosteric-surface, pocket-threading, class-B activation) but is license-blocked (CC-BY-NC) and topology-blocked (cyclic).** The highest-value eligible move is to **reimplement the EvoBind idea** — AF2/AF-Multimer-based in-silico hill-climb toward the activation surface — in a permissive wrapper, restricted to **linear or disulfide** topologies. AF2/AF-Multimer params (CC-BY-4.0) are provenance-clean. [HYP]
- "Depth" is not a first-class control in any current tool; the field controls *contacts*, and depth/efficacy emerge — consistent with EvoBind's own finding that structure/pIDDT fails to predict activation. Expect low efficacy hit-rates and plan oversampling + functional-proxy ranking. [inference]

## Q6. Nanobody/scFv + de novo (non-GLP-1) agonist-peptide fusion — is it an ELIGIBLE route?

### Takeaway
**Defensible but risky, and partly disallowed depending on the scaffold's provenance.** Fusing a *genuinely de novo* message peptide is **not "grafting"** in the banned sense (the ban names GLP-1/exendin/semaglutide specifically). The real eligibility hazards are: (1) a **natural/known nanobody framework or a known binder** = a "modified known binder," which conflicts with "de novo, zero-shot, not a modified known binder"; and (2) **mechanistic**: a nanobody that binds the ECD/extracellular face does not deliver a message into the 7TM pocket, and intracellular active-state binders (Nb80-type) can't be dosed extracellularly. A fully de novo-designed single chain (de novo VHH-like scaffold with de novo CDRs AND a de novo message, co-designed in one shot, ≤250 aa, disulfide-allowed) is the only version with a clean eligibility argument.

### Cited Findings
- **Ban is grafting-specific, not fusion-general.** Challenge rule prohibits grafting of **GLP-1/exendin/semaglutide** and using a **native agonist–receptor complex** as template/motif/backbone — it does not prohibit a two-element construct per se (project brief, ground truth).
- **De novo VHH scaffolds are feasible (RFantibody).** [PR] "Atomically accurate de novo design of antibodies with RFdiffusion" (Bennett et al.), bioRxiv 2024.03.14.585103 (Mar 2024) → Nature 2026, 649(8095):183-193 (Epub 5 Nov 2025, PubMed 41193805). Fine-tuned RFdiffusion builds **CDR backbones** → ProteinMPNN → structure validation; **validated for camelid VHHs/nanobodies**; initial designs tens–hundreds nM, matured (OrthoRep) to single-digit nM. Code RosettaCommons/RFantibody (**license not confirmed this session**) — [bioRxiv 2024.03.14.585103](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.03.14.585103); [GitHub RosettaCommons/RFantibody](https://github.com/RosettaCommons/RFantibody)
- **Nanobody-as-message-carrier doesn't by itself give GPCR agonism.** Active-state-selective nanobodies (Nb80/Nb6B9) bind the **intracellular** G-protein pocket → not extracellularly dosable, not agonists (Q1 citations). Nabla Bio's de novo VHHs to CXCR4/CXCR7 are **binders, not agonists** — [Twist listing](https://www.twistbioscience.com/node/53586)
- **Message–address requires the message to reach the orthosteric site** (Portoghese/Schwyzer, Q2) — an ECD-binding antibody arm holds the address but the message must still thread the 7TM pocket under Trp33, which antibody geometry does not naturally provide — [Portoghese (UMN)](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/bivalent-ligands-and-the-message-address-concept-in-the-design-of/)

### Inferences (eligibility reasoning — [HYP])
- **Clean-eligibility version:** one chain ≤250 aa, co-designed de novo (de novo scaffold + de novo CDRs/surface + de novo message, no GLP-1/exendin sequence, no native complex template), **disulfides allowed** → satisfies the letter of the rules. This is essentially "design a de novo two-domain binder," i.e., Q2's unsolved problem wearing an antibody-shaped scaffold.
- **Likely-INELIGIBLE versions:** (i) taking an existing/known nanobody (e.g., an anti-GLP1R or framework-humanized VHH) and fusing a peptide = "modified known binder"; (ii) using a known agonist peptide (GLP-1/exendin fragment) as the message = banned grafting. The de novo-ness must cover *both* modules.
- **Mechanistic verdict:** even if eligible, an antibody scaffold is a poor message-delivery vehicle for a deep class-B 7TM pocket; the EvoBind result shows a *compact peptide sitting in the pocket* is the mechanism that actually activated GLP1R — favor a compact de novo peptide/mini-domain over an Ig scaffold for the activation function. Reserve antibody/VHH scaffolds for the ECD *address/anchor* role only if a two-domain route is attempted.
- **Zero-shot caveat:** RFantibody's workflow normally expects affinity maturation; a *zero-shot* (≤20 designs, no selection) antibody-fusion agonist is a long shot versus zero-shot compact-peptide designs.

### Gaps
- Adaptyv's exact definition of "de novo" / "modified known binder" for antibody frameworks is a rules-interpretation question — **must be confirmed with the official Challenge 3 rules** before committing to any VHH-fusion route.
- No precedent found for a de novo nanobody+de novo-peptide fusion acting as a GPCR agonist (binding-only VHHs exist; agonism via this architecture is unproven).

## Bottom-line synthesis for the project (eligibility-filtered)
1. **Primary precedent to emulate (not copy):** EvoBind's blind, template-free, orthosteric-surface, pocket-threading strategy *is* the demonstrated path to de novo **class-B GLP1R activation** (cAMP, G-protein-biased) — but the **tool is CC-BY-NC (likely ineligible)** and its designs are **cyclic (ineligible)**. Reimplement the idea (AF2/AF-Multimer in-silico directed evolution toward the activation surface; AF2 params are CC-BY-4.0) constrained to **linear or disulfide** peptides. [HYP]
2. **Eligible permissive generators for pocket insertion:** RFdiffusion (BSD), BoltzGen (MIT code; verify weights), DiffPepBuilder (MIT; PyRosetta caveat) — steer "depth" via **deep-pocket hotspot residue specification**, enforce short helix/disulfide-staple; expect low efficacy hit-rate, oversample. [HYP]
3. **State-selectivity lever:** score candidates by **active-minus-inactive differential state fit** (AlloGen concept) as a *filter*, and design against an **active-state GLP1R structure as target** (allowed; distinct from using the native agonist complex as a motif/backbone). [HYP]
4. **Two-domain (address+message) and nanobody-fusion routes are novel/unproven and carry eligibility risk** (must be fully de novo on both modules; confirm rules). Favor a compact single-element pocket binder for the activation function. [HYP]
5. **Hard truth on validation:** in-silico structure/pIDDT does not predict GLP1R efficacy (EvoBind's own 15-mer bound-but-inactive result) — any in-silico "agonist" ranking is a weak proxy; diversify the ≤20 submissions. [inference]
