# Designing GPCR binders outpaces predicting their agonism

**Executive summary.** Across the four method axes surveyed — generative binder design and deep-pocket reachability, experimentally validated de novo successes, computational binding-versus-function discrimination, and enhanced-sampling-MD feasibility — the 2023–2026 literature converges on one asymmetry: **the field can now design, and sometimes physically reach into, GPCR pockets, but it cannot computationally tell a binder from an agonist, and it has produced exactly one wet-lab-confirmed de novo GPCR agonist, on a class-A receptor.** De novo binder generators report binding hit rates from <1% to 10–100% and have reached deep class-A orthosteric pockets by all-atom-conditioned motif/partial diffusion ([Muratspahić et al., Nature 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10656-8)), yet the same pipeline reached class-B receptors (GLP1R, GIPR, GCGR, CGRPR) only at the solvent-exposed extracellular-domain (ECD) surface and produced class-B **antagonists**, demonstrating de novo GPCR **agonism only for class-A MRGPRX1** ([bioRxiv 2025.03.23.644666](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.23.644666v1.full)). On the prediction side, every validated functional classifier is class-A-dominated and degrades out-of-family ([GPCRact, PMC12805254](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12805254/)), co-folders are biased toward the inactive state and predict peptide poses poorly ([Acta Pharmacol Sin 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41401-024-01429-y)), and the best-powered binder-oracle meta-analysis ranks **binding** (via AF3 ipSAE_min) with strong target dependence, never function ([Overath et al., bioRxiv 2025.08.14.670059](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.14.670059v1.full)). The one genuinely additive positive lever this sweep surfaces is that enhanced-sampling MD is affordable on ~4 GPUs (~2–5 µs and ~3–8 days per system) as a **necessary-condition active-state-compatibility filter with built-in controls — not a quantitative agonism ranker**. This confirms and sharpens the companion campaign reports' binding/activation firewall and extends it into a concrete, tool-level map. Throughout, experimentally demonstrated facts are kept explicitly separate from inference and from gaps, and agonist/antagonist are treated only as binding-mode categories.

## Generators design binders freely but reach class-B pockets only at the surface

**Takeaway: target-conditioned binder design is a mature, mostly permissively licensed toolset, but "reaching into a cavity" is a demonstrated capability only for class-A orthosteric pockets — the published class-B campaigns, GLP1R among them, bound the exposed ECD surface instead.**

The 2024–2026 state of the art for de novo mini-binder design is a small cluster of tools whose reported performance and licensing are summarized below. **Demonstrated:** backbone diffusion with downstream sequence recovery and AlphaFold2 filtering (RFdiffusion + ProteinMPNN + AF2) lifted success roughly two orders of magnitude over pre-AI campaigns ([Baker lab 2023](https://www.bakerlab.org/2023/05/08/deep-learning-improves-protein-binder-design-tenfold/)); AF2-hallucination (BindCraft) reports a **10–100% experimental hit rate** with nanomolar affinities even when the binding site is unknown ([bioRxiv 2024.09.30.615802](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.30.615802v1)); DeepMind's closed AlphaProteo reports **3–300× better affinities** and 88% of designs binding on one viral target ([arXiv 2409.08022](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2409.08022)); and the all-atom universal generator BoltzGen yielded nanomolar binders for **~66% of 9 novel targets** with ≤15 designs each ([bioRxiv 2025.11.20.689494](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.11.20.689494)). All-atom successors condition on non-protein atoms during denoising — RFdiffusionAA ([Science 2024, via ODesign](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22304)) and RFdiffusion3, which diffuses every polymer atom including side chains at ~10× the speed of its predecessor and is open-source ([bioRxiv 2025.09.18.676967](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.18.676967); [GEN](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/artificial-intelligence/rfdiffusion3-now-open-source-designs-dna-binders-and-advanced-enzymes)). For peptides specifically — the format class most relevant to threading a pocket — the field offers SE(3)-diffusion co-design with disulfide stapling (DiffPepBuilder, [arXiv 2405.00128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00128)), flow/latent-diffusion models benchmarked on PepHAR, and AF-guided in-silico evolution from sequence alone (EvoBind/EvoBind2, [bioRxiv 2022.07.23.501214](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.07.23.501214.full.pdf)).

**Inference:** these headline numbers are not comparable — BindCraft/AlphaProteo/BoltzGen figures are experimental binding rates on curated, mostly soluble targets, PepHAR's 14–28% are in-silico ipTM>0.6 pass rates, and none is an agonism rate or a demonstrated GPCR result. BoltzGen's collapse from ~66% to ~3% on the hard Nipah target is the sharpest warning that experimental rates are strongly target-dependent and fall on hard targets ([Tamarind](https://www.tamarind.bio/blog/boltzgen-validated-de-novo-binder-design-for-diverse-targets)).

On **deep-pocket reachability**, the single on-point demonstration is the Baker/Skape GPCR paper. **Demonstrated:** reaching a deep class-A orthosteric 7TM pocket required bespoke seeding — for CXCR4, ~1,000 "penetrating pentamer" motifs were generated and the **50 with the deepest insertion** scaffolded (with protruding kinked-helix/hairpin scaffolds from MetaGen and partial diffusion to refine a pocket-seeded pose), whereas the class-B receptors were addressed at the **soluble ECD surface** because steric hindrance blocked the deep core ([bioRxiv 2025.03.23.644666](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.23.644666v1.full)). RFdiffusion3 exposes explicit buried-atom pocket controls ([RFD3 docs](https://hpc.nih.gov/apps/rfd3.html)), and ODesign's flexible-target mode "consistently surpassed" its rigid mode and beat RFdiffusionAA 2.38× and BoltzDesign1 1,142× on ligand-pocket design ([arXiv 2510.22304](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22304v1)). The most useful quantitative signal is an implicit **"reachability tax":** within one consistent pipeline, moving from an exposed ECD surface to a deep class-A pocket dropped experimental yield 1–2 orders of magnitude — CXCR4 gave **2 hits from ~26,000 screened designs (both from one backbone)**, while the CGRPR ECD-surface campaign gave **36 of 78 expressed designs active** ([bioRxiv 2025.03.23.644666](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.23.644666v1.full)). Model fidelity is not the bottleneck: successful deep-pocket binders matched cryo-EM to ~0.7–1.0 Å Cα RMSD; the difficulty is yield ([bioRxiv 2025.03.23.644666](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.23.644666v1.full)).

This **confirms and sharpens** the companion report's conclusion that only compact peptide/mini-protein formats have a physical path into the 7TM pocket, and extends it with tool-level detail and the quantified reachability tax. **Gap:** there is no standardized "reachability" or "insertion-depth" benchmark; the depth-selection step is bespoke, not a released metric, and **no study demonstrates de novo insertion into a class-B deep 7TM orthosteric pocket** — every demonstrated deep insertion was class A. For a GLP1R campaign this is the load-bearing negative: reaching the class-B core de novo is beyond what any cited method has shown.

## De novo GPCR agonism is demonstrated once — on a class-A receptor

**Takeaway: the only wet-lab-confirmed de novo GPCR agonist is for the class-A receptor MRGPRX1; every de novo functional agonism success at other receptor families works by controlling dimerization/clustering geometry, a mechanism that does not transfer to single-chain GPCR orthosteric agonism.**

**Demonstrated:** the Baker/Skape *Nature* 2026 paper is the first to add bona fide GPCR functional modulation, validated in living cells by a high-throughput "receptor-diversion" microscopy screen and by five cryo-EM structures close to the design models ([PMID 42168559](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10656-8); [GEN](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/drug-discovery/ai-designs-miniprotein-switches-for-gpcr-targeting/)). The matching preprint names the targets precisely: **MRGPRX1 agonists, and CXCR4, GLP1R, GIPR, GCGR and CGRPR antagonists** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.03.23.644666)). The other validated functional-agonism cases sit outside GPCR orthosteric pharmacology: de novo insulin-receptor (an RTK) agonists built by fusing domain-binders in varied orientation/flexibility to lock an active conformation, functionally validated down to in-vivo glucose lowering ([Mol Cell 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2025.09.020); [bioRxiv 2024.10.07.617068](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.07.617068v1)); and the de novo IL-4 mimetic Neo-4, which signals through the cytokine-receptor complex ([Nat Chem Biol 2023](https://www.bakerlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/s41589-023-01313-6.pdf)). A separate widely-cited 2024 paper designs binders to peptide hormone *ligands* (PTH, glucagon) — not receptors — and is binding/biosensing only ([PMID 38109936](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38109936/)). A 2026 RXFP1 (class-A GPCR) conference poster reports RFdiffusion/BindCraft binders with preliminary BLI/functional data but is not peer-reviewed ([Lorne 2026 abstract](https://proteins-2026.p.asnevents.com.au/days/2026-02-09/abstract/132687)).

On **workflow and hit rates for hard, conformation-specific targets**: deep-learning triage (AF2/RoseTTAFold scoring both monomer self-consistency and target binding) raised success ~10× but kept absolute rates low — often <1%, with **zero binders for some targets** ([Baker 2023](https://www.bakerlab.org/2023/05/08/deep-learning-improves-protein-binder-design-tenfold/)) — while AlphaProteo reported 9–88% across seven targets yet **failed entirely on a deliberately chosen hardest eighth target** ([arXiv 2409.08022](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2409.08022)). The de-risking move for conformation-specific targets is a target-appropriate functional screen plus mandatory cryo-EM confirmation that the designed conformation was actually induced, not merely that a binder was found ([GEN](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/drug-discovery/ai-designs-miniprotein-switches-for-gpcr-targeting/)). Finally, documented failure modes argue for heavy oversampling: a 2026 framing states de novo binder design is "increasingly limited by experimental false positives" with substantial verifier-dependent bias among structure-prediction filters ([ProtDBench, ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/62951)), and commentary reports two CROs disagreeing on the same target+sequence ([Tamarind](https://www.tamarind.bio/blog/how-validated-are-de-novo-binders-really)).

**Inference:** the recurring de-risked route to de novo *agonism* is geometric control of receptors that signal by ligand-induced dimerization/clustering (RTKs, cytokine receptors) — mechanistically distinct from GPCR orthosteric agonism, where a single chain must drive an intramolecular conformational switch. This **confirms** the companion report's rejection of the clustering/valency playbook for GLP1R and supplies the methods-level reason it is excluded. That the pioneering group pursued GLP1R/GIPR/GCGR as antagonists while achieving agonism only on class-A MRGPRX1 is the strongest available signal that class-B agonism remained out of reach even for them. **Gap:** per-target functional potencies (EC50/Emax) and designs-ordered-versus-validated counts for the GPCR paper are not in retrievable abstracts, no de novo GPCR positive allosteric modulator was found, and no published post-mortem of a failed GPCR-agonist campaign exists.

## No validated computational method separates agonism from binding

**Takeaway: functional-efficacy prediction trails binding prediction everywhere and collapses for class B — the only quantitatively validated functional classifiers are class-A-dominated, co-folders are inactive-biased and predict peptides poorly, and the best binder oracle ranks binding, not function.**

**Demonstrated:** four method families exist — multi-state structural templates, ensemble-docking, MD interface/contact metrics, and ML classifiers trained on EC50/IC50 — but only the ML classifiers carry ligand-scale quantitative validation. The best, GPCRact, reaches **0.819 three-class (nonbinder/agonist/antagonist) balanced accuracy** but is ~93% class-A data, degrades out-of-distribution (leave-one-receptor-out BAcc 0.63–0.77, leave-one-family-out lower), and — tellingly — reports **binding BAcc 0.890 above three-class BAcc 0.819**, with activity predictions "near-random when binding is misclassified," making the functional call strictly downstream of getting binding right ([PMC12805254](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12805254/)). The other dedicated efficacy methods are explicitly class-A-only (Dynamic-GLEP: 5-HT1A AUC 0.74, A2A >0.85, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbag049)); AiGPro is a sequence+ligand bioactivity regressor (Pearson 0.91) conditioned on assay mode rather than a structure-mechanism model ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13321-024-00945-7)).

The structure-prediction obstacles compound for a class-B peptide target. Default AlphaFold2 produces predominantly one GPCR state with an **inactive-conformation preference**, attributed to a training imbalance of **178 inactive versus 60 active** structures ([Meiler lab, bioRxiv 2021.11.26.470086](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.11.26.470086)); AF3 predicts ligand poses for ions, peptides and proteins poorly, which the authors say "constrain the utility … in functional studies" — directly relevant because class-B endogenous agonists are peptides ([Acta Pharmacol Sin 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41401-024-01429-y)); docking to AF2 models is no more accurate than to homology models and much worse than to experimental structures ([Karelina & Dror, eLife 2023](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.89386)); and co-folding confidence that ranks actives versus decoys well on standard sets **declines specifically on a novel GPCR dataset under-represented in training** ([Chem Sci 2025](https://doi.org/10.1039/d5sc06481c)). For binder ranking specifically, the largest meta-analysis (3,766 de novo binders, 15 targets, 11.6% confirmed) found **AF3 ipSAE_min the single best metric** (1.4× average precision over the AF2-initial-guess interface-PAE that RFdiffusion pipelines commonly filter on), but with **per-target average precision swinging from ~0.1 to ~1** and no learned model beating a simple composite — and it did **not** evaluate Boltz-2 or Chai-1 ([Overath et al.](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.14.670059v1.full)). Independent calibration shows ipTM is a noisy proxy even for binding (EGFR binding-classification AUROC 0.64, [AalphaBio](https://aalphabio.substack.com/p/overconfident-what-structure-prediction)).

**Inference:** because agonists and antagonists share the orthosteric site and default co-folders lean inactive, a single-conformation confidence score reflects binding plausibility in a non-signaling-leaning geometry, not efficacy — no located source shows interface PAE / ipTM / co-fold confidence tracking measured agonist efficacy. The more reliable methods-level signal is a set of **necessary-but-not-sufficient structural filters** (sufficient insertion depth, engagement with activation-critical orthosteric contacts, and compatibility of the pose with the active rather than inactive state), which function as go/no-go gates that a surface/ECD-only pose fails ([Weber et al., Commun Chem 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42004-026-01936-5); [Mansoor & Morra, JCIM 2025](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.4c02101)); the specific activation contacts are receptor-mechanism content and out of scope for this methods report. This **confirms and extends** the companion reports: the earlier round empirically showed a two-state binding-free-energy differential ranks binding (the orthosteric antagonist scored highest) and that normal-mode/ENM models miss the localized activation motion; this sweep adds the independent ML-classifier and co-folder evidence that the same firewall holds, and recasts the surviving in-silico role as a *necessary-condition filter* rather than an efficacy ranker. **Gap:** no public benchmark demonstrates an in-silico efficacy predictor ranking a class-B antagonist versus agonist, GPCRact's class-B1 numbers are supplementary-only, and the necessary-condition filters have never been prospectively validated as a screening gate on predicted (as opposed to experimental) de novo GPCR structures.

## Enhanced-sampling MD is a 4-GPU necessary-condition filter, not an agonism ranker

**Takeaway: biased MD can qualitatively classify whether a bound ligand permits or destabilizes the active state, is affordable on ~4 GPUs, and is reliable only as an ordinal compatibility filter run against known controls — not as a quantitative ΔΔG agonism ranker.**

**Demonstrated:** a stable toolkit — well-tempered / multiple-walker / path-CV metadynamics, string-with-swarms, adaptive-sampling + Markov State Models — probes the GPCR activation landscape along validated collective variables such as the TM3–TM6 distance and NPxxY RMSD ([Tong et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/cbdd.70181); [Meral et al. 2018, PMC6291190](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6291190); [Fleetwood et al.](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/627026v1.full-text)). The cost anchor is strikingly consistent across three independent CV-based methods: **~2–5 µs of aggregate biased MD per activation landscape** (path-CV metadynamics 3 µs; string-with-swarms 2–3 µs; binding metadynamics 3–5 µs, [UCL protocol](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1559344/1/Protocol_JCIM_final.pdf)), whereas unbiased adaptive-sampling+MSM is **~240 µs to ~2 ms** aggregate and cloud/supercomputer-scale ([Kohlhoff et al. 2014](https://research.google/pubs/cloud-based-simulations-on-google-exacycle-reveal-ligand-modulation-of-gpcr-activation-pathways/)). Reliability evidence cuts both ways: metadynamics/MSM and multiple-walker metadynamics **can separate ligand classes** — inverse agonists collapse to a single inactive minimum while agonists shift population toward the active well ([Calderón Reyes et al., JCIM 2024](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.3c01763); [Roessner et al., JCIM 2025](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.5c00202)) — but convergence assessment for combined biased simulations is "still an open problem" ([ChemRxiv 2024](https://client.prod.orp.cambridge.org/engage/chemrxiv/article-details/6625a37791aefa6ce10d4b04)), only well-tempered (not standard) metadynamics converges to the exact free energy ([Barducci et al., PRL 2008](https://archive.aps.org/mar/2008/y13/2)), the string method converges only to the path nearest its initial guess ([eLife 60715 reviews](https://elifesciences.org/articles/60715/peer-reviews)), pulling-work estimators are noise-prone at small sample size ([enhanced-sampling review, arXiv 2202.04164](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.04164)), and a wrong Asp2.50 protonation choice can flip the qualitative answer ([Fleetwood et al.](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/627026v1.full-text)). A reassuring cross-check: 3 µs of path-CV metadynamics reproduced a ~240 µs adaptive-sampling landscape for the µ-opioid receptor except in the high-energy intermediate ([Meral et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6291190)).

**Inference (compute budgeting, explicitly derived, not quoted):** at the measured GROMACS membrane-protein throughput of **~241–266 ns/day per A100** for an ~80k-atom system ([FAU HPC](https://hpc.fau.de/2022/02/10/gromacs-performance-on-different-gpu-types/)), running 4 GPUs as 4 concurrent walkers gives ~0.6–1.2 µs/day aggregate, so a 2–5 µs landscape is **~3–8 days of wall-clock per system** and a minimum control+test panel (apo + known agonist + known inverse agonist + one test ligand) is **~2–4 weeks**. The defensible use is a **binary/ordinal active-state-compatibility filter** — "does the bound ligand permit or destabilize the active well?" — run identically for the test ligand and known-agonist/known-inverse-agonist controls, with well-tempered metadynamics along a validated CV, correct protonation, and demonstrated convergence; the controls double as a fail-loud calibration gate, since if the known agonist does not separate from the known inverse agonist, the protocol is untrustworthy. The practical stack is GROMACS+PLUMED with CHARMM-GUI membrane setup and CHARMM36m/TIP3P/POPC ([M2 workflow, arXiv 2006.11066](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.11066)), and the highest-leverage reliability investment is **setup, not sampling**. This **extends** the companion reports: the first mentioned GaMD/metadynamics only in passing, and the second recorded a normal-mode/ENM NO-GO because activation is a localized rather than a global soft mode — enhanced-sampling MD is the explicit-dynamics method that *can* read that localized change, but only as an ordinal compatibility call. **Gap:** no source reports run-to-run reproducibility statistics (replica spread/CI) for a GPCR activation free-energy difference used to rank ligands — the central missing number for a ranking application — and no turnkey "4-GPU, N-day, this-many-replicas" recipe exists; the wall-clock figures are inferred, not quoted.

## Implications for the GLP1R campaign

At the methods and feasibility level, the four axes compose into a single decision: **binding is designable and triageable, functional efficacy is neither, so the campaign should be structured as diversity-spread binding bets filtered for active-state compatibility, with the functional assay as the sole arbiter.** Concretely, tool selection should favor the compact-format generators — peptide diffusion models (DiffPepBuilder, PepFlow), AF-guided sequence-only evolution (EvoBind-style), RFpeptides macrocycles, and all-atom conditioning (RFdiffusion3, ODesign flexible mode) — because all-atom target conditioning plus motif/partial diffusion is the only demonstrated route that reaches into a pocket, and because bulky immunoglobulin formats are steered to surface binding, which at class B has produced the antagonist category. A runnable, ODC-BY-clean pipeline is well supplied by the permissive set (MIT: ProteinMPNN/BindCraft/Boltz/BoltzGen; BSD: RFdiffusion family), whereas AlphaProteo (closed), Chroma (gated weights), and EvoBind (no LICENSE file; CC-BY-NC protocol) carry provenance constraints for submitted designs.

Feasibility expectations should be calibrated to two demonstrated facts and one gap. The reachability tax is real and steep — moving from an exposed surface to a deep pocket cost 1–2 orders of magnitude in experimental yield even in the hands that pioneered the method — and **no cited method has demonstrated de novo insertion into a class-B deep 7TM pocket**, so heavy oversampling toward a confident class-B agonist is not available within a ≤20-design budget; diversity across distinct lineages is the rational allocation, not depth in one. The in-silico limits are firm: co-folds plus AF3/Boltz ipSAE_min are usable only as a **binding-plausibility triage calibrated per-target against positive and scramble controls**, never as an agonism score, and the agonist-versus-antagonist distinction can be represented in silico only as a binding-mode category (active-state-compatible pose versus surface/inactive-leaning pose), with even the best necessary-condition filters unvalidated prospectively. The one additive positive lever this sweep contributes is that **enhanced-sampling MD is affordable on the project's hardware as a necessary-condition active-state-compatibility filter** (~3–8 days per system on 4 GPUs), provided it is run as a controlled, well-tempered, convergence-checked protocol whose known-agonist/known-inverse-agonist controls form a fail-loud gate before any test ligand is interpreted — an ordinal go/no-go, not an efficacy ranking.

The net effect is to confirm the companion reports' firewall with independent, tool-level evidence and to sharpen its operational consequences rather than overturn them. Nothing in the 2023–2026 methods literature provides an in-silico score that proves agonism for a class-B GPCR; what it provides is a mature way to make and rank credible binders, a quantified expectation that reaching the class-B core de novo is unprecedented, and a cheap-enough explicit-dynamics filter to down-select which credible binders are at least compatible with the active state before committing them to the functional assay that alone can settle the question.
