# Cheap (CPU-only) elastic-network / normal-mode / network methods to score ligand–activation coupling (TM6 swing) as an agonism/efficacy ranking proxy for GLP1R

**Scope note / tiering key:** [PR] = peer-reviewed; [pre] = preprint; [HYP] = inference/hypothesis by this researcher (not directly sourced); [in-silico] = computational validation only (no wet-lab efficacy confirmation of the *method's* ranking). Throughout, BINDING (does it dock/stay) is kept distinct from ACTIVATION (does the bound ligand mechanically/thermodynamically drive the inactive→active transition). All elastic-network/NMA/PRS/network methods below measure *mechanical coupling or dynamics perturbation*, which is an activation **proxy**, not a measured efficacy.

---

## Q1. Do ENM/NMA low-frequency modes of a GPCR reproduce the TM6 activation transition?

### Takeaway
Yes, partially and receptor-dependently: elastic-network low-frequency modes of class A GPCRs recover the extracellular-vestibule-contraction / intracellular-pocket-opening collective motion that accompanies activation, and ENM/NMA+network analysis cleanly separates inactive vs active states — but **no single low mode reproduces the full inactive→active (TM6) path**, rhodopsin is an outlier, and there is **no published ENM/NMA activation study for GLP1R or any class B receptor** (only MD energy-flow work exists).

### Cited Findings
- Elastic-network normal modes computed for M2/M3 muscarinic, A2A adenosine, β2-adrenergic and CXCR4 receptors show the extracellular vestibule **dilating** (ligand passage) and **contracting** (activation), and the extracellular contraction **correlates with cavity formation of the G-protein binding pocket on the intracellular side**, i.e. the collective mode couples the two ends of the 7TM bundle — Kolan, Fonar & Samson 2014, *Proteins* 82(4):579–586 [PR] [in-silico] ([DOI 10.1002/prot.24426](https://doi.org/10.1002/prot.24426); summary via [cris.biu.ac.il](https://cris.biu.ac.il/en/publications/elastic-network-normal-mode-dynamics-reveal-the-gpcr-activation-m-4/)).
- In the same study, **rhodopsin's normal modes did not correlate well with the motion of the other GPCR family members** — a receptor-specific failure of mode transferability — Kolan et al. 2014 [PR] ([DOI 10.1002/prot.24426](https://doi.org/10.1002/prot.24426)).
- Protein-structure-network (PSN) analysis built on ENM-NMA of crystallographic **inactive (dark) vs active (meta-II) rhodopsin** shows clear, interpretable changes in the residue-interaction network between the two states (and the centrality of the retinal) — i.e. ENM+network *discriminates* activation states — Fanelli, Felline & Raimondi 2013, *Methods Cell Biol* 117:43–61 [PR] ([DOI 10.1016/B978-0-12-408143-7.00003-7](https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-408143-7.00003-7)).
- A rhodopsin-focused analysis reported (per search summary) that **none of the normal modes calculated from the inactive structure has a dominant contribution along the inactive→active rearrangement path**, and that TM3/TM5 (not only TM6) participate — i.e. the activation vector is spread over several modes, not one soft mode — search summary of rhodopsin ENM work ([WebSearch summary](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2016/ra/c6ra18243g)) [PR, secondary summary — confirm in primary].
- ENM/NMA of monomeric vs dimeric/tetrameric rhodopsin: oligomerization perturbs modes mainly at interfaces, and the TM4,5–TM4,5 activation rearrangement can be rationalized by ENM modes — Niv & Filizola 2008, *Proteins* 71(2):575–586 [PR] ([DOI 10.1002/prot.21787](https://doi.org/10.1002/prot.21787)).
- A class A GPCR homodimer ENM + PSN study (CXCR4, κ-OR, β1AR) found low-frequency modes share a common pattern including **asymmetric TM6 and ECL2 motion** near the dimer interface, linked to activation asymmetry — 2016 *RSC Adv.* study [PR] [in-silico] ([pubs.rsc.org c6ra18243g](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2016/ra/c6ra18243g)).
- **GLP1R / class B specifically:** no ENM/NMA activation-overlap study found. The only coupled-dynamics work is MD-based energy-flow: Poudel & Leitner 2022 computed energy-exchange networks through GLP-1R in inactive and two active states (water near the central polar network is slower in active states), and a 2025 study examined vibrational energy transport in GLP-1R comparing all-atom and **coarse-grained** models — both MD/energy-flow, not ENM activation-overlap — ([WebSearch summary](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2025/cp/d5cp02918j)) [PR].

### Inferences
- [HYP] The class A result (extracellular contraction ↔ intracellular G-protein pocket opening in the soft modes) is the mechanistic basis for using ANM mode-overlap as an activation proxy: if a candidate's binding-site perturbation projects onto those same collective modes, it is a priori more likely to drive the TM6/G-protein-pocket opening.
- [HYP] Because the full activation vector is spread over several low modes (not one), the correct readout is **cumulative overlap over the first k modes** (or PRS-based projection), not single-mode overlap.
- [HYP] The absence of any class B / GLP1R ENM activation study means every number below is transferred from class A; GLP1R's large extracellular domain (ECD) + peptide-in-groove mechanism may change mode structure, so the proxy must be calibrated on GLP1R's own active(6X18)-minus-inactive pair before trust.

### Gaps
- No ENM/NMA paper quantifies the **cumulative-overlap value** (how many low modes, what overlap) for a GPCR inactive→active transition specifically; I could not retrieve a single clean "mode N gives overlap X with the activation deformation" GPCR number. (Generic ProDy p38 example: top-3 mode pairs reach cumulative overlap 0.73 — not a GPCR.)
- No class B / GLP1R validation of ENM activation modes exists.

## Q2. Has ENM/NMA/PRS/network been used to RANK ligand efficacy or separate agonists from antagonists? How well, and false-positive risk?

### Takeaway
Yes — the strongest, most directly relevant result is a 2024 μ-opioid-receptor study that **predicts ligand efficacy from coarse-grained NMA of the ligand–receptor complex at ~3 CPU-seconds per complex**, and crucially shows that **receptor-dynamics features generalize to novel chemistry where a binding-contact-only model fails** (the key binding-vs-activation separation). A separate 2025 deep-learning framework (GPCRact) classifies agonist/antagonist/nonbinder at 0.82 balanced accuracy using an explicit allosteric-site (apo→holo displacement) representation. Both are [in-silico] validated against experimental efficacy labels; neither is ENM-overlap per se, but they establish that *dynamics/allosteric coupling carries efficacy signal*.

### Cited Findings
- **MOR efficacy prediction via coarse-grained NMA (most relevant):** Galdino, Mailhot & Najmanovich 2024 trained a LASSO model on **entropic signatures** (ENCoM coarse-grained normal-mode fluctuation features) of **179 MOR ligand–receptor complexes** with experimentally measured efficacies, split into *strictly chemically different* cross-validation sets. Key results: (i) the dynamics model predicts efficacy and recovers known activation residues; (ii) a **contact-only model (ligand–protein interactions, i.e. a binding proxy) had predictive power but FAILED for ligands with low structural similarity to the training set**, while the dynamics model generalized — "emphasizing the importance of receptor dynamics for predicting ligand-induced receptor activation"; (iii) cost **≈3 CPU-seconds per ligand–receptor complex**, explicitly positioned for large virtual screens — Galdino et al. 2024, *J Chem Inf Model* 64(22):8549–8561 [PR] [in-silico] ([DOI 10.1021/acs.jcim.4c00788](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.4c00788)).
- The DynaSig-ML/QDAR methodology this uses (dynamical signatures → LASSO) reached **R² = 0.66 (LASSO; 0.69 for gradient-boosting/random-forest)** on its benchmark (miR-125a maturation, 29,000+ variants), establishing the dynamics-signature→ML regression as a working, interpretable pipeline — Mailhot et al., DynaSig-ML, *Bioinformatics* 2023 [PR] ([PMC10130421](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10130421/); preprint [DOI 10.1101/2022.07.06.499058](https://doi.org/10.1101/2022.07.06.499058)).
- **GPCRact (agonist/antagonist/nonbinder classification via allosteric communication):** a two-stage E(n)-equivariant GNN + attention model. Inputs = a subgraph of the **consensus orthosteric site** (residues within 4.0 Å of a bound ligand) + a **consensus allosteric site defined as the top-100 residues by apo→holo Cα displacement** (i.e. the activation-displacement set), plus a single ETKDG ligand conformer from SMILES (no docking/bound pose required). Three-class balanced accuracy **0.819** (scaffold split); class recalls antagonist 0.873 / agonist 0.829 / nonbinder 0.757; on "allosterically complex" receptors (>200 allosteric-site residues) it held at 0.801 vs larger drops for baselines. Attention concentrates on TM3 (DRY), **TM6 (CWxP)**, TM7 (NPxxY); an R3.50 mutation lowered agonist probability while barely changing binding probability (binding-vs-activation separation). **Leave-one-family-out accuracy dropped markedly (distribution shift)** — GPCRact 2025 [PR] [in-silico], code [github.com/hyojin0912/HJ-GPCRact](https://github.com/hyojin0912/HJ-GPCRact) ([PMC12805254](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12805254/)).
- **PRS as a general allostery-coupling method:** Perturbation-Response Scanning couples an ENM with linear-response theory; applied to 25 proteins with known conformational changes it identifies single residues whose perturbation drives the functional conformational change — Atilgan & Atilgan 2010, *Biophys J* [PR] [in-silico] ([PMC2913187](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2913187)). On PDZ domains, PRS ranks residues by mean-square-fluctuation response to binding-site perturbation and builds allosteric pathways from same-direction responders — General, …, Atilgan 2011, *PLoS Comput Biol* [PR] ([PMC3188487](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3188487/)).

### Inferences
- [HYP] The MOR result is the template for GLP1R: build each candidate **peptide–GLP1R co-fold complex**, compute its ENCoM entropic/dynamical signature, and (a) if a labelled efficacy set exists, train QDAR/DynaSig-ML; (b) with no labels, rank candidates by how far each shifts the receptor's dynamical signature **toward the active-state (6X18) signature**. The explicit MOR finding that a binding-contact-only model fails on novel chemistry is the core argument for adding this orthogonal signal on top of a co-fold/docking score for *de novo* peptides.
- [HYP] GPCRact's "consensus allosteric site = top-100 apo→holo Cα displacement residues" is operationally the **activation difference vector** restricted to residues — the same object the ProDy overlap/PRS recipe (Q4) uses, independently corroborating that the right target is the active-minus-inactive displacement set.
- **False-positive risk (explicitly):** [HYP, grounded in cited results] (1) These proxies measure *coupling/geometry*, not sign of efficacy — a ligand lying on the allosteric path can still be an **antagonist/inverse agonist** that stabilizes the inactive state; a magnitude-only overlap will flag it as a false positive unless the **signed** direction toward active is enforced. (2) GPCRact's leave-one-family-out drop and the MOR contact-model failure both warn that transfer to an *unseen* fold/family (class B, novel de-novo peptides) is the regime of highest FP risk. (3) Any method keyed to the bound pose inherits the co-fold's pose error as a false signal.

### Gaps
- I did not find an ENM/PRS method that reports a *ranked agonist-vs-antagonist AUC/accuracy for a single receptor using only the overlap/PRS readout* (as opposed to ML on dynamics features). The efficacy evidence is QDAR-ML (MOR) and GNN (GPCRact), both learned models, not bare mode-overlap.
- No efficacy-ranking validation exists for class B / peptide agonists / GLP1R.

## Q3. Concrete, installable, permissively-licensed CPU tools

### Takeaway
ProDy (MIT) is the primary, directly-installable ANM/GNM/PRS/overlap engine and already exposes the exact overlap-with-a-target-vector function needed; NRGTEN/ENCoM + DynaSig-ML is the sequence-sensitive CG-NMA + ML stack behind the MOR efficacy result (~3 CPU-s/complex); MD-TASK (GPL3) and correlationplus (LGPLv3) add PRS + residue-network analysis; Ohm and DynOmics are servers for allosteric pathways. Several require an MD trajectory (not single-structure-cheap) — flagged below. Licenses I could not source first-hand are marked **[verify LICENSE]**.

### Cited Findings (per tool)

**ProDy** — [PR tool] license **MIT** ([license page](https://prody-official.readthedocs.io/en/latest/about/license.html); note older PyPI 1.2.1 listed GPLv3 — the current docs say MIT, [PyPI history](https://pypi.python.org/pypi/ProDy/1.2.1)). Install: `pip install prody` / conda. Computes: ANM, GNM, PCA; `calcModes`; `calcOverlap`, cumulative & subspace overlap (overlap measure of Marques & Sanejouand 1995); and a full **Perturbation-Response-Scanning module** ([perturb API](https://prody-official.readthedocs.io/en/latest/reference/dynamics/perturb.html)):
  - `calcPerturbResponse(model, **kwargs)` → PRS matrix + **effectiveness** and **sensitivity** profiles; `turbo=True` (default) gives the fast closed-form PRS (no explicit random forces); `return_vectors=True` returns directional responses as a `ModeEnsemble` (needs a 3D model = ANM/PCA); `force=` lets you apply a chosen force direction; `perturb_node=` restricts to chosen nodes.
  - `comparePerturbResponses(response_ensemble, target, stat='max')` → **overlap of the directional response against a target** (target can be an `NMA`, `Mode`, or a `Vector` = the active-minus-inactive difference vector), reduced per perturbed node by max/mean/sum. **This is the ready-made "does perturbing the peptide site drive the activation vector?" readout.**
  - `calcDynamicCouplingIndex(matrix, atoms, select, func_sel, norm=False)` → **DCI** (functional DFI; Kumar, Glembo & Ozkan 2015) between a residue set (`select` = peptide site) and functional residues (`func_sel` = TM6/Gs interface).
  - `calcDynamicFlexibilityIndex(matrix, atoms, select, norm=False)` → **DFI** (Gerek, Kumar & Ozkan 2013).
  - ProDy reference paper: Bakan, Meireles & Bahar 2011, *Bioinformatics* ([PMC3102222](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3102222)).
  - Runtime: [HYP] a ~400-residue 7TM Cα ANM is a ~1200×1200 Hessian; building + solving the first ~20 modes is **seconds to ~1 min on one CPU core**; `turbo` PRS from the covariance is comparably fast. (Not separately sourced; standard ENM scaling.) Adding a 15–40-mer peptide (~15–40 extra Cα) is negligible.

**NRGTEN** (incl. **ENCoM**) — [PR tool] Python package implementing four NMA models including ENCoM, the *only sequence-sensitive coarse-grained NMA model*; repo [github.com/gregorpatof/nrgten_package](https://github.com/gregorpatof/nrgten_package), docs [nrgten.readthedocs.io](https://nrgten.readthedocs.io/); paper Mailhot & Najmanovich 2021, *Bioinformatics* ([DOI 10.1093/bioinformatics/btab189](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btab189)). License **[verify LICENSE on repo]**. Input: PDB structures (proteins, nucleic acids, small molecules, and **their complexes**). Computes dynamical/entropic signatures (per-position fluctuations) usable directly or as ML features.
**ENCoM** C implementation: [github.com/NRGlab/ENCoM](https://github.com/NRGlab/ENCoM) (forked from vince1589/encom), ~97% C, license **[verify LICENSE]**.

**DynaSig-ML** — [PR tool] Python; wraps NRGTEN/ENCoM dynamical signatures into scikit-learn (LASSO default; any sklearn backend); maps LASSO coefficients back onto the 3D structure for PyMOL. Repo [github.com/gregorpatof/dynasigml_package](https://github.com/gregorpatof/dynasigml_package), docs [dynasigml.readthedocs.io](https://dynasigml.readthedocs.io/). Deps: NRGTEN, scikit-learn, numpy, matplotlib. License **[verify LICENSE]**. This is the exact stack used for the MOR efficacy result (**≈3 CPU-s/complex**, [DOI 10.1021/acs.jcim.4c00788](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.4c00788)).

**MD-TASK** — [PR tool] license **GNU GPL 3.0** ([docs home](https://md-task.readthedocs.io/en/latest/home.html)). Authors incl. A.R. Atilgan & C. Atilgan (the PRS originators) — so its PRS is canonical. Three script categories: (1) residue-interaction-network analysis (betweenness-centrality, shortest-path "L"), (2) **Perturbation-Response Scanning** ([PRS page](https://md-task.readthedocs.io/en/latest/prs.html)), (3) dynamic cross-correlation. Primarily consumes MD trajectories, but PRS/network scripts can run on a single structure / ENM. Cite PMC5860072. Companion **MODE-TASK** = NMA/ENM/PCA; web server **MDM-TASK-web** ([bioRxiv 10.1101/2021.01.29.428734](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.01.29.428734)).

**correlationplus** — [PR tool] license **LGPL v3**; install via conda or pip ([repo tekpinar/correlationplus]; paper Tekpinar, Néron & Delarue 2021, *J Chem Inf Model*, [DOI 10.1021/acs.jcim.1c00742](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.1c00742)). Computes/visualizes/analyzes pairwise residue correlations **from MD trajectories OR coarse-grained normal-mode models** (so it works on an ENM, no trajectory required), and extracts key residues / shortest paths for allosteric communication.

**DynOmics ENM server (a.k.a. ENM 1.0 / oGNM-oANM)** — [PR tool, web server] Li, Chang, Lee, Bahar & Yang 2017, *Nucleic Acids Res* 45:W374–W380 ([PMC5793847](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5793847)), at [enm.pitt.edu](http://enm.pitt.edu) and [dyn.life.nthu.edu.tw/oENM/](https://dyn.life.nthu.edu.tw/oENM/). Integrates GNM + ANM **extended to account for the molecular environment — explicitly a lipid bilayer, crystal contacts, bound ligand, or neighboring subunits** (directly addresses the membrane-absent failure mode); finds functional sites, allosteric/signal-transduction pathways, and generates ANM conformer ensembles. Server, not a local pip install (no CPU-budget cost to you, but not offline/batchable).

**Ohm** — [PR tool, web server + code] Wang et al. (Dokholyan lab) 2020, *Nat Commun* 11 ([DOI 10.1038/s41467-020-17618-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-17618-2); [PMC7395124](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7395124/)), [ohm.dokhlab.org](https://ohm.dokhlab.org). **Starts from a single tertiary structure** (extracts contacts), runs repeated stochastic perturbation-propagation on the residue network to predict allosteric sites, **allosteric pathways**, critical pathway residues, and residue-pair allosteric correlations; validated on Caspase-1, CheY, and 20 allosterically-regulated proteins. License **[verify — check code availability section]**.

**AlloViz** — [PR tool] Python meta-package wrapping **8 tools: GetContacts, correlationplus, dynetan, PyInteraph2, pytraj, MD-TASK, MDEntropy, CARDS**; builds residue-interaction networks and **delta-networks** and analyzes with NetworkX/nglview; Nerín-Fonz et al. 2024, *Comput Struct Biotechnol J* ([DOI 10.1016/j.csbj.2024.04.047](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csbj.2024.04.047)); docs [alloviz.readthedocs.io](https://alloviz.readthedocs.io/). Install: clone `--recursive`, conda env (Python ≥3.10) then `pip install ./AlloViz`. **Requires a topology + MD trajectory** → NOT single-structure CPU-cheap. License **[verify LICENSE]**. Example system = β-arrestin-1.

**GetContacts** — [tool] Dror lab; computes residue–residue contact frequencies **from MD trajectories** (needs a trajectory) — not a single-structure ENM method; wrapped by AlloViz. License **[verify LICENSE on getcontacts repo]** (could not source).

**MDAnalysis** — [tool] trajectory/structure I/O + analysis library (common dependency for the above). License **[verify — commonly GPL]** (could not source first-hand; do not rely on it for a license-sensitive decision without checking).

**bio3d** — [tool] R package; provides `nma.pdb` (all-atom & ENM NMA), PCA, and ENM building ([CRAN bio3d nma.pdb](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/bio3d/html/nma.pdb.html)). License **[verify — commonly GPL-2]** (could not source first-hand). R, not pip; use only if you prefer R.

**WISP** — [tool] Not retrieved this session. WISP (weighted implementation of suboptimal paths, Durrant/McCammon) computes allosteric path ensembles, typically from a correlation/covariance matrix (ENM or MD). License/install **[not sourced — gap]**.

### Inferences
- [HYP] Minimal offline CPU stack that needs **no MD trajectory**: **ProDy** (ANM + `calcPerturbResponse`/`comparePerturbResponses` + DCI/DFI + overlap) as the workhorse, plus **NRGTEN/ENCoM (+DynaSig-ML)** for the sequence-sensitive dynamics-signature efficacy ranking. correlationplus can run on the ENM if you want an independent network view. Everything else (AlloViz, GetContacts, pytraj paths) wants a trajectory and is a poorer fit to the minutes-per-candidate budget.
- [HYP] Ohm (single structure) and DynOmics (adds the bilayer) are useful *one-off* server cross-checks of the allosteric path from the peptide site to the TM6/Gs face, but being servers they don't batch 20–200 candidates cheaply.

## Q4. The right readout + a concrete recipe to rank GLP1R candidates

### Takeaway
Rank each candidate by the **signed projection / overlap of the peptide-binding-site perturbation response onto the GLP1R active(6X18)-minus-inactive deformation vector**, implemented directly with ProDy PRS (`comparePerturbResponses` against that vector) and cross-checked by (a) DCI coupling from the peptide site to the TM6/Gs-interface residues and (b) an ENCoM dynamical-signature shift toward the active-state signature. Sign/direction toward active is mandatory to avoid flagging antagonists.

### Cited Findings
- ProDy's `comparePerturbResponses(response_ensemble, target, stat='max')` computes exactly the **overlap of the directional perturbation response against a target vector/mode**, per perturbed node — the activation-vector-overlap readout, out of the box ([perturb API](https://prody-official.readthedocs.io/en/latest/reference/dynamics/perturb.html)).
- ProDy provides `calcOverlap` + **cumulative overlap** to measure how well a subspace of low modes reconstructs a target deformation (the active-minus-inactive vector); the generic example reaches cumulative overlap 0.73 with the top-3 mode pairs for p38 ([WebSearch summary](https://prody-official.readthedocs.io/)). 
- PRS methodology ranks residues/sites by the fluctuation response to a localized perturbation and links **same-direction** responders into functional pathways — i.e. direction matters — General…Atilgan 2011 ([PMC3188487](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3188487/)); Atilgan & Atilgan 2010 ([PMC2913187](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2913187)).
- DCI (`calcDynamicCouplingIndex`, `select`→`func_sel`) quantifies dynamic coupling between a chosen site and a functional-residue set — the native way to score "peptide site → TM6/Gs interface" coupling ([perturb API](https://prody-official.readthedocs.io/en/latest/reference/dynamics/perturb.html)).
- Independent corroboration of the target object: GPCRact defines its allosteric site as the **top-100 residues by apo→holo Cα displacement** and keys efficacy on TM3/TM6/TM7 motif engagement ([PMC12805254](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12805254/)); the MOR efficacy model shows dynamics features (not contacts) carry the generalizable efficacy signal ([DOI 10.1021/acs.jcim.4c00788](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.4c00788)).

### Inferences — concrete recipe [HYP, assembled from the cited tools]
1. **Define the activation vector (once).** Superpose active GLP1R (6X18, the design template) onto an inactive GLP1R 7TM structure; take the Cα displacement vector Δ = (active − inactive). This is the "TM6-swing / Gs-pocket-opening" target. (Restrict to the 7TM core; ECD/peptide handled separately.)
2. **Sanity-check the modes (once).** Build an ANM of the *inactive* GLP1R 7TM in ProDy; compute `calcOverlap`/cumulative overlap of the first ~10–20 modes with Δ. If a handful of soft modes reconstructs Δ with decent cumulative overlap (class A precedent, Kolan 2014), the ENM is informative for GLP1R; **if not (possible class B / rhodopsin-type failure), downweight the overlap readout and lean on ENCoM-signature + DCI.**
3. **Per candidate (20–200×), build the co-folded peptide–GLP1R complex**, then on its Cα ANM run `calcPerturbResponse(..., return_vectors=True)` perturbing the **peptide-contact residues**, and score with `comparePerturbResponses(response_ensemble, target=Δ, stat='max'/'mean')`. **Use the signed overlap** (projection toward active positive, toward inactive negative) — rank candidates by the positive-toward-active score.
4. **Cross-check coupling with DCI:** `calcDynamicCouplingIndex(ANM, atoms, select="peptide-contact residues", func_sel="TM6 cytoplasmic end + R3.50/E6.30 + Gs-interface")`. High DCI = strong dynamic conduit from the peptide site to the activation lever.
5. **Dynamics-signature efficacy rank (orthogonal, sequence-sensitive):** compute each complex's ENCoM (NRGTEN) entropic/dynamical signature (~3 CPU-s each); rank by similarity-shift of the receptor signature toward the **active-state (6X18) signature**, or, if any labelled GLP1R efficacy data exist, train DynaSig-ML/QDAR (LASSO) as in the MOR study.
6. **Consensus rank** across (3) signed overlap, (4) DCI, (5) ENCoM-signature; optionally one-off path cross-check via Ohm (single structure) or DynOmics (with bilayer). Keep BINDING score (co-fold ipTM/iPAE) as a *separate* axis — these methods presume a plausible bound pose and add the ACTIVATION axis on top.

### Gaps
- No sourced GPCR-specific threshold for "good" overlap/DCI that separates agonists from antagonists; thresholds must be calibrated on GLP1R with known agonist (GLP-1/semaglutide-like) vs antagonist/negative controls before trusting the rank.

## Q5. Failure modes — when do ENM/PRS proxies mislead?

### Takeaway
The proxies mislead (1) when the membrane is absent and the bare Cα ENM invents non-physical soft modes, (2) when the activation vector is not captured by low modes for that particular receptor (rhodopsin-type / possibly class B), (3) when coarse-graining discards the sidechain chemistry that actually distinguishes agonist from antagonist, (4) when the bound pose is wrong, and (5) when a magnitude-only (unsigned) readout confuses an inactive-state-stabilizing antagonist for an agonist.

### Cited Findings
- **Receptor-specific mode failure:** rhodopsin's normal modes "do not correlate well with the motion of the other GPCR family members," and (per rhodopsin ENM summary) no single inactive-structure mode dominates the inactive→active path — Kolan et al. 2014 ([DOI 10.1002/prot.24426](https://doi.org/10.1002/prot.24426)); rhodopsin summary ([WebSearch](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2016/ra/c6ra18243g)). → a soft-mode-overlap proxy can be near-blind for the "wrong" receptor.
- **Membrane environment matters:** the DynOmics ENM server was built specifically to extend GNM/ANM "to take account of molecular environment" including a **lipid bilayer** — implying the plain (environment-free) ENM of a membrane protein is deficient and the bilayer changes the collective dynamics — Li et al. 2017 ([PMC5793847](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5793847)).
- **Coarse-graining / contacts-only insufficiency:** the MOR study's contact-only (geometry) model failed to predict efficacy for ligands dissimilar to the training set, and sequence/dynamics features were required — Galdino et al. 2024 ([DOI 10.1021/acs.jcim.4c00788](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.4c00788)). ENCoM's whole selling point is being the *only sequence-sensitive* CG-NMA model, implying standard Cα ENM (ProDy ANM/GNM) is sequence-blind and cannot by itself distinguish two peptides with the same backbone but different sidechains — NRGTEN ([DOI 10.1093/bioinformatics/btab189](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btab189)).
- **Pose dependence:** QDAR/DynaSig-ML and GPCRact both operate on the ligand–receptor *complex* / binding-site subgraph — the signal is only as good as the input pose (GPCRact uses a consensus site + single conformer and still shows family-transfer drop) — Galdino et al. 2024 ([DOI 10.1021/acs.jcim.4c00788](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.4c00788)); GPCRact ([PMC12805254](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12805254/)).
- **Distribution shift / novel family:** GPCRact's leave-one-family-out accuracy dropped markedly — a direct warning for transferring class-A-trained intuition to class B GLP1R and to *de novo* peptides — GPCRact ([PMC12805254](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12805254/)).

### Inferences
- [HYP] **Binding ≠ activation, and coupling ≠ efficacy sign.** An ENM/PRS overlap or DCI only says the peptide site is *mechanically connected* to the TM6 lever; a NAM/antagonist is also connected but pushes the wrong way. Mitigation: always use the **signed** overlap with the active-minus-inactive vector, and/or compare the perturbed response of the candidate against both an active and an inactive template (two-state scoring).
- [HYP] For GLP1R specifically the biggest risks are: (a) the peptide binds the ECD/extracellular groove far from TM6, so a short-range ENM perturbation may not propagate (use DCI/path betweenness to confirm a conduit exists, not just proximity); (b) no class B ENM validation exists, so the mode-overlap sanity check (recipe step 2) is mandatory before trusting the rank; (c) the 7TM must be modeled with (or the ENM corrected for) the membrane, else soft modes are artefactual — prefer DynOmics-style environment or at least restrain/validate against the known 6X18 activation vector.
- [HYP] Because ProDy's bare ANM is sequence-blind, two de-novo peptides that co-fold to near-identical backbones but differ in sidechain chemistry will get near-identical ANM/PRS scores — exactly the regime where ENCoM's sequence sensitivity (or the co-fold energy/contact terms) must break the tie.
