# Agonize GLP1R by threading its transmembrane pocket

**Bottom line:** A de novo agonist of human GLP1R (UniProt **P43220**, a **class B1 GPCR**) is **feasible but at or beyond the current frontier**, and the win condition — cell-surface **Gαs → cAMP → CRE-SEAP activation**, not binding — is the hardest part. The single governing fact is that **binding ≠ activation, and the gap is maximal for a class B receptor**: GLP1R activates by a two-domain mechanism in which an agonist's **N-terminal "message" must insert deep into the 7TM bundle to drive a sharp TM6 kink and an ~18 Å outward swing** that opens the Gαs site ([Zhang 2017, *Nature*](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22394)). **No standalone designed protein, antibody, or nanobody has ever agonized any class B GPCR**; the state-of-the-art de novo lab produced GLP1R **binders/antagonists down to 5.3 nM but zero agonists**, achieving de novo GPCR agonism only for a class A receptor ([Baker, *Nature* 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10656-8)). The **only** direct precedent for GLP1R agonism by a de novo molecule is a single preprint of **cyclic peptides (n=3, best EC50 32 nM)** ([EvoBind, bioRxiv 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.06.658268v1.full)). Because a bulky immunoglobulin fold cannot reach the deep pocket while a small peptide N-terminus can, the mechanism forces a **format decision: a compact de novo peptide/mini-protein that threads a non-native message into the orthosteric pocket is the only route with a path to activation; antibody/nanobody/scFv/Fab formats are steered toward ECD binding and therefore toward antagonism.** Agonism **cannot be reliably predicted in silico** — co-folders default to the inactive state and no validated activation oracle exists — so computation can at best rank "credible active-state binder," and **only the functional cAMP/CRE-SEAP assay can confirm an agonist**. The recommended submission is a diversity-spread portfolio of ≤20 sequence-novel pocket-threading peptides/mini-proteins, built and validated on the active-state template 6X18 with positive/negative controls, and explicitly expecting most candidates to be antagonists.

## How to read this report: evidence tiers and the binding/activation firewall

The brief **supplies** these facts, treated as ground truth: Track 3 accepts **≤20 de novo, zero-shot designs**; allowed formats are **protein (10–250 aa), nanobody, scFv, Fab**; evaluation is a **detergent-solubilized GLP1R(24–432) binding assay followed by a cell-surface Gαs→cAMP CRE-SEAP functional assay**; deadline **2026-10-25**. Everything else is graded: **[PR]** peer-reviewed, **[pre]** preprint, **[HYP]** hypothesis/inference, **[in-silico]** prediction only, and **[ASSAY-ONLY]** meaning a claim that only the functional cAMP/CRE-SEAP assay can settle. Throughout, I keep **BINDING** (affinity to the construct) rigorously separate from **ACTIVATION** (driving the Gαs conformational switch); conflating them is the dominant failure mode of this challenge. One reconciliation across the notes: an earlier note treated the wet-lab readout as unknown, but the live challenge page and the brief confirm a **functional** assay and that "a design must be an agonist, confirmed in a functional assay, to count" ([Challenge-3 page, fetched 2026-10-13](https://proteinbase.com/competitions/anthropic-adaptyv-2026/challenges/glp1r)) — so the functional requirement is settled, and the task is unambiguously about activation.

## Binding is trivial; activation requires an ~18 Å TM6 swing

GLP1R works by a **two-domain ("two-step") mechanism** that physically separates affinity from efficacy, and this is the key to everything downstream. The receptor's **extracellular domain (ECD, ~residues 24–139)** captures the C-terminal helix of the peptide — the "address," which supplies affinity — while the peptide's **N-terminus (His7…Phe12) inserts into the deep 7TM orthosteric pocket** — the "message," which triggers activation ([Zhang 2017, *Nature*](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22394); [de Graaf 2016, *Pharmacol Rev*](https://doi.org/10.1124/pr.115.011395)). The activation hallmark is a **sharp kink of TM6 at the conserved class-B PxxG motif** and an **~18 Å outward swing of the cytoplasmic end of TM6** (Cα of K346) with a smaller ~7 Å TM5 movement, opening the intracellular cavity for the Gαs α5 helix [PR] ([Zhang 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22394)). A **central polar network (R190/N240/Q394/H363/E364)** acts as the fulcrum coupling pocket occupancy to this transition and to signalling bias ([Wootten 2016, *Mol Pharmacol*](https://doi.org/10.1124/mol.115.101246)). The decisive consequence: **an ECD-only binder, or any molecule that occupies the site without driving TM6, is an antagonist** — proven directly by the anti-ECD Fab 3F52, a competitive antagonist that blocked peptide but not a small-molecule agonist ([Hennen 2016, *Sci Rep*](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep26236)), and by the fact that truncating the peptide N-terminus (exendin-4(9-39), GLP-1(9-36)) converts agonist to antagonist.

The **minimal activation pharmacophore** is well mapped and is what a de novo design must reproduce without copying the native sequence. On the peptide side, **His7's cationic imidazole is indispensable for activation** (its positive charge, ring, and positioning — not the free α-amino — are decisive) ([Adelhorst 1994 SAR](https://www.docketalarm.com/cases/PTAB/IPR2023-00722/Mylan_Pharmaceuticals_Inc/03-16-2023-Petitioner/Exhibit-1005-Adelhorst_1994); [Endocrine J 1997](https://doi.org/10.1507/endocrj.44.701)), with **His7, Gly10, Asp15 most critical for cAMP** while **Phe28 dominates ECD affinity** (F28A causes ~1300-fold affinity loss) ([Underwood 2010, *JBC*](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M109.033829)). On the receptor side, the message is read by **R299 (ECL2, contacts His7), K197/R190 (TM2), and L388/S392/Q394 (TM7)** ([Zhang 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22394)). Crucially, the pharmacophore can be radically shrunk while retaining full agonism: an **11-mer** (optimized N-terminal 9-mer + a biphenylalanine cap) matches the native 30-mer's potency ([Mapelli 2009, *J Med Chem*](https://doi.org/10.1021/jm900752a)), **cyclic/stapled N-terminal analogues** stay nanomolar and can bias toward Gs ([Hoang 2015, PMID 25839426](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25839426/); [PMC7366314](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7366314)), and the non-peptide **Boc5** reaches full agonism purely by inserting one arm into the pocket overlapping GLP-1 residues A8–D15 (PDB 7X8S/7X8R) ([PNAS 2022, PMC9171782](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9171782)). This means **the activating element is a compact pocket-filling "message," not a large interface** — favorable for a small threaded peptide, unfavorable for a bulky scaffold.

Two state-dependence facts set the design templates. First, the clean human active-state reference is **6X18 (2.1 Å, GLP-1(7-36)NH2 + Gs)** — essentially wild-type receptor (FLAG+3C tag, no BRIL/T4L, no thermostabilizing mutations), stabilized by dominant-negative Gαs + Nb35 rather than receptor mutations ([Zhang 2020, *Mol Cell*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2020.09.020)); the Baker campaign used exactly this (plus 6VCB, 7DUQ) as the GLP1R design target ([Baker 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10656-8)). Note the PDB corrections that matter for template choice: **6X1A = danuglipron (small molecule)**, **7KI0 = semaglutide (peptide)**, and **5VAI is a rabbit receptor** (mechanism only). Second, **Gs-biased agonism** (what the cAMP readout rewards) correlates structurally with binding high in the bundle, **not engaging ECL3**, and leaving an open extracellular cavity — the signature of the oral small molecules (orforglipron/6XOX, danuglipron, TT-OAD2), which are partial, G-protein-biased agonists ([Kawai 2020, *PNAS*](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2014879117)). A partial agonist that still kinks TM6 is sufficient to win a cAMP readout and tends to be cleaner on bias — a useful lowered bar discussed below.

## No designed protein has agonized any class B GPCR

This is the central feasibility finding and must be stated plainly to the team. **There is no precedent — peer-reviewed or preprint — for a standalone de novo or engineered protein/antibody/nanobody that directly agonizes a class B (secretin-family) GPCR, including GLP1R.** The strongest evidence is a **direct negative result**: the state-of-the-art de novo campaign designed GLP1R (and GIPR, GCGR, CGRPR, PTH1R) **only as ECD-binding antagonists** (GLP1R binders reaching **5.3 and 27 nM**; antagonist IC50 **39–61 nM**), and obtained de novo GPCR **agonism only for the class A receptor MRGPRX1** (7 agonists / 50 expressed ≈ 14%, two full at EC50 390 nM and 1 µM, from ~13,000 screened designs), explicitly noting agonists are "particularly difficult… [needing] conformational selectivity" [PR] ([Baker, *Nature* 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10656-8); [PMC12157396](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12157396/)). The one direct GLP1R agonism precedent is **EvoBind's cyclic peptides** — sequence-only (no structure) design yielding an 18-mer at **EC50 32 nM** (GLP1R) with "no similarity to known agonists" — but it is a **single preprint, n=3, AF2-only structural validation, macrocyclic, no SPR or in vivo** [pre] ([bioRxiv 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.06.658268v1.full)).

The precedent that *does* exist clarifies the mechanistic menu, and all of it is **class A or non-GPCR**. Agonist antibodies/nanobodies at GPCRs work by (a) **ECD reorientation** (TSHR autoantibody M22 — specific to glycoprotein-hormone receptors with a large leucine-rich ECD; **GLP1R's small α/β ECD does not work this way**) ([Faust 2022, *Nature*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-05173-3)); (b) **CDR3-loop insertion into the orthosteric pocket** (apelin JN241-9, where adding one Tyr to CDR3 converted antagonist to full agonist; MC4R pN162) — demonstrated only at **class A receptors with accessible pockets** ([Ma 2020, *Sci Adv*](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aax7379); [MC4R, *Nat Commun* 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-50827-7)); or (c) **tethering a weak peptide agonist** — the only route shown at class B, where the PTHR1 nanobody-PTH conjugate reached **EC50 0.075 nM vs 4.3 nM free peptide** but the authors state flatly that **"no VHHs that directly activate GPCRs have been identified" and the VHH alone did not activate** — the peptide supplies the agonism ([Cheloha 2020, *Nat Commun*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-15884-8)). Separately, the entire **de novo clustering/valency playbook** (IL-2 mimic Neo-2/15, FGFR oligomers, Notch, TLR3, Novokines, DR5) **does not transfer to GLP1R**, because GLP1R signals as a monomer via an *intramolecular* conformational switch, not by receptor dimerization ([Silva 2019, *Nature*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0830-7); [FGFR, *Cell* 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2024.05.025)). Only two mechanisms conceptually transfer: **orthosteric active-state stabilization** (MRGPRX1 template; Boc5; EvoBind) and **TM-surface "exoframe" allostery** (GEM ago-PAMs that even rescued a class A D1 loss-of-function mutant — but class-B-unproven) ([GEM, *Nature* 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09957-1)). Note that the validated chemical GLP1R ago-PAMs (BETP, "compound 2") act **covalently at intracellular Cys347** — a site **unreachable by an extracellular biologic**, so it is not a design target ([*Nat Chem Biol* 2016, PMC4865917](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4865917/)).

The decisive synthesis is a **format → mechanism mapping**: an antibody/nanobody/scFv/Fab CDR loop can reach the ECD or extracellular vestibule but **almost certainly cannot thread into the deep 7TM pocket under the Trp33 cap**, so in those formats you are steered toward ECD/ECL binding — exactly what Baker's data show yields **antagonists** [HYP, grounded in PR]. A **small peptide/mini-protein/macrocycle (~12–40 aa) is the only format that can physically present a pocket-inserting message**. This is the most important architecture constraint in the entire project.

## Generators can build a binder, but none can tell you it fires

The 2024–2026 de novo ecosystem is **mature for soluble binders and now for peptides/macrocycles, but essentially blind to agonism and barely demonstrated on GPCRs** [PR/pre]. On soluble targets, generators report high binder rates — BindCraft 10–100%, PXDesign 20–73%, BoltzGen nanomolar binders for **66% of 26 targets**, AlphaProteo 9–88% — but **every one of these is a *binding* rate on mostly soluble benchmarks, not an agonist rate, and none has a demonstrated GPCR result** ([BindCraft, *Nature* 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09429-6); [BoltzGen, bioRxiv 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.11.20.689494v1); [PXDesign](https://seed.bytedance.com/en/public_papers/pxdesign-fast-modular-and-accurate-de-novo-design-of-protein-binders)). The only families with any GPCR experimental validation are the **Baker motif-scaffolded RFdiffusion + MetaGen pipeline** (which gave class B antagonists only) and **EvoBind** (peptides). Peptide/micro-protein generators — **EvoBind/EvoBind2, RFpeptides, BoltzGen-peptide, DiffPepBuilder** — are the only family with a plausible physical path into the 7TM pocket, because a small N-terminus can insert where a 60–120 aa fold cannot ([RFpeptides, *Nat Chem Biol* 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41589-025-01929-w)). De novo antibody tools (**RFantibody ~0–2%, Germinal 4–22%, Chai-2 16–20%, JAM-2 ~39% developer**) can target a defined epitope but inherit the ECD→antagonist problem for agonism ([RFantibody, *Nature* 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09721-5); [Germinal, bioRxiv 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.19.677421.full.pdf)).

There is a sharp **eligibility red line** here. The forbidden archetype is **Wei et al.**, titled "de novo GLP-1RA" but actually a **semaglutide redesign using the semaglutide–GLP1R complex (7KI0) as template with 13 sites fixed and 0.68–0.81 sequence identity to semaglutide** — the "modify a known agonist" path that the challenge prohibits ([*Adv Sci* 2025, PMC12561408](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12561408/)). The defensible archetype is EvoBind's "no similarity to known agonists." **Do not use the GLP-1/exendin/semaglutide sequence or the native agonist–receptor complex as template, motif, or backbone.** This creates the genuine dilemma of the project: the activation pharmacophore is so specific that recreating it tends to converge on the native motif, yet eligibility and novelty both demand a sequence with ≤30% identity to the incretins — so a **learned/search-based rediscovery of an activating surface (EvoBind-style), not motif-grafting, is the clean route**.

On scoring, the honest verdict is that **agonism is not reliably predictable in silico, least of all for class B/GLP1R** [in-silico]. Co-folders (AF3, Boltz-2, Chai-1, RF3) **default to the inactive conformation even when an agonist is present** — AF2's active-state accuracy lags inactive (median TM-RMSD 1.86 Å vs 1.33 Å), and a 2026 benchmark found agonist-bound receptors "pulled toward inactive-like geometry" in a dataset that was **228/253 agonists** ([Heo & Feig](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.11.26.470086v1.full); [*Nature* npj 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44386-026-00066-1)). **No co-folder represents the membrane, lipid, or detergent.** Confidence metrics track neither state nor agonism (**protein pLDDT vs pose accuracy r ≈ +0.04**), and the best dedicated peptide-agonism predictor (DeorphaNN) reaches only **peptide-pLDDT mAP 0.32 / GNN 0.41–0.72**, with **pocket occupancy non-discriminative (99.8% of agonists vs 96.6% of non-agonists)** ([2026 state benchmark](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.06.723350v1.full); [DeorphaNN, *Mol Cell* 2026](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.19.644234v1.full)). The Baker effort used **no in-silico efficacy score at all** — it stabilized the active state with templates and measured agonism experimentally. The defensible [in-silico] stack is therefore **triage only**: co-fold the design + GLP1R on the active-state template 6X18 (optionally + mini-Gs) across AF3/Boltz-2/Protenix with many seeds; score with **ipSAE_min + interface pLDDT/PAE** (ipSAE beats ipTM for designed binders), treating thresholds as relative and **calibrating per-target against positive controls (GLP-1(7-36)NH2, exendin-4, orforglipron) and negative scramble controls**; then test active-state stability with explicit-state **GaMD/metadynamics** reading out TM6-outward, the PxxG kink, and the central polar network ([ipSAE, Dunbrack 2025](https://pypi.org/project/foldmetrics/); [GaMD, *PNAS* 2016](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.1614538113)). **These gate "credible active-state binder," never "agonist." [ASSAY-ONLY] Only the cAMP/CRE-SEAP assay decides.**

## The thermostabilized detergent antigen can pass a molecule that never activates

The binding assay construct is designed to be permissive, and that is a trap. The **Sino 13944-H18H-DD antigen (Arg24–Ser432, N-FLAG + C-His, HEK293-expressed, DDM/CHS detergent, undisclosed thermostabilizing mutations)** contains both the ECD groove and the full 7TM, and its ECD groove is natively folded — it captures dulaglutide-hIgG4 (EC50 10–30 ng/mL) and an anti-GLP1R antibody (2–6 ng/mL) ([Sino product page](https://www.sinobiological.com/recombinant-proteins/human-glp1r-13944-h18h-dd)). So the construct will register **any** ECD- or vestibule-binder, **including antagonists** — making **affinity rank a poor predictor of functional rank**. Three construct-specific risks compound this [PR/T3 extrapolation]: (1) **thermostabilizing mutations frequently work by preventing the agonist conformation** (β1AR thermostabilization lowered agonist affinity ~10×, more for efficacious agonists), so a binder optimized to the construct's 7TM pocket may be stabilizing an inactive state and read as a non-agonist on wild-type cells ([Serrano-Vega 2008, *PNAS*](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0711253105); [Frontiers Pharmacol 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2015.00082/full)); (2) **DDM/CHS detergent under-represents the active/agonist conformation** (antagonist states are robust across mimetics; agonist states are detergent-sensitive; DDM/CHS is further from the bilayer than LMNG/CHS or nanodiscs) ([A2AR ¹⁹F-NMR, *JACS* 2024](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/jacs.4c15106)); (3) **tag artifacts** — the acidic N-FLAG sits adjacent to the ECD α1 helix that forms one wall of the groove, and His-capture surfaces give nonspecific binding, so standard SPR/BLI controls and an orthogonal immobilization (the AVI-biotin sibling 13944-H49H-B) are needed. The mitigations: **anchor affinity on the conformation-independent ECD groove** (identical in construct and wild-type, carrying no 7TM thermostabilizing mutations) for binding *transfer*, design the activating 7TM element against the **wild-type active-state structure (6X18 / 5NX2), not the construct**, and counter-screen against the **nanodisc sibling (13944-H98H-NA-B)**. Also model complex N-glycans at **N63/N82/N115** (all on the ECD, present in both assays) as a ~15–20 Å exclusion zone, since blocking glycosylation abolishes agonist binding ([AJP-Endo 2010](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/ajpendo.00067.2010)).

One construct-related fact is genuinely favorable: the **CRE-SEAP reporter is strongly amplified**, lowering the efficacy bar. Tirzepatide and MAR709 are partial for proximal Gαs recruitment (Emax 31% and 59%) yet give **cAMP Emax indistinguishable from the full agonist** because of downstream amplification, and a transcriptional reporter adds further gain ([Novikoff 2021, *Mol Metab*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2212877821000211)). So **a weak/partial de novo agonist can still read as functionally positive** — the organizer sets no published Emax floor and a confirmed partial agonist should count. But amplification does not rescue a zero-efficacy binder: the molecule still must drive some TM6 opening.

On compliance and scoring, the **novelty score is a 1–4 scale** where **level 4 ("de novo") requires sequence ≤30% identity (MMseqs2 vs SwissProt/PDB/patents/therapeutic-antibody DB/PLAbDab) AND less-than-moderate structural similarity (Foldseek→TM-align, no domain match at TM ≥0.5 over ≥70% coverage)**; this scientific score is **separate from the binary eligibility filter** (de novo/zero-shot/not-a-modified-binder) ([Adaptyv novelty](https://www.adaptyvbio.com/blog/novelty)). De novo helical bundles typically cap at level 3 on the structural axis, so an unusual fold plus a novel method earns full credit. Submission is a **ranked CSV (`name,sequence,molecule_class`, row order = ranking), ≤20 designs, 10–250 aa, molecule_class ∈ {protein, nanobody, scfv, fab_kappa, fab_lambda}, Fab as `{VH}:{VL}`**, names published verbatim (use neutral codenames, never the user's identity/email), everything released under **ODC-BY** including negative results, with biosecurity screening on synthesized designs ([Challenge-3 page](https://proteinbase.com/competitions/anthropic-adaptyv-2026/challenges/glp1r); [Terms](https://proteinbase.com/competitions/anthropic-adaptyv-2026/terms)). Liability filters for a de novo `protein`: **no unpaired Cys, low SAP/hydrophobic-patch, acceptable CamSol solubility**; antibody formats add **TAP** plus a CDR motif scan (N-X-[S/T], NG/NS, DG/DS, free Cys) — treating motifs as flags, not verdicts. Because selection is multi-metric (quality + design-novelty + method-novelty, "not a single in-silico metric"), the ≤20 slots are a **diversity exercise**: spread across distinct folds and generative methods, cap cousins.

## The 8×H100 stack is installed and license-clean

Tooling is not a bottleneck. The machine is a local **8× NVIDIA H100 80GB** install (driver 580, CUDA 13-capable), and the generate→sequence→fold→rank stack is **~90% already installed and reusable** from the sibling TNF project's `uplifting-biomolecular-modeling` kits, with every GLP1R-scale complex fitting comfortably on a single H100 ([local inventory, note 07]). The stack is dominated by **permissive licenses (MIT/Apache-2.0/BSD-3/CC-BY)** that cleanly permit open ODC-BY release of sequences and predicted structures: RFdiffusion/RFpeptides/RFdiffusion3 (BSD-3), ProteinMPNN/LigandMPNN/Boltz-2/BoltzGen (MIT), Chai-1/Genie3/PXDesign/Protenix-v1/Caliby (Apache-2.0), BindCraft + AF2 params (MIT + CC-BY). For a clean ODC-BY provenance chain, **keep four tools out of the submitted-design pipeline**: **AlphaFold3 official weights** (gated, non-commercial, output-notice + no-distillation — use OpenFold3/Boltz-2/Chai-1 instead), **Protenix-v2 weights** (proprietary, 403), **ESM-3/ESMC-600M** (Cambrian non-commercial — use ESM-2/ESMC-300M), and **Profluent-E1** (attribution). **PyRosetta** is free for non-commercial use but raises a commercial-status question for the challenge. The only genuinely missing pieces are the **novelty gate (FoldSeek + MMseqs2)** and, critically, **EvoBind/EvoBind2 is not confirmed installed** — the single most important generator for the top strategy would need to be set up and its methods read from the preprint PDF. Reproducibility is covered by seedable generators and the kits' `--det 1` bitwise mode for byte-stable submitted artifacts.

## Ranked strategies: thread a de novo message, don't coat the ECD

The core design logic: GLP1R activation is an **intramolecular conformational change**, so reject clustering/valency entirely and pursue **active-state stabilization**, with **format dictating whether activation is even reachable**. Strategies are graded by the strength of evidence that the mechanism *activates* (not just binds). The cross-cutting rule for all of them: **every candidate must be treated as a presumptive antagonist until the cAMP/CRE-SEAP assay proves otherwise**, and the CSV should be led by the top strategies while spreading across lineages for the multi-metric selection.

| Rank | Format × Epitope × Mechanism | Activation rationale + evidence | Novelty ceiling | Primary failure modes | Validation required |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **`protein` micro-binder (<40 aa), optionally cyclic/stapled × 7TM orthosteric pocket (His7-surrogate + A8–D15 footprint under the Trp33 cap) × active-state stabilization by message insertion** | Mimics the native message (Zhang 2017), Boc5 pocket-occupancy full agonism, MRGPRX1 class-A method; **direct precedent EvoBind EC50 32 nM** | **Medium.** Sequence-novel is achievable (EvoBind "no similarity"); small peptides may still hit structural matches — aim for an unusual constrained fold | Binds-but-doesn't-activate; can't thread deep enough; macrocycle format-eligibility unconfirmed; thermostabilized construct masks activation | Active-state co-fold on 6X18 reproducing message contacts vs GLP-1/exendin positive + scramble negative controls; GaMD TM6-opening; **[ASSAY-ONLY] cAMP/CRE-SEAP** |
| **2** | **`protein` mini-binder (40–100 aa) × ECD groove (address, around glycans) + 7TM vestibule (message) × reconstruct native two-domain activation** | Directly instantiates the validated two-domain model (Zhang 2017; de Graaf 2016); 11-mer shows address can be minimal; IR precedent shows fused-module geometry can induce an active conformation | **Medium-high.** A fully de novo two-domain class B agonist is essentially unprecedented → strong design + method novelty | Geometric coupling (address must present message at correct register/depth); more moving parts; still risks antagonism | As (1) plus ECD-binding transfer check (ECD is conformation-independent across construct/WT); confirm the address does not lock the auto-inhibited ECD |

**Strategy 1 (top pick, Medium confidence)** is the only route with a direct GLP1R agonism precedent and the simplest geometry. Build it EvoBind-style (sequence/learned rediscovery of an activating surface) rather than by grafting the GLP-1 N-terminus, both to stay eligible and because grafting converges on the native motif. The chief caveat: EvoBind's precedent is a single preprint and the macrocycle format-eligibility must be confirmed — a **linear ≤40-mer micro-protein is explicitly allowed** and is the safer encoding. **Strategy 2 (Medium-low)** edges Strategy 1 on novelty and on buying ECD affinity/selectivity that a bare message lacks, and it has the strongest mechanistic rationale, at the cost of a harder geometric-coupling problem; it is the best novelty×mechanism fit and a natural second lineage. **Strategy 3 (Medium-low/Low)** is the high-method-novelty hedge — orthogonal to every orthosteric attempt — but it asks a designed protein to bind a hydrophobic membrane surface, which is hard to design, express, and validate (and the co-folders cannot see the membrane). **Strategy 4 (Low for agonism)** is the only route that trivially passes the *binding* assay, which is exactly why it is dangerous: the documented outcome of ECD-targeting de novo binders at class B is **antagonism**. Include an antibody-format design only if it is explicitly built against the active-state ECD pose and gated functionally, and understand it is most likely to be an antagonist. A sensible ≤20-slot portfolio leads with several Strategy-1 micro-binders and Strategy-2 two-domain mini-agonists across distinct folds/methods, adds one or two Strategy-3 exoframe moonshots for method-novelty, and at most a single Strategy-4 antibody as a long-shot — never 20 cousins of one lineage.

## Gaps and organizer-unspecified unknowns

Several load-bearing facts are **not knowable from the available evidence and must not be fabricated**. The organizer has published **no numeric threshold** for binding affinity, EC50, Emax, or fold-over-basal that defines a "hit," and **does not name the reference agonist** (plausibly GLP-1 or exendin-4, but unconfirmed) — so the agonism bar is qualitative ("confirmed in a functional assay"). The **identity, number, and state-bias of the construct's thermostabilizing mutations are undisclosed**, which is the single largest translation risk and the reason the wild-type active-state structure, not the commercial antigen, is the correct design reference. Scientifically, there is **no peer-reviewed de novo class B GPCR agonist** anywhere — the entire top of the strategy ranking rests on one preprint (EvoBind) plus class-A method transfer, so class-B agonism is **proof-of-feasibility, not a validated recipe**. There is **no prospective, experimentally validated in-silico agonism predictor** for any class B GPCR; the proposed active-state-co-fold-plus-MD gate is an unvalidated hypothesis-generator that must be positive-controlled against GLP-1/exendin/Boc5, and whether a bulky protein loop can physically thread past the Trp33 cap into the core pocket has never been structurally demonstrated. Procedurally, **macrocycle eligibility** as a "protein," the exact CSV strictness, the selection-prompt weighting of quality vs novelty vs method-novelty, the novelty DB snapshot versions, and whether unselected designs are published are all unspecified. Finally, two local setup gaps matter for execution: **FoldSeek/MMseqs2 (the novelty gate) and EvoBind/EvoBind2 (the top-strategy generator) are not confirmed installed**, and the EvoBind methods (cyclization details, how agonism was quantified) should be read from the preprint PDF before committing to that route.

## Conclusion

The decisive reframing this synthesis produces is that **GLP1R Challenge-3 is not a binder problem with an activation bonus — it is an activation problem in which binding is the easy, misleading part.** The field can already make sub-nanomolar GLP1R binders, and the detergent antigen will happily report them, but the best-resourced de novo lab on Earth turned every one of its class B binders into an antagonist, and the only molecule that has ever de-novo-activated GLP1R is a three-member set of cyclic peptides in a preprint. That asymmetry should set expectations: the realistic goal is not a confident agonist but a **well-reasoned, diversity-spread bet** that a compact, sequence-novel, pocket-threading peptide/mini-protein can do what no designed protein has yet done at a class B receptor — with the explicit acceptance that most candidates will be antagonists and that **only the cAMP/CRE-SEAP assay can tell agonist from impostor**.

What this changes going forward: the entire antibody/clustering intuition from receptor-agonism literature should be discarded for GLP1R, computational interface scores should be demoted from "selection criterion" to "binding triage with controls," and design effort should concentrate on the one lever with any precedent — threading a de novo His7-surrogate message into the 7TM pocket on the active-state 6X18 template, validated by explicit-state dynamics rather than by a good-looking co-fold. The CRE-SEAP amplification is the quiet ally here: it means a *partial* agonist that merely nudges TM6 can still score, which is exactly the kind of modest, first-of-its-kind result this frontier task can realistically deliver within the 2026-10-25 deadline.
