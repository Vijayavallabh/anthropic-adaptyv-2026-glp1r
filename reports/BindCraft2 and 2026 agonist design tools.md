# BindCraft2 leads eligible tools; only assays prove agonism

**BindCraft2 v1.0.4 — published 2026-10-19, the same day this research was done — is a clean GO for ODC-BY submission: its entire design/validation stack is AlphaFold 2 plus ProteinMPNN (with HyperMPNN weights for the positive term), with no AF3, Protenix-v2, ESM-3 or ESMC anywhere in the loop, and its bespoke "Source-Available (Hosting-Restricted)" license explicitly permits sharing design outputs such as sequences and structures ([README](https://github.com/PacesaLab/BindCraft2); [LICENSE](https://github.com/PacesaLab/BindCraft2/blob/main/LICENSE)).** But the two BindCraft2 features that sound activation-relevant are not: its `induced_fit` objective reshapes the *binder's* own surface between free and bound states — it does not induce or score a receptor conformation — and its VHH/scFv/ARP (Ankyrin Repeat Protein) modalities all build disulfide-bearing folded domains that violate this project's linear, natural-AA, no-disulfide constraints, leaving the **12–25-residue linear peptide modality as the only BindCraft2 mode that fits** ([reference](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PacesaLab/BindCraft2/main/docs/source/reference.md); [modality guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PacesaLab/BindCraft2/main/docs/source/design-guide/03-choosing-a-modality.md)). The wider 2025–2026 wave — BoltzGen, BoltzDesign1, RFdiffusion3, PXDesign (all GO), against Chai-2, AlphaProteo, and any AF3/ESM-3-in-loop pipeline (all NO-GO) — can condition on a supplied active-state structure such as 6X18, but every one of them treats the receptor as near-rigid context, so active-state engagement is a consequence of *which PDB you freeze as the target*, never a tool-computed activation. Above all, the firewall holds without exception: no validated in-silico method separates agonism from binding for a class-B GPCR, the single published de novo GPCR agonist is for class-A MRGPRX1, and GLP1R together with every class-B sibling the Baker lab targeted yielded **antagonists only** ([bioRxiv 2025.03.23.644666](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.23.644666v1.full)). Everything structural below is therefore framed as a necessary condition you can bias toward — deep TMD-core insertion of a pre-organized natural-AA helix, engagement of ECL3 and the central polar network — and never as a prediction of efficacy, which only the wet cAMP/SEAP assay can settle.

## BindCraft2 v1.0.4's AlphaFold-2-only stack clears the ODC-BY bar

BindCraft2 (BC2) is a ground-up second-generation rewrite of the original BindCraft binder pipeline by Martin Pacesa's group, now at the University of Zurich, and it is brand new: the public release line runs **v1.0.0 (2026-09-16) → v1.0.1 → v1.0.2 → v1.0.3 → v1.0.4 (2026-10-19)**, confirmed by the GitHub releases API `published_at` timestamps ([releases](https://github.com/PacesaLab/BindCraft2/releases); [releases API](https://api.github.com/repos/PacesaLab/BindCraft2/releases)). This is a separate repository and version line from the 2024 tool (martinpacesa/BindCraft, now at v1.5.3, MIT-licensed) — the two must not be conflated ([BindCraft releases](https://github.com/martinpacesa/BindCraft/releases)). BC2 generalizes the original miniprotein-only tool into a multi-modality suite (de novo miniprotein, VHH, scFv, Fab, ARP/ankyrin, linear and cyclic peptide, plus the conformational objectives `induced_fit` and `fold_switch`), and the **v1.0.4 headline is "Fold switching and induced fit modality improvements"**: scoring is now performed only on confidently folded residues, a new RMSD-based `fold_switch_delta` objective is added, interface-contact weighting is doubled (1.0→2.0), and antibody modalities are retuned to bind through the CDRs rather than the framework ([releases API](https://api.github.com/repos/PacesaLab/BindCraft2/releases)). One caveat on maturity: **no BC2 preprint or paper exists** — all documentation is the GitHub repo and docs site — and at roughly five weeks old with a days-old point release, the tool is under active churn.

The eligibility verdict is the decisive fact for this project, and it is **GO on both provenance and license**. On provenance, the README names only **AlphaFold 2 and ProteinMPNN**; a campaign uses seven AF2 models (five multimer models for design plus two monomer models for validation, a 5.3 GB standard DeepMind parameter archive), and the "positive" design term draws on **HyperMPNN** weights — a ProteinMPNN re-weighting, not a protein language model and not ESM-derived ([README raw](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PacesaLab/BindCraft2/main/README.md); [installation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PacesaLab/BindCraft2/main/docs/source/installation.md)). There is no AF3, no Protenix, no ESM-3 or ESMC in the stack, so BC2 sits squarely in the "AF2-based provenance is acceptable" bucket and designs are eligible to publish under ODC-BY. On license, BC2 ships under the bespoke **"BindCraft2 Source-Available License (Hosting-Restricted)"** (© 2026 Martin Pacesa, University of Zurich), which grants free use including internal commercial use, permits modification and redistribution, and — critically — states that **"you can share design outputs such as binders, sequences, or structures"**; the only restrictions (no third-party hosted SaaS/API offering, no use of the "BindCraft2" trademark to market a derivative) do not touch a local design-and-submit campaign ([LICENSE](https://github.com/PacesaLab/BindCraft2/blob/main/LICENSE)). Note this is a change from the original BindCraft's plain MIT. The declared third-party components are themselves permissive (AF2 code + ColabDesign under Apache-2.0, ProteinMPNN under MIT; AF2 model parameters "obtained separately" under their own terms — commonly CC-BY-4.0 but not pulled first-hand this session, so verify before leaning on it for a strict ODC-BY claim). Operationally, BC2 writes a `campaign_metadata.json` recording model choices and checkpoint hashes — the natural artifact to cite as byte-level provenance when documenting submission eligibility ([README raw](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PacesaLab/BindCraft2/main/README.md)).

Availability and hardware are routine for the local 8×H100 DGX. Everything lives at **github.com/PacesaLab/BindCraft2**; install is a one-shot `bash install.sh` (venv-based), AF2 weights auto-download on first use or via `bindcraft fetch-weights`, and the CUDA-13 wheels need compute capability ≥7.5 (H100 qualifies) ([installation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PacesaLab/BindCraft2/main/docs/source/installation.md)). Memory scales as roughly `2.0 × (3.4 GB + 38 kB × N²)` per worker, so a short peptide against a few-hundred-residue GLP1R target sits far under the 24 GB-per-worker regime, and a 40-trajectory campaign ran **3620 s at one worker and 2021 s at seven on a GH200** — a peptide campaign is well under an hour and many workers per GPU are feasible ([installation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PacesaLab/BindCraft2/main/docs/source/installation.md)).

## In BindCraft2, "induced fit" reshapes the binder, not the receptor

The single most important clarification for this project is what BC2's `induced_fit` objective actually does, because the name invites exactly the over-reading the firewall forbids. **`induced_fit` is binder-side: it optimizes the designed binder's own binding surface to change shape between its free and bound states, judged by the binder's free-versus-bound interface RMSD and TM-score.** The README defines it as "a conformational objective for a binder whose binding surface changes shape between free and bound states"; the reference docs say it "requests movement of the binding surface" and "freezes one bound structure to compare a free state against"; and a `Binder_RMSD` gate is deliberately switched off because it "measures the very displacement they exist to create" — i.e., the binder's own backbone is what moves ([README raw](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PacesaLab/BindCraft2/main/README.md); [reference](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PacesaLab/BindCraft2/main/docs/source/reference.md); [modality guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PacesaLab/BindCraft2/main/docs/source/design-guide/03-choosing-a-modality.md)). The v1.0.4 acceptance gates (`min_induced_fit_interface_rmsd_final` 2 Å to "require movement of the binding surface", `max_induced_fit_tm_final` 0.85 to "require a whole-fold change") compare the binder's two states, not the target's ([releases API](https://api.github.com/repos/PacesaLab/BindCraft2/releases)). One ambiguous phrase in the "Typical applications" column lists induced_fit for "targets that change shape on binding (conformational selection) … allosteric or state-selective binders," but every mechanistic and scoring statement describes the binder's shape change; the binder-side reading rests on four-plus convergent statements and is high-confidence though not author-confirmed.

This maps cleanly and safely onto the firewall. The target in BC2 is **supplied as exactly one fixed structure that the tool templates and freezes** — `weights_target_rmsd` and `weights_target_rigidity` keep it at the supplied coordinates (both off by default but available), and `target_flexibility` only withholds target *sidechain/sequence* information while retaining the backbone; it does not let the backbone flex ([reference](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PacesaLab/BindCraft2/main/docs/source/reference.md)). You therefore engage the active state by **choosing the input PDB — the project's active-state 6X18 7TM coordinate set** — not by any induce-the-target mechanism; BC2 will not morph an inactive input into an active pocket. So `induced_fit` is legitimately usable as a *binder-geometry* lever (letting a peptide fold-on-binding to complement a deep vestibule), and it must never be reported as evidence the design will activate GLP1R. For the 6X18 vestibule specifically, the docs' own guidance points at the peptide modality: "grooves and pockets want an extended peptide or VHH CDR3," the peptide modality is "a short linear peptide into a groove or pocket — not a flat surface," and "BC2 designs against what you give it, membrane and glycan context included or not" ([design guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PacesaLab/BindCraft2/main/docs/source/design-guide.md); [modality guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PacesaLab/BindCraft2/main/docs/source/design-guide/03-choosing-a-modality.md)). The honest gap: **no GPCR, 7TM, membrane-protein or vestibule design result appears anywhere in BC2's docs or in search — GPCRs are never named — so whether AF2 co-folding reliably places a de novo peptide inside a class-B 7TM vestibule is untested and undocumented here**, and independent evidence (below) says class-B deep insertion is unprecedented de novo.

On the other advertised modalities, the verdict is that they are off-constraint for this project. **VHH** is a single-domain antibody scaffold with editable CDRs (sampling extended and folded-back CDR3), aimed at "concave/cryptic epitopes and enzyme active sites"; **scFv** is heavy + light variable domains as two chains; and **ARP is an Ankyrin Repeat Protein — a DARPin-like consensus ankyrin-repeat scaffold with editable repeat positions**, not any membrane or receptor term ([modality guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PacesaLab/BindCraft2/main/docs/source/design-guide/03-choosing-a-modality.md)). All three are folded domains that rely on framework disulfides and are not linear peptides, so they fail the project's "linear, natural-AA, no reliable disulfide" trio. Only the **peptide modality** ("a 12–25 residue linear peptide, judged bound without a free fold") satisfies all three constraints, and it also accommodates the expressed-construct layout: treat the N-terminal (f)Met and the C-terminal GFP11/Twin-Strep tag as non-designed flanking segments and keep hotspots away from both termini.

The eligibility pivot is almost entirely the **weights license of the generator and of any model kept *in the generation loop*** (an eval-only AF3 step is avoidable and does not by itself sink eligibility). Three of the strongest general designers (BoltzGen, BoltzDesign1, RFdiffusion3) are permissively licensed down to the weights, and all four leading co-folding-inversion tools sidestep the AF3-weights trap. ## No in-silico score separates agonism from binding — class B yields antagonists only

The firewall is not a conservative house rule; it is what the 2023–2026 literature actually shows. **The single wet-lab-confirmed de novo GPCR agonist is for the class-A receptor MRGPRX1**; in the same landmark Baker/Skape *Nature* 2026 study, CXCR4, OXTR, and the class-B peptide receptors **GLP1R, GIPR, GCGR and CGRPR all yielded antagonists** ([bioRxiv 2025.03.23.644666](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.23.644666v1.full); [DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10656-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10656-8)). That the group which pioneered de novo GPCR design pursued GLP1R as an antagonist is the strongest available signal that **class-B agonism remained out of reach even for them**, and no published de novo class-B agonist of any modality — miniprotein or peptide-from-scratch — was found in this sweep. The lone apparent counterexample, Wei et al.'s AI-designed GLP-1RAs with 52% in-vitro success and ~3× longer half-life than semaglutide ([Adv Sci 2025, DOI 10.1002/advs.202507044](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202507044)), is a sequence redesign of the **GLP-1 backbone** that inherits the native activation pharmacophore — it says nothing about discovering activation de novo and must not be over-read.

On the prediction side, every would-be agonism score fails for class B. The best functional classifier, GPCRact, reaches 0.819 three-class balanced accuracy but is **~93% class-A data, degrades out-of-family (leave-one-receptor-out 0.63–0.77), and reports binding accuracy (0.890) above its function-inclusive accuracy (0.819), with activity predictions "near-random when binding is misclassified"** — the functional call is strictly downstream of getting binding right ([PMC12805254](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12805254/)). Co-folders compound the problem: default AlphaFold2 produces a predominantly inactive GPCR state (training imbalance of 178 inactive vs 60 active structures), and AF3 predicts peptide ligand poses poorly — precisely the class-B agonist case ([bioRxiv 2021.11.26.470086](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.11.26.470086); [Acta Pharmacol Sin 2024, DOI 10.1038/s41401-024-01429-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41401-024-01429-y)). The best-powered binder oracle (Overath et al., 3,766 binders) ranks **binding** via AF3 ipSAE_min with per-target average precision swinging from ~0.1 to ~1, and never function ([bioRxiv 2025.08.14.670059](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.14.670059v1.full)). Because agonists and antagonists share the orthosteric site and co-folders lean inactive, a single-conformation confidence score reflects binding plausibility in a non-signaling geometry — not efficacy. The operational consequence is a steep **"reachability tax" with no agonism payoff**: within the one GPCR pipeline that exists, a deep class-A pocket (CXCR4) gave **2 hits from ~26,000 screened designs**, whereas the class-B GLP1R campaign bound the solvent-exposed extracellular-domain surface (27 nM and 5.3 nM binders) rather than the deep 7TM core, and the CGRPR ECD-surface campaign gave **36 of 78 active** ([bioRxiv 2025.03.23.644666](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.23.644666v1.full)). High-affinity ECD/surface binding is the easy, antagonist-prone outcome; it is exactly what to avoid reading as progress toward agonism. The wet cAMP/SEAP assay is the sole arbiter.

## Bias the design toward deep insertion and a pre-organized natural-AA helix

What the newest class-B activation literature gives is not a score but a set of **necessary conditions you can design toward**, and the 2024–2026 cryo-EM/MD record sharpens them considerably. Activation is now understood as a two-step, G-protein-coupled process: ligand-free GLP1R–Gs structures show the **Gs protein alone can pull the intracellular TM bundle (including TM6) into a near-active geometry with no peptide bound**, so the agonist's distinctive job is to complete the *extracellular* reorganization — insert its N-terminal head into the TMD orthosteric core and set the ECL3/polar-network states ([Cong et al., Cell Discovery 2024, PMC10861504](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10861504/); PDB 8WG7). TM6-out is necessary but not sufficient. The "G-protein-first" MD model makes the same point from the other side: a Gs-precoupled, TM6-bent-out receptor stays **unactivated until an agonist N-terminus arrives** ([ACS figshare 27011779](https://acs.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/The_G_Protein-First_Mechanism_for_Activation_of_the_Class_B_Glucagon-like_Peptide_1_Receptor_Coupled_to_N_Terminal_Domain-Mediated_Conformational_Progression/27011779/1)). This yields the clearest design discriminator available: the depth analysis in the Baker GPCR paper measures the deepest heavy atom relative to the predicted membrane boundary and finds that **how far a ligand reaches into the orthosteric pocket below the membrane surface tracks with agonist-versus-antagonist function** — an antagonist differs chiefly by shallower penetration, filling the vestibule and blocking the native N-terminus without pushing the microswitches ([IPD PDF](https://www.ipd.uw.edu/publication-pdfs/432/db1453519cebe452e987a99f60ceb945/De%20novo%20design%20of%20miniproteins%20targeting%20GPCRs.pdf)). This is a necessary-condition filter to bias toward, never an efficacy ranking: a pose that engages only the ECD or the pocket rim fails it, but satisfying it does not predict Emax.

Three biasable levers follow, all compatible with the natural-AA / linear / no-disulfide constraints. **(1) Insertion depth:** design the inserting element to reach deep into the TMD orthosteric core below the membrane boundary, not merely to perch on the vestibule rim — the activator/antagonist discriminator above. **(2) Microswitch engagement:** bias toward displacing ECL3 outward (now identified as a G-protein-bias switch, where N-terminal modifications such as Ac-semaglutide produce G-protein bias and prolonged cAMP) and toward engaging the central polar network, whose recurrent communication hub is R190^2.60b ([Cell Reports 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117718](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117718); [eLife 96513](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/96513/figures); [Mol Pharmacol 2016](https://molpharm.aspetjournals.org/content/molpharm/early/2015/12/23/mol.115.101246.full.pdf)). **(3) Natural-AA helix pre-organization:** the activation pharmacophore compresses to a short N-terminal element (~8–11 residues), and pre-organizing it as an α-helix is the dominant potency lever — up to ~1000-fold in the ultra-short 11-mer GLP-1R agonist series, where the mechanism is entropic stabilization of the α-helical binding conformation in the *unbound* state ([Sawyer et al., Molecules 2024, PMC11721672](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11721672/)). The transferable, constraint-compatible move is to base the activator on the GLP-1 N-terminal element (the 11-mer SAR flags Gly10/Phe12/Thr13 as load-bearing while His7 is surprisingly forgiving) and pre-organize it with **natural means — i,i+4 Glu↔Lys/Arg salt bridges, Ala-rich stretches, N-/C-cap motifs** — since helicity, not the unnatural α-methyl group per se, is the mechanistic driver.

Two constraint collisions must be stated plainly. First, **the strongest published minimal activators rely on non-natural residues (Aib, Bip) or disulfide macrocyclization** — both off-limits here — so this project's constraint set is in relatively uncharted territory and a potency/efficacy penalty versus those benchmarks should be expected ([PMC11721672](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11721672/); PDB 9J1P). Second, the **expressed-construct layout is itself a mechanistic risk**: the native activating N-terminus (His7) sits deep and makes precise polar contacts, so a retained N-terminal (f)Met or extension could sterically block deep insertion — the activator/antagonist discriminator — while a C-terminal GFP11/Twin-Strep tag is comparatively safe if appended as a non-designed segment away from the interface. Biasing toward a correctly shaped, unobstructed N-terminal helical head is therefore a necessary condition, and the dynamic, ensemble nature of the bound agonist (a "conformational spectrum rather than a single fixed structure") is a further reason a single static co-fold pose can never be read as "active" ([ChemBioChem 2023, DOI 10.1002/cbic.202300504](https://doi.org/10.1002/cbic.202300504)).

## Conclusion

The tool landscape and the science point in the same direction and reinforce the firewall rather than soften it. On tools, the decision is clean: **BindCraft2 v1.0.4 is a GO whose only constraint-compliant mode is the linear peptide modality, and whose `induced_fit` is a binder-geometry lever, not an activation signal**; it is joined by a GO cohort (BoltzGen, BoltzDesign1, RFdiffusion3, PXDesign, with La-Proteina/Complexa GO-with-conditions and the peptide diffusion models pending a LICENSE check) and a NO-GO set defined almost entirely by closed or non-commercial *weights* (Chai-2, AlphaProteo, and any AF3- or ESM-3-in-loop pipeline). Every one of these tools engages the active state only by being handed the active-state 6X18 coordinates as fixed context — a designer's choice, not a computed outcome. On the science, the newest cryo-EM and MD work does not hand over an agonism predictor; it hands over sharper *necessary conditions* — deep sub-membrane TMD-core insertion, ECL3 and central-polar-network engagement, and a pre-organized N-terminal helix buildable from natural amino acids — each a go/no-go bias a surface-binding antagonist would fail, none a ranker of efficacy.

The implication going forward is that the campaign's in-silico budget should buy **diversity across distinct deep-insertion-biased lineages, not confidence in any one design's agonism**, because no computational step can earn that confidence for a class-B GPCR. Reaching the GLP1R 7TM core de novo is unprecedented and the reachability tax is steep, so the rational posture is many credible, constraint-compliant, depth-and-helix-biased peptide bets, each stripped of obstruction at the activating N-terminus and tagged only at the C-terminus — with the wet cAMP/SEAP assay left as the one and only judge of whether any of them crosses from binding into agonism.
