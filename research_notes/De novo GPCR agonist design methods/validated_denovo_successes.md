# Experimentally-Validated De Novo Protein/Peptide Design Successes (2023–2026): Membrane Receptors, Conformational Selectivity, and Functional Signaling

Scope: wet-lab-validated de novo designs 2023–2026 targeting membrane receptors, requiring conformational selectivity, or producing functional (not merely binding) signaling outcomes. Methods-and-evidence level. "Binding-only" vs "functional cell-assay" validation is flagged throughout. Primary sources preferred.

> **Headline for the GLP1R-agonist project:** The single most relevant paper (Baker lab / Skape Bio, *Nature* 2026) demonstrated de novo GPCR miniproteins, but the only experimentally-confirmed **agonist** was for **MRGPRX1** (a class-A, Gq-coupled itch receptor). **GLP1R was explicitly targeted — but as an ANTAGONIST**, alongside GIPR and GCGR antagonists. No published de novo protein/peptide **GLP1R agonist** was found in this search. This is a direct calibration point: de novo *GPCR agonism* has been shown once, on an easy class-A peptidergic receptor, not on a class-B secretin-family receptor like GLP1R.

---

## Key Question 1: De novo binders/peptides targeting GPCRs or other membrane receptors validated 2023–2026 — method and assay (binding vs functional)

### Takeaway
Three substantive membrane-receptor cases exist in-window: (a) Baker/Skape *Nature* 2026 GPCR miniproteins — the first de novo designs that bind GPCR orthosteric/transmembrane pockets with conformational-state selectivity, validated functionally in living cells and by cryo-EM; (b) Baker *Molecular Cell* 2025 insulin-receptor (IR, an RTK) agonists — functionally validated (autophosphorylation, MAPK, glucose-lowering in vivo); (c) a 2026 conference poster on RFdiffusion/BindCraft binders to the GPCR RXFP1 (preliminary). A separate, widely-cited 2024 paper (Vazquez Torres, *Nature*) designs binders to GPCR peptide *ligands* (e.g., glucagon, PTH) — NOT to receptors — and is binding/biosensing only.

### Cited Findings
- **Baker lab + Skape Bio, "De novo design of miniproteins targeting GPCRs," *Nature* 656(8129):1044–1053, published 21 May 2026.** First author Edin Muratspahić; corresponding Christoffer Norn (Skape Bio); senior David Baker (IPD/UW). Abstract: "we describe de novo design methods and a high-throughput receptor-diversion microscopy-based screen for generating GPCR-binding miniproteins with high affinity, potency and selectivity. We design miniprotein agonists that activate receptors involved in itch and pain, as well as antagonists that inhibit receptors implicated in cancer, metabolic disorders such as diabetes and obesity, and migraines. The cryo-EM structures of five receptor-bound designs are close to the computational design models. A designed chemokine receptor antagonist mobilizes haematopoietic stem and progenitor cells in vivo at a level comparable to a clinically used drug, with fewer adverse effects." — According to PubMed, PMID 42168559, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10656-8)
- **The matching bioRxiv preprint names the exact targets** (same author list; "design of miniprotein agonists and antagonists targeting G protein-coupled receptors," bioRxiv 23 Mar 2025): "the use of these methods to generate **MRGPRX1 agonists and CXCR4, GLP1R, GIPR, GCGR and CGRPR antagonists**. Cryo-electron microscopy data reveals atomic-level agreement between designed and experimentally determined structures for **CGRPR-bound antagonists and MRGPRX1-bound agonists, confirming precise conformational control of receptor function**." — According to PubMed, PMID 40501737, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.03.23.644666); [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.23.644666)
- The GPCR designs are miniproteins (<100 aa) built to fit deep, flexible receptor pockets and to recognize specific receptor states (active vs inactive); cryo-EM of five receptor-bound designs matched the computational models; designs "control GPCR signaling in living cells" (functional). The high-throughput "receptor diversion" screen tested tens of thousands of designed proteins in living human cells, keeping receptors in their native membrane environment. — [GEN news summary of the Nature paper](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/drug-discovery/ai-designs-miniprotein-switches-for-gpcr-targeting/)
- GPCR author list includes GPCR-pharmacology/structure labs (Bryan Roth/UNC NIMH PDSP; Patrick Sexton & Denise Wootten/Monash cryo-EM; Christopher Tate/MRC-LMB; Arun Shukla/IIT-K), plus Novo Nordisk and Lundbeck co-authors — i.e., functional GPCR assays and cryo-EM were done by specialist collaborators. — According to PubMed, PMID 42168559, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10656-8)
- **Insulin receptor (RTK), Baker lab: Wang et al., "Tuning insulin receptor signaling using de novo-designed agonists," *Molecular Cell* 85:4064 (2025)**; bioRxiv 2024.10.07.617068. Method: de novo design of binders to individual IR extracellular domains, then fusion of these binders in different orientations and with different conformational flexibility to lock/loosen receptor conformation. Validation is FUNCTIONAL: "a series of synthetic agonists that signal through the IR that differ from insulin … in the induction of receptor autophosphorylation, MAPK activation, intracellular trafficking, and cell proliferation." Cryo-EM: PDB 9DN6 (agonist RF-405, 4.0 Å), 9DNI (S2-F1-S1, 8.2 Å), 9DNN (insulin + site-2 binder, 6.1 Å). — [bioRxiv preprint/abstract](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.07.617068v1); [DOI (Mol Cell)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2025.09.020)
- **RXFP1 (relaxin family peptide receptor 1, a class-A GPCR), 51st Lorne Proteins Conference 2026 student poster (PRELIMINARY, not peer-reviewed).** Method: RFdiffusion + BindCraft + ProteinMPNN; top 48 BindCraft and 96 RFdiffusion designs selected for testing. Results: eight antagonist candidates showed binding by bio-layer interferometry (BLI) and nanomolar-range competition in functional inhibition studies; for agonist candidates from BindCraft, 66% of designs interacted with the target in a BLI screen. — [Lorne 2026 abstract](https://proteins-2026.p.asnevents.com.au/days/2026-02-09/abstract/132687)
- **Binds the ligand, NOT the receptor (binding-only):** Vazquez Torres et al., "De novo design of high-affinity binders of bioactive helical peptides," *Nature* 626:435–442 (2024). Extended RFdiffusion to design binders to flexible/helical targets using partial diffusion; produced picomolar-affinity binders to peptide hormones including **parathyroid hormone (PTH) and glucagon**. Application is detection/biosensing (mass-spec enrichment, bioluminescent biosensors) — no receptor and no signaling assay. PDB 8GJG, 8GJI, 8T5E, 8T5F. — [PubMed PMID 38109936](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38109936/)
- **RTK minibinder (binding/structural):** a KIT receptor tyrosine kinase minibinder from "β-pairing targeted RFdiffusion" methods work is deposited as PDB 9H71 (membrane-receptor-adjacent; not a signaling-function study). — [RCSB 9H71](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/9H71)

### Inferences
- The *Nature* 2026 GPCR paper is the first demonstration that de novo miniproteins can engage a GPCR's conformationally-dynamic pocket AND produce a defined functional outcome confirmed by cellular signaling + cryo-EM — a qualitative step beyond all prior "binding-only" membrane-receptor designs.
- Agonism was demonstrated only on MRGPRX1 (class A, Gq, peptide-sensing, relatively accessible pocket). The class-B secretin-family metabolic receptors (GLP1R, GIPR, GCGR) in the same paper were addressed as ANTAGONISTS. This strongly implies that, as of mid-2026, de novo *agonism* of a class-B GPCR (the GLP1R project's goal) has not been published and is materially harder than the antagonism/class-A-agonism that has been shown.
- The IR work shows the established, tractable route to de novo functional receptor *agonism* is geometric control of a receptor that signals by dimerization/conformational rearrangement (RTK), not orthosteric mimicry — a mechanism that does not transfer directly to single-chain GPCR orthosteric agonism.

### Gaps
- Per-target quantitative success rates, Kd/IC50/EC50 values, and the specific functional assay formats (cAMP, Ca2+, β-arrestin/BRET, GTPγS) for the *Nature* 2026 GPCR paper are not in the abstract and the full text/supplement was not retrievable in this session (Nature paywall; IPD full PDF exceeded fetch size limits). The named assay vendor Eurofins DiscoverX ("LeadHunter," β-arrestin PathHunter is their platform) appears in the author list, suggesting β-arrestin recruitment assays were used, but this is inference not a confirmed quote.
- Whether the GLP1R/GIPR/GCGR antagonists reached cell-based functional antagonism (vs binding only) is not separable from the abstract; cryo-EM confirmation is explicitly stated only for CGRPR antagonists and MRGPRX1 agonists.

## Key Question 2: Any de novo workflow that FUNCTIONALLY modulated a receptor (agonist/antagonist/PAM) confirmed in a cell assay (not binding only) — method, target class, measured outcome

### Takeaway
Yes — multiple, but concentrated in the Baker lab and almost entirely on receptors that signal by dimerization/clustering (cytokine receptors, RTKs, death receptors) rather than classical GPCR orthosteric pharmacology. The *Nature* 2026 GPCR paper is the first to add bona fide GPCR functional modulation (MRGPRX1 agonism; CXCR4/CGRPR etc. antagonism). PAM (positive allosteric modulator) de novo designs were not found.

### Cited Findings
- **GPCR, agonist + antagonist (functional, in vivo):** *Nature* 2026 (as above) — MRGPRX1 agonists activate the receptor; CXCR4/GLP1R/GIPR/GCGR/CGRPR antagonists inhibit; a designed **chemokine-receptor (CXCR4) antagonist mobilized haematopoietic stem and progenitor cells in vivo comparably to a clinically used drug** (the clinical comparator for HSPC mobilization is plerixafor/AMD3100, a CXCR4 antagonist). — According to PubMed, PMID 42168559, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10656-8); targets named in [bioRxiv DOI](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.03.23.644666)
- **Insulin receptor (RTK), agonist/partial-agonist/antagonist (functional, in vivo):** Baker *Mol Cell* 2025 — "Computational design yielded super agonists, partial agonists, and antagonists of IR"; "De novo agonists induce a distinct IR active conformation"; designs "tune IR signaling by modulating conformational dynamics"; "more potent than insulin, reducing glucose levels longer and activating disease-causing IR mutants." Functional readouts: autophosphorylation, MAPK activation, trafficking, proliferation; in vivo glucose-lowering. Design strategy: fuse domain-binders in varied orientation/flexibility to set receptor geometry. — [bioRxiv abstract](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.07.617068v1); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2025.09.020)
- **Cytokine receptors, functional agonism (in-window):** de novo IL-4 mimetic "Neo-4" built on a de novo IL-2-mimetic scaffold; hyperstable; signals exclusively through the type-I IL-4 receptor complex (separates type-I from type-II signaling) — functional cell signaling. *Nature Chemical Biology* 2023. — [Baker lab PDF (s41589-023-01313-6)](https://www.bakerlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/s41589-023-01313-6.pdf)
- **Cytokine receptor, functional (pre-window context):** de novo IL-2/IL-15 mimetic "Neo-2/15" (Silva et al., *Nature* 2019) binds IL-2Rβγc without IL-2Rα/IL-15Rα and "elicits downstream cell signaling independent of IL-2Rα"; Neo-2/15 showed superior anti-tumor activity vs IL-2 with reduced toxicity in mouse melanoma/colon models. Foundational but out of the 2023–2026 window. — [PMC6521699](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6521699)
- **EpoR surrogate agonists via geometry (pre-window context):** DARPin-scaffold dimerizers set erythropoietin-receptor dimer geometry, yielding full, partial, and biased agonism and stage-selective hematopoiesis effects (Mohan et al., 2019) — establishes "geometry sets agonism" principle that the IR paper later used with de novo binders. — [Baker lab PDF (Mohan2019)](https://www.bakerlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/Mohan2019_CytokineSignaling.pdf)
- **G-CSF receptor, agonist/antagonist (PRELIMINARY, conference abstract):** designed binders engineered to engage G-CSFR in different configurations to make full agonists, "tuning" agonists, or inhibitors; designs described as small, hyperthermostable, protease-resistant. ASH 2024 abstract — preliminary, not peer-reviewed. — [ASH 2024 abstract](https://ash.confex.com/ash/2024/webprogram/Paper209668.html)

### Inferences
- The recurring, de-risked mechanism for de novo functional *agonism* is geometric control of receptors that signal by ligand-induced dimerization/clustering (cytokine receptors, RTKs like IR). This is mechanistically distinct from GPCR orthosteric agonism, where a single miniprotein must push the 7TM bundle into an active conformation.
- The *Nature* 2026 GPCR result (MRGPRX1 agonist) is the exception that establishes de novo GPCR agonism is possible on at least one class-A peptidergic receptor, but the paper's own choice to pursue GLP1R/GIPR/GCGR as antagonists (not agonists) indicates class-B agonism remained out of reach even for the group that pioneered the method.
- No de novo PAM (positive allosteric modulator) was found; all functional modulators are orthosteric/geometric agonists or antagonists.

### Gaps
- Exact functional potencies (EC50/IC50) and efficacy (%Emax vs natural ligand) for the GPCR agonists/antagonists are not in retrievable abstracts.
- No published de novo GPCR *PAM* or biased-agonist design with cell-assay confirmation was found (search did not surface one).

## Key Question 3: Baker lab & peers — reported success rate and experimental workflow for hard targets requiring a specific bound conformation (designs ordered vs validated)

### Takeaway
Reported wet-lab hit rates vary enormously by method and era: energy-based + deep-learning filtering (Bennett/Coventry, 2023) raised success ~10× but kept absolute rates low (often <1%, 0 for some targets); newer hallucination/co-design pipelines (BindCraft) and DeepMind's AlphaProteo report far higher rates (tens of %), but with strong target dependence and acknowledged failures. The canonical workflow is: generate many designs in silico → AlphaFold2/RoseTTAFold self-consistency + interface-metric filtering → order a few dozen to a few thousand genes → screen (yeast display / SPR / BLI) → structurally and functionally validate top hits.

### Cited Findings
- **Bennett, Coventry et al. (Baker lab), "Improving de novo protein binder design with deep learning," *Nat. Commun.* 2023:** adding deep learning (AlphaFold2 or RoseTTAFold used to score both monomer folding and target binding) gave ~10× higher success rate vs energy-based design alone; absolute success rates among targets remained low (<1%), and no binders were found for some protein targets. — [Baker lab post](https://www.bakerlab.org/2023/05/08/deep-learning-improves-protein-binder-design-tenfold/)
- A secondary summary states the earlier 2023 Nature-line work had best-case hit rates ~0.5%, improved ~10× by AF2/RoseTTAFold triage. — [Rowan summary (secondary)](https://rowansci.substack.com/p/breaking-boltzgen-now-live-on-rowan)
- **Workflow / filtering:** Bennett's 2023 UW PhD thesis describes configuring AlphaFold2 to classify which in-silico designs are likely to bind, and pairing ProteinMPNN with Rosetta for sequence design. — [UW thesis record](https://digital.lib.washington.edu/researchworks/items/6dc882fc-ab14-4cdd-8215-36c74d65e21f/full)
- **BindCraft (Pacesa et al., not Baker lab):** a model-centric review reports BindCraft achieving ~49.5% average experimental success across 10 diverse protein targets (reported range ~10–100%), well above classic Baker-lab numbers. BindCraft is AF2-hallucination-based miniprotein binder design. — [Deep-learning protein-design review, arXiv 2502.19173](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.19173)
- **AlphaProteo (Google DeepMind, 2024):** reported wet-lab success rates between ~9% and 88% of tested designs across seven targets; the authors chose "one of the most challenging targets" as an 8th target and **failed to obtain binders** for it. — [AlphaProteo preprint, arXiv 2409.08022](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2409.08022)
- **Conformational-selectivity workflow (GPCR, *Nature* 2026):** because GPCRs are integral membrane proteins and conformationally dynamic, the group built a dedicated high-throughput "receptor-diversion" microscopy screen testing tens of thousands of designs in living human cells with receptors in native membrane, rather than purified-protein display — then confirmed the designed bound conformation by cryo-EM (five structures). — [GEN summary](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/drug-discovery/ai-designs-miniprotein-switches-for-gpcr-targeting/); According to PubMed, PMID 42168559, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10656-8)
- **Conformational-selectivity workflow (IR, *Mol Cell* 2025):** design binders to individual extracellular domains first, then combinatorially fuse (orientation × flexibility) to bias a specific active conformation; validate the induced conformation by cryo-EM and the function by signaling assays. — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.07.617068v1)

### Inferences
- "Success rate" is not comparable across papers without fixing the definition (binds at all vs binds as designed vs functions as designed) and the filtering stringency; for hard, conformation-specific membrane targets the effective pre-screen pass rate is far lower than the headline BindCraft/AlphaProteo numbers measured on easier soluble targets.
- For conformation-specific targets the field's de-risking move is a target-appropriate functional screen (receptor-diversion in cells for GPCRs; domain-wise binders + fusion scan for IR) plus mandatory cryo-EM confirmation that the *designed* conformation was actually induced — not just that a binder was found.

### Gaps
- The *Nature* 2026 GPCR paper's own designs-ordered vs designs-validated counts and per-receptor hit rates were not retrievable (would be in the main text/supplement).
- A single authoritative, apples-to-apples success-rate table across methods for "hard conformation-specific" targets does not exist in the retrieved sources; numbers above come from different definitions and target sets.

## Key Question 4: Cautionary / negative results — in-silico-confident designs that failed in the wet lab, and the stated reasons

### Takeaway
Documented failure modes in-window: (1) outright inability to get binders for the hardest targets even for top methods (AlphaProteo 8th target; Bennett "no binders" for some targets); (2) in-silico metrics (AlphaFold3 ipTM, iPAE) not predicting experimental outcome; (3) unexpected oligomerization / developability failures despite correct-looking models; (4) assay/CRO irreproducibility, so "validated" is itself unreliable. These collectively argue for heavy wet-lab oversampling and orthogonal confirmation.

### Cited Findings
- **Hardest-target failure:** AlphaProteo authors "failed to obtain binders" for a deliberately-chosen most-challenging 8th target, despite 9–88% success on the other seven. — [AlphaProteo, arXiv 2409.08022](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2409.08022)
- **Some targets yield nothing:** Bennett/Coventry 2023 report no binders identified for certain protein targets even with deep-learning filtering. — [Baker lab post](https://www.bakerlab.org/2023/05/08/deep-learning-improves-protein-binder-design-tenfold/)
- **In-silico metrics not tracking experiment + unexpected oligomerization:** a reported RFdiffusion campaign against SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid had all 19 selected proteins soluble and purified, but 13 unexpectedly formed oligomers, and AlphaFold3 ipTM did not align with the experimental data. — reported in [RFdiffusion membrane/binder web search summary]; primary paper not directly fetched in this session (treat as a lead to verify)
- **Experimental false positives dominate at the frontier:** a 2026 framing states de novo binder design is "increasingly limited by experimental false positives: many candidates appear plausible in silico but fail during expression, assay, folding or binding affinity characterization." — [ProtDBench, ICML 2026 poster page](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/62951)
- **Verifier-dependent bias:** structure-prediction models used to filter designs show substantial verifier-dependent bias and limited agreement under identical filtering protocols. — [ProtDBench](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/62951)
- **CRO irreproducibility / developability:** commentary reports the Boltz team found two CROs' results disagreed with each other for the same target+binder sequences; and that many tight binders never advance because they don't express, are immunogenic, or don't perform the intended function (expressability, stability, low polyreactivity, sequence-liability-free, low predicted immunogenicity cited as needed filters). Blog-level source, treat as commentary. — [Tamarind blog](https://www.tamarind.bio/blog/how-validated-are-de-novo-binders-really)

### Inferences
- For a conformation-specific, functional membrane-receptor goal, the dominant risk is not "will a binder appear in silico" but "will the in-silico-confident design (a) express/behave well, (b) actually adopt/induce the intended conformation, and (c) function — none of which current confidence metrics (ipTM/iPAE/pLDDT) reliably predict." This supports large wet-lab oversampling and mandatory orthogonal + structural confirmation rather than trusting in-silico ranking.
- The fact that even AlphaProteo/Bennett fail entirely on the hardest targets implies a non-trivial probability of zero functional hits for a genuinely hard target (e.g., class-B GPCR agonism) within a modest design budget.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed paper found that is specifically a post-mortem of a *failed GPCR agonist* de novo campaign with stated mechanistic reasons.
- The SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid oligomerization/ipTM-mismatch example was surfaced only via a search summary; its primary source was not directly fetched and should be verified before citing in the final report.
- Quantitative failure-rate breakdowns by cause (expression vs misfold vs wrong-conformation vs non-function) were not found as a consolidated table.
