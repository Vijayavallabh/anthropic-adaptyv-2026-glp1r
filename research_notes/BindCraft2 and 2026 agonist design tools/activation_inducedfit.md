# Class B GPCR (GLP1R) activation & induced-fit literature 2024–2026 — design-biasable insight

> FIREWALL (applies to every line below): class-B agonism is UNPROVABLE/UNRANKABLE in-silico. Everything here is framed as a **necessary condition** or **design-biasable prior**, never a predictive agonism metric. "Deeper/engages microswitch" = a structural prerequisite you can bias toward, NOT a score that predicts efficacy.
>
> Design constraints this must respect: natural amino acids only, linear chain, no reliable disulfide, expressed construct with N-terminal (f)Met and C-terminal tag.

## KQ1 — Newest cryo-EM active-state GLP1R / class-B structures (2024–2026): TM6 kink, ECL3, central polar network, ligand-induced change

### Takeaway
The 2024–2026 structural record reframes activation as a **two-step, G-protein-coupled process**: the G protein alone can pull the intracellular TM bundle (incl. TM6) into a near-active geometry even with NO peptide, while the agonist's job is to complete the *extracellular* reorganization — insert the N-terminal head into the TMD orthosteric core and set ECL3/polar-network states that then fix efficacy and bias. TM6-out is necessary but NOT sufficient for signaling.

### Cited Findings
- **Ligand-free GLP-1R/GCGR/GIPR–Gs structures (Cong et al., Cell Discovery 2024):** cryo-EM of all three class-B receptors bound to Gs with NO peptide. The Gs protein by itself widens the intracellular cavity and rewires the extracellular orthosteric pocket; **GLP-1R's extracellular region already sits close to its active conformation without any agonist.** Proposed: Gs mobilizes the intracellular half of the TMD, which rearranges the extracellular half into a transitional shape that lets the peptide N-terminus enter. PDB **8WG7** (GLP-1R, 2.54 Å), **8WA3** (GIPR, 2.86 Å), **8WG8** (GCGR, 2.71 Å). — [PMC10861504 / Cell Discovery](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10861504/); DOI 10.1038/s41421-024-00649-0
- **ECL3 displacement = G-protein-bias switch (Zhao, He, …, H.E. Xu, Cell Reports 2026; PMID 42467532):** cryo-EM GLP1-Y11–GLP1R–Gs at 2.64 Å. **Outward displacement of extracellular loop 3 (ECL3) is the key structural feature associated with G-protein-biased signaling**, distinguishing it from the β-arrestin-biased agonist-bound state. N-terminal modifications (acetylation or aa substitutions, e.g. Ac-semaglutide) produce G-protein bias, attenuated β-arrestin recruitment, prolonged cAMP. According to PubMed — [DOI 10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117718](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117718)
- **Bound-peptide DYNAMICS set signaling (Cary, …, Sexton, Wootten, Gellman, PNAS 2025; PMID 40168114):** backbone-modified GLP-1 analogues (β-amino-acid substitutions, e.g. ACPC18 + Aib16). Cryo-EM of two analogue–GLP-1R–Gs complexes shows "**substantial alterations to bound peptide structure and dynamics**" vs native GLP-1, supporting the "emerging view that **agonist dynamics in the receptor-bound state influence signaling profiles**" (prolonged signaling, altered trafficking). According to PubMed — [DOI 10.1073/pnas.2407574122](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2407574122)
- **TM6 kink (foundational, 2017, 5VAI):** in the peptide–GLP-1R–Gs complex, TM-domain changes produce a sharp kink in the middle of TM6, pivoting its intracellular half outward to accommodate the α5-helix of Gs. — [RCSB 5VAI](https://rcsb.org/structure/5vai); DOI 10.1038/nature22394
- **Central polar network + HETx motif, MD (mwSuMD; Deganutti/Sexton group, eLife reviewed preprint 96513):** simulations track the **central polar network and the TM6 kink converging toward the active-state structure (RMSD to active GLP-1R 7LCI)** during activation. **R190^2.60b is a recurrent communication hub** across systems. A NAM (NNC0640) reinforces interactions WITHIN the central polar network (locks inactive), whereas PAM C-1 or GLP-1+Gs route signaling through TM3/TM5 or TM6/TM7 respectively. — [eLife 96513 figures](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/96513/figures)
- **2025 GLP-1R entries:** PDB **9C0K** exendin-phe1 ("Structural insights into activation of GLP-1R by biased ligands", Zhang 2025); PDB **9J1P** designed disulfide-directed multicyclic peptide agonist (Fan et al., JACS 2025, 2.99 Å). — [RCSB 9C0K](https://swissmodel.expasy.org/smtl/9c0k.1); [RCSB 9J1P](https://swissmodel.expasy.org/smtl/9j1p.1)
- **Review of cryo-EM GPCR principles (Duan, He, Li, H.E. Xu, Nat Rev Endocrinol 2024; PMID 38424377):** >650 GPCR cryo-EM structures by 2024; covers ligand recognition, activation, Gs coupling, arrestin, allosteric-site diversity and biased signaling, using GLP-1R as a worked example. According to PubMed — [DOI 10.1038/s41574-024-00957-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41574-024-00957-1)

### Inferences
- The **design-relevant discriminator for "activating" vs "just binding"** is NOT whether the receptor can reach TM6-out (the G protein helps do that) but whether the ligand **completes the extracellular half**: inserts a head group into the orthosteric core and sets the ECL3 / central-polar-network state. A binder that only grips the ECD or the pocket rim leaves that step undone.
- ECL3 is emerging as a **tunable efficacy/bias element**, not just scenery — a design that perturbs ECL3 outward is biasing toward the G-protein-coupled geometry (necessary-condition framing only).
- Because the bound agonist is a **dynamic ensemble** (Cary 2025), a single static co-fold pose of a designed binder cannot be read as "active" — consistent with the firewall that in-silico agonism is unrankable.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 source explicitly tied the **PxxG motif** to the GLP-1R TM6 kink hinge by residue. The PxxG/TM6 kink link is standard class-B lore but I could not attach a current citation — treat the PxxG-specific mechanism as unverified here.
- 9C0K has no accompanying full paper/mechanistic text I could retrieve (listed "To Be Published").

## KQ2 — Induced fit vs conformational selection; what a ligand must DO; activator vs antagonist at the same site

### Takeaway
Class-B activation is **mixed**: conformational SELECTION at the ECD (open/closed capture of the peptide C-terminus) plus INDUCED FIT at the TMD (the peptide N-terminal head drives the final orthosteric/polar-network rearrangement). The newest MD ("G-protein-first") argues the receptor can be pre-coupled to Gs and TM6-bent-out yet **remain unactivated until the agonist N-terminus arrives** — so the activating act is specifically **deep N-terminal insertion into the TMD core**, and that depth is what separates an activator from an antagonist occupying the same vestibule.

### Cited Findings
- **Two-domain binding model (established):** peptide C-terminal region engages the ECD (capture/affinity); N-terminal region reaches into the TMD orthosteric pocket (activation). — [Nature 2018, nature25153 context](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature25153)
- **G-protein-first mechanism (Li, Yang, Kim, Goddard, JACS Sept 2024):** atomistic simulation argues **GLP-1R is preactivated by pre-coupling to Gs before ligand binding; despite a TM6-bent-out, active-like conformation, the precoupled complex stays UNACTIVATED until an agonist binds to elicit signaling.** The long ECD N-terminus holds the peptide C-terminal half and shifts the peptide's N-terminal head into the orthosteric pocket ("N-terminal-domain-mediated conformational progression"). — [ACS figshare 27011779](https://acs.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/The_G_Protein-First_Mechanism_for_Activation_of_the_Class_B_Glucagon-like_Peptide_1_Receptor_Coupled_to_N_Terminal_Domain-Mediated_Conformational_Progression/27011779/1)
- **Conformational selection at the ECD (glucagon/GCGR, Nat Commun 2015):** open/closed ECD states → "glucagon binds GCGR by a **conformational selection** mechanism." — [ncomms8859](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/ncomms8859)
- **Dual-binding-site trigger / induced fit at TMD (GCGR partial agonist, Nature 2017):** marked change in ECD–TMD relative orientation on binding; the **stalk and ECL1 change secondary structure during peptide binding**; proposed that stalk + ECL1 + TMD must all change conformation for activation. — [Nature nature25153](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature25153)
- **Functional complex is a conformational spectrum (Brown & Gellman, ChemBioChem 2023; PMID 37624685):** GLP-1 Gly22 (central, low helix propensity, no direct receptor contact) tolerates helix-propensity changes → "the functional receptor–agonist complex **may comprise a conformational spectrum rather than a single fixed structure**." According to PubMed — [DOI 10.1002/cbic.202300504](https://doi.org/10.1002/cbic.202300504)
- **Depth as the activator/antagonist discriminator (Baker/Muratspahic, Nature 2026 — see KQ3):** across designed and known ligands, a depth analysis measures the **deepest heavy atom relative to the predicted outer membrane boundary (OPM)**, color-coded by agonist vs antagonist — i.e. how far a ligand reaches into the orthosteric pocket below the membrane surface tracks with function. — [IPD PDF](https://www.ipd.uw.edu/publication-pdfs/432/db1453519cebe452e987a99f60ceb945/De%20novo%20design%20of%20miniproteins%20targeting%20GPCRs.pdf)

### Inferences
- **What the ligand must DO (necessary conditions, design-biasable):** (1) present a pre-organized element that **reaches deep into the TMD orthosteric core (below the membrane boundary)**, not just the ECD or vestibule; (2) displace/engage ECL3 and the central polar network (R190^2.60b hub) toward the active arrangement; (3) tolerate/participate in a **dynamic bound ensemble** rather than needing one rigid pose. An antagonist is predicted to differ chiefly by **shallower penetration** — it fills the vestibule and blocks the native N-terminus without pushing the activation microswitches.
- **Timescale/flexibility:** conformational selection at the ECD implies the design only needs to be complementary to an already-sampled open ECD state; induced fit at the TMD implies the design's inserting element must be flexible/pre-organized enough to complete the last rearrangement. This is why **helix pre-organization** (KQ4) is a lever on both affinity and the induced-fit step.

### Gaps
- No kinetic/single-molecule timescale numbers for GLP-1R peptide binding (on/off rates, induced-fit dwell times) were found — timescale claims are qualitative/MD-derived only.
- "G-protein-first" (pre-coupling before ligand) is a single-group MD model and is in tension with simpler ligand-first schemes; not experimentally settled. Flag as contested.

## KQ3 — Designing conformation-inducing / state-selective ligands/proteins for GPCRs (2024–2026): crossing binding → activation

### Takeaway
The landmark 2025–2026 result (Baker lab) shows de novo miniprotein **binding to GPCRs is solved, but de novo AGONISM was achieved only for class A** — **every class-B peptide receptor they targeted (GLP1R, GIPR, GCGR, CGRPR, PTH1R) yielded ANTAGONISTS only.** Current thinking: agonism correlates with how deep the binder penetrates the orthosteric pocket; inducing the class-B active state de novo remains an open frontier. This is the single most important firewall-respecting datapoint for this project.

### Cited Findings
- **De novo miniproteins targeting GPCRs (Muratspahić, Norn, …, Baker, Nature 2026; bioRxiv 2025.03.23.644666):** computational de novo design + a microscopy "receptor diversion" high-throughput screen. **Agonists were obtained for class A receptor(s) — MRGPRX1 (confirmed); one summary also lists NK1R and CCR5. Antagonists were obtained for CXCR4, OXTR, CCR5, and the class-B peptide receptors GLP1R, GIPR, GCGR, CGRPR (and PTH1R).** Cryo-EM showed atomic-level agreement for a CGRPR-bound antagonist and an MRGPRX1-bound agonist ("precise conformational control"). Designs can **penetrate deep into binding pockets below the membrane surface** while staying selective; a depth analysis (deepest heavy atom vs OPM boundary) distinguishes agonist from antagonist reach. Spun out as Skape Bio. — [bioRxiv PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.23.644666.full.pdf); [IPD full text](https://www.ipd.uw.edu/publication-pdfs/432/db1453519cebe452e987a99f60ceb945/De%20novo%20design%20of%20miniproteins%20targeting%20GPCRs.pdf); DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10656-8; PDB 9MNI
- **AI de novo GLP-1RAs (Wei et al., Adv Sci 2025; PMID 40787887):** deep-learning design + functional screen; 10,000 de novo GLP-1RAs designed, 60 passed a virtual stability/efficacy/diversity screen, **52% in-vitro success**; leads D13/D41 have ~3× longer half-life than semaglutide and D13 matches semaglutide on glucose/weight in mice. According to PubMed — [DOI 10.1002/advs.202507044](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202507044). CAVEAT: these are GLP-1 **backbone analogues** (sequence redesign of the GLP-1 scaffold), so they inherit the native activation pharmacophore — NOT a novel topology that had to re-discover how to activate.
- **RXFP1 agonist designs (Lorne Proteins 2026 poster — NOT peer-reviewed):** 66% of agonist binder designs bound target in BLI; two binders reported nanomolar agonist activity; authors claim the pipeline "can selectively stabilize active conformational states." RXFP1 is a class-A LGR, not class B. — [Lorne 2026 abstract](https://proteins-2026.p.asnevents.com.au/days/2026-02-09/abstract/132687)
- **AlloGen (preprint):** computational design of state-selective binders, motivated by GPCR functional directionality, but **validated on calmodulin, not any GPCR.** — surfaced via [search listing]; primary validation not GPCR.

### Inferences
- **For a de novo GLP1R ACTIVATOR specifically, there is no published precedent of success** — the one authoritative attempt (Baker) got antagonists for GLP1R and all class-B siblings. Crossing binding→class-B activation is unsolved, which is exactly why in-silico agonism must stay unranked here. Bias the design toward the *necessary conditions* (deep TMD-core insertion of a pre-organized helical head mimicking the GLP-1 N-terminus), while treating high-affinity surface/ECD binding as the EASY, antagonist-prone outcome to avoid.
- The Wei et al. "success" is real but says nothing about de-novo-discovering activation — it keeps GLP-1's pharmacophore. Don't over-read it as "de novo agonism is easy."

### Gaps
- Minor discrepancy between two search summaries on whether NK1R/CCR5 (besides MRGPRX1) were agonists in the Baker paper; both agree **all class-B targets were antagonists only**. The full PDF (>10 MB) could not be machine-read to resolve the exact class-A agonist list.
- No published de novo class-B (GLP1R family) agonist of any modality (miniprotein or peptide-from-scratch) was found.

## KQ4 — Newest minimal / non-GLP-1 / engineered peptide activators of GLP1R and their mechanism

### Takeaway
The activation pharmacophore compresses to a **short N-terminal element (~8–11 residues)**, and **pre-organizing it as an α-helix is the dominant potency lever** (up to ~1000-fold). But the best minimal activators rely on **non-natural residues (Aib, Bip) and/or disulfide cyclization** — both outside this project's constraints. The transferable, natural-AA principle: a pre-organized N-terminal helix that inserts deep into the TMD core.

### Cited Findings
- **Ultra-short 11-mer GLP-1R agonists (Sawyer et al., Molecules 2024; Resolute Bio / Pentelute lab):** parent RXL-100 = H-**Aib-Glu-Gly-Thr-Phe-Thr-Ser-Asp-Bip-Bip**-NH2 (11 residues; peptide His1 ≈ His7 of GLP-1; Bip replaces Val16/Ser17-region, Aib replaces Ala8). **α-methylation biases toward the α-helix and raises potency up to ~1000-fold**; RXL-3000 EC50 0.1 nM, α-methyl analog RXL-101 EC50 0.01 nM. Proposed mechanism: α-methylation **stabilizes the peptide's α-helical binding conformation in the UNBOUND state** (entropic pre-organization). Ala-scan: substituting **Gly10, Phe12, Thr13 cost >100-fold** (essential), whereas **His7 is non-essential** here (Aib replacement <10-fold weaker). Modeling places the two C-terminal Bip side chains between TM1/TM2 and near ECL2 — i.e. these ultra-short peptides **bind GLP-1R differently from full-length GLP-1.** — [PMC11721672](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11721672/)
- **Disulfide-directed multicyclic peptide agonist (Fan et al., JACS 2025; PDB 9J1P, 2.99 Å):** a designed multicyclic peptide activates GLP-1R–Gs. — [RCSB 9J1P](https://swissmodel.expasy.org/smtl/9j1p.1)
- **Biased exendin-phe1 (PDB 9C0K, Zhang 2025):** N-terminal modification of the exendin scaffold tunes signaling bias at GLP-1R. — [RCSB 9C0K](https://swissmodel.expasy.org/smtl/9c0k.1)
- **Fragment precedent (older, via 2024 search context):** GLP-1(9–36) and GLP-1(15–36) retain partial agonism; an 11-residue N-terminal GLP-1 analogue cyclized to give nanomolar agonists (2015) — establishing the N-terminal region as the activation determinant. — [search listing, Molecules 2024 intro](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11721672/)

### Inferences
- **Direct design levers compatible with our constraints (natural AA, linear, no disulfide):** (1) base the activator on the **GLP-1 N-terminal activation element** (~His7…Glu9-Gly10-Thr11-Phe12-Thr13-Ser14-Asp15, the residues the 11-mer SAR flags as load-bearing: Gly10/Phe12/Thr13); (2) **pre-organize it as an α-helix using natural means** — i,i+4 Glu↔Lys/Arg salt bridges, Ala-rich stretches, N-/C-cap motifs — since helix pre-organization (not an unnatural α-methyl) is the mechanistic driver of the 1000-fold gain; (3) ensure the N-terminal head can reach **deep into the TMD orthosteric core**, the activator/antagonist discriminator from KQ2–3.
- **Constraint collisions to flag:** the strongest published minimal activators need Aib/Bip (non-natural) or disulfide macrocyclization — neither is available to us, so expect a potency/efficacy penalty vs those benchmarks. The **N-terminal (f)Met + C-terminal tag** of an expressed construct is a specific risk: the native activating N-terminus (His7) sits deep and makes precise polar contacts; a retained fMet or N-terminal extension could block deep insertion. Biasing toward a correctly shaped, unobstructed N-terminal helical head is a necessary condition. (His7 being *non-essential* in the 11-mer SAR is a hopeful note — the head identity may be more forgiving than the dogma suggests, provided helicity and depth are preserved.)

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 paper on an **all-natural-AA, linear, disulfide-free minimal GLP1R activator** was found — the minimal-activator field leans on unnatural residues / cyclization, so our constraint set is in relatively uncharted territory (useful signal for the report: the obvious potency tricks are off-limits).
- EMAX / full-vs-partial-agonist classification for the RXL ultra-short series was in tables not captured in the retrieved excerpt — efficacy (not just potency) of the minimal 11-mers is unconfirmed here.
