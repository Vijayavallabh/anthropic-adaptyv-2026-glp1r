# Enhanced-sampling MD for GPCR conformational landscapes / the active–inactive transition: feasibility, compute cost, and reliability as a filtering/ranking tool (2020–2026)

> Scope note: methodology and compute-feasibility only. This file does NOT discuss how to design an activating ligand. It evaluates whether biased/enhanced-sampling MD can act as a *necessary-condition filter* ("does a bound ligand permit or destabilize the active-state conformation?") on a ~4-GPU budget, and how trustworthy that readout is. Several key primary papers (ACS/Wiley) are paywalled; where a number could not be extracted from an accessible source it is flagged as a Gap. Articles surfaced via the PubMed MCP are cited with their DOI.

---

## KQ1: Which enhanced-sampling methods are used for GPCR activation; CVs, simulation length, hardware, wall-clock cost

### Takeaway
The field uses a small, stable toolkit: **well-tempered / multiple-walker / path-CV metadynamics**, **string method with swarms-of-trajectories**, **adaptive sampling + Markov State Models (MSMs)**, plus umbrella sampling and steered MD as supporting techniques. For a *per-ligand activation free-energy landscape*, the convergent cost anchor across independent groups is **~2–5 µs of aggregate biased MD per landscape** (metadynamics or string). Unbiased adaptive-sampling+MSM is far more expensive (**~240 µs to ~2 ms aggregate**) and is cloud/supercomputer-scale, not 4-GPU-scale.

### Cited Findings
- A 2025 review (kappa-opioid receptor) lists the standard enhanced-sampling methods used to probe the GPCR "activation landscape": **umbrella sampling, metadynamics, and Markov State Models**, explicitly to "evaluate the convergence of free energy surfaces along coordinates of functional interest." — Tong et al., Chem Biol Drug Des 2025, [DOI 10.1111/cbdd.70181](https://doi.org/10.1111/cbdd.70181)
- **Collective variables (CVs) actually used for the activation transition:**
  - **TM3–TM6 Cα distance** (ionic-lock / DRY-motif opening) and **NPxxY(A) motif RMSD** vs the inactive structure — used as the 2D activation landscape for µ-opioid receptor (MOR). Specifically the R165(3.50)–T279(6.34) Cα distance. — Meral, Provasi & Filizola, J Chem Phys 2018, [PMC6291190](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6291190)
  - **A "A100" activation index** = a single CV built from **five inter-helix distances**, described as "almost universal" across class A GPCRs; used as the lone CV in well-tempered multiple-walker metadynamics. — Calderón Reyes, Ibrahim, Gobbo, Gervasio, Clark (5-HT1A), JCIM 2023, [DOI 10.1021/acs.jcim.3c00208](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.3c00208); β2AR follow-up JCIM 2024, [DOI 10.1021/acs.jcim.3c01763](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.3c01763)
  - **Five machine-learned inter-helix distances** (TM2–TM7, TM6–TM4, TM7–TM4, TM3–TM6, TM6–TM5), selected by training a neural network on a deactivation trajectory + deep Taylor decomposition; the activation progress was tracked through microswitches: binding-site contraction (Ser5.46–Gly7.41), **connector region Ile3.40/Phe6.44 RMSD**, **Asp2.50 cavity hydration**, **NPxxY RMSD**, and **TM6 displacement (Leu6.34–Arg3.50)**. — Fleetwood et al. (β2AR string method), bioRxiv 627026, [full text](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/627026v1.full-text) (published eLife 2021; see KQ2)
  - **Path collective variables** between inactive and active crystal endpoints (e.g., 2RH1→3P0G for β2AR; rhodopsin→opsin). The rhodopsin→opsin path-CV metadynamics resolved ≥4 metastable states distinguished by the amplitude of **TM6 outward movement**. — multiple (Provasi/Filizola lineage), summarized in WebSearch results
  - The canonical activation signature is a **~1.1 nm (11 Å) outward movement of TM6** on the intracellular side. — Fleetwood et al. preprint discussion, [bioRxiv 627026](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/627026.full.pdf)
  - A simpler surrogate: the **TM3–TM6 "ionic-lock" distance** has been used directly as an activation readout to compare ligands (sweet-taste receptor TAS1R2/1R3). — Jang, Kim, Goddard, J Agric Food Chem 2021, [DOI 10.1021/acs.jafc.1c03779](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jafc.1c03779)
- **Simulation length / aggregate cost per method:**
  - **Path-CV well-tempered metadynamics (MOR):** two runs × 1.5 µs = **3 µs total per system**, bias factor 12, Gaussian deposition every 5 ps. — Meral et al. 2018, [PMC6291190](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6291190)
  - **String-with-swarms (β2AR):** "Derivation of one free energy landscape requires a total of ~2–3 µs simulation time"; 20–43 string points, 16–32 swarm trajectories per point, **each swarm trajectory only 10 ps**, 300 iterations per system. — Fleetwood et al., [bioRxiv 627026 full text](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/627026v1.full-text)
  - **Metadynamics for GPCR–ligand binding** (a related but distinct task): a published protocol "requires **3–5 µs average simulation time to converge one GPCR–ligand binding free-energy profile**." — Clark/Gervasio group protocol, [JCIM protocol AAM PDF](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1559344/1/Protocol_JCIM_final.pdf)
  - **Adaptive sampling + MSM (β2AR, Kohlhoff et al. 2014):** "over **2 ms of aggregate** MD … using tens of thousands of individual MD trajectories, each with a mean length of ~10 ns," run on Google Exacycle cloud. No single trajectory crossed the full activation path; the MSM stitched short runs. — [Kohlhoff et al. 2014 Nature Chemistry, Google Research summary](https://research.google/pubs/cloud-based-simulations-on-google-exacycle-reveal-ligand-modulation-of-gpcr-activation-pathways/)
  - **Adaptive sampling + MSM (MOR, HTMD):** the reference unbiased dataset was **~240 µs aggregate**. — as cited by Meral et al. 2018, [PMC6291190](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6291190)
  - **Weighted ensemble (WESTPA 2.0):** a rigorous-kinetics rare-event sampler for ">µs" processes; demonstrated on the Abl1 kinase active↔inactive transition (not yet a published GPCR activation study found here). — [WESTPA 2.0, bioRxiv 2021](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.12.05.471280.full.pdf)
- **Hybrid metadynamics→MSM is the current "generally applicable" recipe:** a 2025 protocol combines metadynamics + MSM to compute the full activation free-energy landscape of a class A GPCR (GHSR-1a) in apo and ligand-bound states, finding multiple metastable states whose energies/transition probabilities shift with the bound ligand; the authors pitch it as applicable to "all class A GPCRs." — Roessner, Floquet, Louet, JCIM 2025, [DOI 10.1021/acs.jcim.5c00202](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.5c00202)
- **Hardware / throughput anchors (GROMACS, membrane protein):** on a single **NVIDIA A100**, an 80,289-atom "protein-in-membrane + explicit water" benchmark ran at **~241 ns/day (GROMACS 2021)** and **~266 ns/day (GROMACS 2024.1)**, with all calculations offloaded to the GPU. — [FAU HPC benchmarks 2022](https://hpc.fau.de/2022/02/10/gromacs-performance-on-different-gpu-types/); [FAU 2024](https://hpc.fau.de/2024/08/13/gromacs-2024-1-on-brand-new-gpgpus/)
- The MOR activation system was **~54,000 atoms** (POPC + 10% cholesterol, 80×80 Å² patch, ~150 mM NaCl). — Meral et al. 2018, [PMC6291190](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6291190)

### Inferences
- A GPCR-in-POPC + water + ions system is typically **~50k–160k atoms**; at FAU's measured ~241–266 ns/day (A100, ~80k atoms), a smaller ~54k-atom system runs faster (~300–400 ns/day) and a larger ~120–160k-atom system slower (~120–180 ns/day per A100). Metadynamics/string bias adds only modest overhead (PLUMED), so **single-GPU throughput ~150–350 ns/day** is a reasonable planning range.
- **Per-ligand activation landscape cost is remarkably consistent (~2–5 µs aggregate)** across three independent CV-based methods (path-CV metadynamics 3 µs; string-with-swarms 2–3 µs; binding metadynamics 3–5 µs). This is the number to plan around — NOT the ~240 µs–2 ms adaptive-sampling figures, which reflect unbiased/cloud approaches and are out of reach on 4 GPUs.
- Methods cleanly split by whether they also give **kinetics**: metadynamics/string give thermodynamics (free-energy landscape) only; adaptive-sampling MSM and weighted ensemble give kinetics/rates. For a *necessary-condition thermodynamic filter* ("is the active state accessible/stable with this ligand?"), the cheaper thermodynamic methods suffice.

### Gaps
- Exact walker count and per-walker length for the Calderón/Clark **A100-index well-tempered multiple-walker** β2AR/5-HT1A runs, and for the Roessner 2025 GHSR-1a protocol, could not be extracted (both papers paywalled; UCL/ACS 403). The ~2–5 µs/landscape anchor from Meral/Fleetwood/Clark-binding is the best available proxy.
- No peer-reviewed **weighted-ensemble GPCR activation** study was found (only the method, WESTPA 2.0, and a kinase application). Whether WE has been applied to a GPCR activation transition is an open gap.

## KQ2: Reproducibility / reliability for RANKING ligands by their effect on the active-state free energy

### Takeaway
Enhanced-sampling MD can **qualitatively classify** ligands by whether they stabilize the active vs inactive state (demonstrated for agonists vs inverse agonists on β2AR and MOR), but **convergence assessment remains "an open problem,"** small-sample/short-trajectory metrics (pulling work, few trajectories) are demonstrably noisy, and results are sensitive to the starting structure and to the CV choice. Treat it as a **direction/compatibility classifier with controls, not a quantitative ΔΔG ranker**, and only after explicit convergence checks.

### Cited Findings
- **It can separate ligand classes (the encouraging evidence):**
  - Adaptive-sampling MSMs showed "agonists and inverse agonists interact differentially with these [activation] pathways." — [Kohlhoff et al. 2014](https://research.google/pubs/cloud-based-simulations-on-google-exacycle-reveal-ligand-modulation-of-gpcr-activation-pathways/)
  - Well-tempered multiple-walker metadynamics: **inverse agonists (carazolol, ICI-118551) stabilize a single inactive minimum** in both binary (receptor–ligand) and ternary (receptor–ligand–G-protein) complexes, consistent with their pharmacology; neutral antagonists behaved more ambiguously (also affecting G-protein recruitment). — Calderón Reyes et al., JCIM 2024, [DOI 10.1021/acs.jcim.3c01763](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.3c01763); [open AM record](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10197737)
  - Metadynamics ΔG along a TM3–TM6 activation coordinate distinguished **synergistic vs suppressive** sweetener pairs (more-negative ΔG for synergistic combos, less-negative for a known suppressive pair). — Jang et al. 2021, [DOI 10.1021/acs.jafc.1c03779](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jafc.1c03779)
  - Metadynamics+MSM found "energies and transition probabilities change depending on the bound ligand," distinguishing basal vs agonist-induced activation of GHSR-1a. — Roessner et al. 2025, [DOI 10.1021/acs.jcim.5c00202](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.5c00202)
- **Convergence / reliability limits (the cautionary evidence):**
  - Convergence assessment for combining biased simulations is described as **"still an open problem"** in a 2024 methods preprint proposing a new convergence metric via Mean Force Integration. — [ChemRxiv 2024](https://client.prod.orp.cambridge.org/engage/chemrxiv/article-details/6625a37791aefa6ce10d4b04)
  - **Ordinary metadynamics only fluctuates around the correct free energy** (error tied to bias deposition rate); **well-tempered metadynamics** converges to the exact free energy in the long-time limit and makes error controllable — i.e., use well-tempered, not standard, for any ranking. — Barducci, Bussi, Parrinello, PRL 2008, [APS](https://archive.aps.org/mar/2008/y13/2)
  - **String method converges only to the path nearest the initial guess, not necessarily the globally most probable path**; eLife reviewers of the β2AR string study explicitly demanded statistical-reliability/error estimates and **"at least one demonstration that reliable results emerge with a very different initial structure."** — [Fleetwood et al. preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/627026v1.full-text); [eLife 60715 peer reviews](https://elifesciences.org/articles/60715/peer-reviews)
  - **Steered-MD / pulling-work is fragile:** the Jarzynski estimator has high variance because the dominant work values are rare/low-probability; "reliable work distributions are not always produced in a finite number of simulations." — [Enhanced-sampling review, arXiv 2202.04164](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.04164)
  - For decaalanine, **~10 trajectories** plus a **second-order cumulant expansion** were needed for the most accurate nonequilibrium estimate; faster pulling needs the cumulant correction to avoid bias from wide work distributions. — [NAMD/decaalanine SMD tutorial paper](https://tcbg.illinois.edu/Training/Tutorials/science/10Ala-tutorial/files/paper.pdf)
  - Counterpoint (pulling work *can* rank, on curated sets): a 2020 study of 23 compounds across six receptors reported **Jarzynski ΔG_neq correlates with experiment at R ≈ 0.80–0.89** and recommended it for ranking binding affinities. — [Figshare collection](https://acs.figshare.com/collections/Probing_the_Binding_Affinity_by_Jarzynski_s_Nonequilibrium_Binding_Free_Energy_and_Rupture_Time/4076297). Caveat flagged by the search itself: these are small, curated benchmark sets; robustness across other targets is not shown.
  - Standard practice for metadynamics error is **block analysis** (error rises with block length then plateaus = converged) and tracking ΔG drift over the late part of the run. — [NRAS metadynamics example via WebSearch]; concrete threshold used for MOR activation: **free-energy-difference standard deviation < 20 kJ/mol over the last 150 ns**. — Meral et al. 2018, [PMC6291190](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6291190)
- **Reliability-critical input dependence:** protonation of the conserved **Asp2.50 (Asp79 in β2AR)** materially changes the landscape — protonation "shifts the NPxxY and cavity landscapes toward a single active-like well and stabilizes TM6 in more active-like conformations." A wrong protonation choice flips the qualitative answer. — [Fleetwood et al. full text](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/627026v1.full-text)
- **Cross-method validation that gives confidence when it holds:** path-CV metadynamics (3 µs) reproduced the HTMD adaptive-sampling (~240 µs) free-energy landscape for MOR *except* the high-energy intermediate region; metastable-state populations agreed well, and MaxCal-derived mean first-passage times were within ~2× of the adaptive-sampling values. The authors call well-tempered metadynamics with path CVs "a fast and reliable method for studying the thermodynamic properties of GPCR activation" at ~2 orders of magnitude less cost. — Meral et al. 2018, [PMC6291190](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6291190)

### Inferences
- The literature supports using enhanced-sampling MD as a **binary/ordinal "active-state compatibility" classifier** (does the ligand allow/stabilize vs destabilize the active well?) far more than as a **numeric ΔΔG ligand ranker**. The agonist/inverse-agonist separations are qualitative; nobody shows robust numeric rank-ordering of many similar agonists by active-state free energy on an affordable budget.
- Reliability is dominated by **three controllable choices**: (1) well-tempered (not standard) metadynamics; (2) a validated activation CV (A100 index, path CVs, or the TM3–TM6 + NPxxY pair) rather than a single hand-picked distance; (3) correct Asp2.50/His protonation. Getting these wrong is a more likely failure mode than raw sampling.
- Because methods converge only to the **nearest** basin/path and depend on the starting structure, a credible filter must run **matched protocols from the same prepared system** for test ligand vs controls, and ideally seed from **both** active and inactive endpoints to check path/landscape independence.

### Gaps
- No study found that reports **run-to-run reproducibility statistics (replica spread / CI) for a GPCR activation free-energy difference used to rank ligands** — i.e., there is no published "how many independent replicas to call a ΔΔG_active significant" for this specific task. This is the central missing reliability number for a ranking application.

## KQ3: Minimum credible protocol with positive/negative controls (known agonist vs antagonist) and cost on ~4 GPUs

### Takeaway
A minimum credible attempt = **well-tempered (multiple-walker) metadynamics or string-with-swarms along a validated activation CV, run identically for apo + a known agonist (positive control, should stabilize/permit the active well) + a known inverse agonist/antagonist (negative control, should stabilize the inactive well) + the test ligand**, with explicit convergence checks and correct Asp2.50 protonation. Budget **~2–5 µs aggregate per system**; on ~4 A100-class GPUs this is roughly **3–7 days wall-clock per ligand**, so a 3–4 system control+test panel is **~2–4 weeks**.

### Cited Findings
- The published protocols that this minimum is built from:
  - Path-CV well-tempered metadynamics, **3 µs/system**, CVs = TM3–TM6 distance + NPxxY RMSD, convergence = ΔG std < 20 kJ/mol over last 150 ns; validated against a ~240 µs adaptive-sampling landscape. — Meral et al. 2018, [PMC6291190](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6291190)
  - A100-index **well-tempered multiple-walker** metadynamics with a preliminary single-walker run used to *select walkers* for production — an explicit, reusable "general metadynamics protocol to simulate activation." — [Clark/Gervasio group AAM PDF](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10170849/7/Gervasio_General%20Metadynamics%20Protocol%20To%20Simulate%20Activation_AAM.pdf); [UCL record](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10170849/)
  - String-with-swarms, **2–3 µs/landscape**, with convergence demonstrated by string diffusing around a stationary equilibrium position and uncertainty from MCMC over the transition-matrix posterior. — [Fleetwood et al.](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/627026v1.full-text)
  - Generally applicable metadynamics+MSM recipe for class A GPCRs with apo vs ligand-bound comparison. — Roessner et al. 2025, [DOI 10.1021/acs.jcim.5c00202](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.5c00202)
- **Built-in controls already in the literature** (what "positive/negative control" looks like in practice): agonist vs inverse-agonist differential pathway modulation (Kohlhoff); inverse agonists collapsing to a single inactive minimum (Calderón 2024); basal vs agonist-induced landscapes (Roessner 2025). These establish that a **known agonist should shift population/stability toward the active (TM6-out, NPxxY-active) well and a known inverse agonist toward the inactive well** — the exact readout a filter would use. — refs above
- Single-A100 membrane-protein throughput **~241–266 ns/day (~80k atoms, GROMACS)**. — [FAU HPC](https://hpc.fau.de/2022/02/10/gromacs-performance-on-different-gpu-types/)
- Multiple-walker metadynamics and swarms are **embarrassingly parallel** across walkers/trajectories (one walker per GPU), and WESTPA likewise parallelizes the ensemble — i.e., 4 GPUs = 4 concurrent walkers. — [Clark AAM](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10170849/7/Gervasio_General%20Metadynamics%20Protocol%20To%20Simulate%20Activation_AAM.pdf); [WESTPA 2.0](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.12.05.471280.full.pdf)

### Inferences (compute budgeting on ~4 GPUs — explicitly derived, not directly cited)
- At ~150–300 ns/day per GPU for a ~80–120k-atom GPCR/POPC system, **4 GPUs running 4 walkers ≈ 0.6–1.2 µs/day aggregate**. A 2–3 µs landscape → **~2–5 days wall-clock**; a 3–5 µs landscape → **~3–8 days**. This treats the 4 GPUs as 4 parallel walkers (the natural multiple-walker layout), which is more efficient than multi-GPU domain decomposition of one replica.
- A **minimum control+test panel = apo + known agonist + known inverse agonist + 1 test ligand (4 systems)** → **~1.5–4 weeks** total wall-clock on 4 GPUs (serial over systems), or less if systems are time-shared. Adding replicas for reliability (recommended, given KQ2) multiplies this.
- Adaptive-sampling+MSM at 240 µs–2 ms aggregate is **~100–1000× more expensive** than the metadynamics/string route and is **not feasible on 4 GPUs** in any reasonable window — it belongs to cloud/Folding@home-scale efforts. For a 4-GPU filter, **metadynamics or string is the only viable family.**
- Because the controls have *known* answers, they double as a **fail-loud calibration**: if the known agonist does not stabilize the active well relative to the known inverse agonist under your protocol, the protocol (CV, force field, protonation, or sampling) is not trustworthy for the test ligand — a clean go/no-go gate before interpreting any test result.

### Gaps
- No source gives a turnkey "4-GPU, N-day, this-many-replicas" recipe for GPCR activation ranking; the wall-clock figures above are inferred from per-landscape µs cost × measured GROMACS A100 throughput, not quoted. Real throughput depends on system size, PLUMED overhead, and GPU model (H100 would be ~1.5–2× faster than A100).

## KQ4: Practical software stack, membrane setup, and common pitfalls that invalidate results

### Takeaway
The dominant stack is **GROMACS + PLUMED** (metadynamics, string, umbrella) with **CHARMM-GUI Membrane Builder** for setup, **CHARMM36m + TIP3P + POPC (± cholesterol) + ~0.15 M NaCl at 310 K**; OpenMM (+ openmm-plumed, + WESTPA for weighted ensemble) and Amber are the main alternatives. The result-invalidating pitfalls are concentrated in **setup**: wrong protonation (especially Asp2.50), poor bilayer equilibration, force-field/lipid choice, and ligand parameterization.

### Cited Findings
- **Software actually used in GPCR activation studies:**
  - **GROMACS + PLUMED**: GROMACS 2016.5 + PLUMED 2.4.1 (string/β2AR); GROMACS 5.1.4 + PLUMED 2.1 + PyEMMA 2.4 (MOR metadynamics→MSM). — [Fleetwood et al.](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/627026v1.full-text); [Meral et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6291190)
  - **WESTPA** (weighted ensemble) runs on **AMBER, GROMACS, and OpenMM** engines. — [WESTPA 2.0](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.12.05.471280.full.pdf); [LiveCoMS WESTPA tutorials](https://livecomsjournal.org/index.php/livecoms/article/download/v1i2e10607/955/2745)
  - **MSM analysis**: PyEMMA (Meral et al.); adaptive sampling historically via HTMD / Folding@home / Exacycle. — refs above
- **Membrane setup requirements:**
  - **CHARMM-GUI Membrane Builder** is the standard: it embeds the GPCR in a POPC bilayer and also performs protein protonation and topology/coordinate generation (even for AMBER). — [M2 muscarinic CHARMM-GUI workflow, arXiv 2006.11066](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.11066); [CHARMM-GUI 10-years review](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/charmm-gui-10-years-for-biomolecular-modeling-and-simulation/)
  - Typical membrane composition in activation studies: **POPC, or POPC + 10% cholesterol**, patch ~80×80 Å², ~150 mM NaCl, NPT at 300–310 K. — [Meral et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6291190)
  - Ligand parameters come from **CGenFF** (CHARMM) or equivalent; the bound ligand's own protonation state must be set explicitly (e.g., iperoxo modeled at +1e). — [arXiv 2006.11066](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.11066); [CHARMM-GUI tutorial, RIKEN](https://www.r-ccs.riken.jp/labs/cbrt/?p=18919)
- **Pitfalls that invalidate results:**
  - **Protonation of Asp2.50** (and His residues) changes the activation landscape qualitatively (see KQ2). β2AR runs protonated Glu3.41 and set His4.64/His4.70 to the epsilon tautomer, and ran Asp2.50 in *both* ionized and neutral forms as separate strings precisely because it matters. — [Fleetwood et al.](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/627026v1.full-text)
  - **Bilayer equilibration is the hard part**: "the setup of a bilayer system is difficult compared to solvation of globular proteins. If the system starts … far from equilibrium … it must take much time to equilibrate." Under-equilibrated membranes contaminate downstream free energies. — [CHARMM-GUI Membrane Builder tutorial](https://www.r-ccs.riken.jp/labs/cbrt/?p=18919)
  - **Force-field / lipid-model choice** is a known source of divergence; CHARMM coverage is broad (complemented by CHARMM-GUI), and OPLS-AA/M has GPCR extensions (palmitoylation/myristoylation) that "compare favorably to … CHARMM36m," implying non-trivial force-field sensitivity. — [bioRxiv force-field preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.01.05.475148v1.full.pdf)
  - A dedicated review concludes GPCR–lipid MD methodologies are **"still emerging"** and complex-membrane assembly **"remains challenging."** — [Exploring GPCR lipid interactions review](https://library.ncl.res.in/node/44474)
  - Protonation-state assignment tools in common use: **CHARMM-GUI, Amber LEAP, H++** (and pKa predictors). — [AIChE system-creation workshop abstract via WebSearch]
- **Method hygiene for the biased run itself** (from KQ2): use **well-tempered** (not standard) metadynamics; verify convergence by **block analysis + late-run ΔG drift** (e.g., ΔG std < 20 kJ/mol over last 150 ns); check **starting-structure independence** (seed both endpoints); remember **standard metadynamics cannot give kinetic rates** (bias accelerates transitions) — MaxCal or an MSM is required if rates are wanted.

### Inferences
- The **highest-leverage reliability investment is setup, not sampling**: a correct, well-equilibrated membrane system with validated protonation (especially Asp2.50) and good ligand parameters determines whether the agonist/antagonist controls even separate. Budget real time for CHARMM-GUI setup + multi-stage membrane equilibration before any production biased run.
- For a 4-GPU filter, **GROMACS+PLUMED** is the most proven path (every GPCR activation metadynamics/string study above uses it); **OpenMM+openmm-plumed** is a viable alternative and is the natural engine if weighted ensemble (WESTPA) is chosen.

### Gaps
- No single source here provides a consolidated, GPCR-specific **protonation checklist** (per-residue pKa guidance at the orthosteric site and activation pathway); the Asp2.50 sensitivity is documented, but a systematic per-receptor list was not found.
- Head-to-head force-field benchmarks specifically for the *activation free-energy difference* (CHARMM36m vs OPLS-AA/M vs Amber lipid17) were not located; force-field sensitivity is asserted qualitatively, not quantified for this readout.

## Bottom-line synthesis for the go/no-go (methodology only)
- **Feasible on ~4 GPUs?** Yes, but only via the **thermodynamic metadynamics / string family (~2–5 µs per landscape, ~3–8 days wall-clock/system)**. Adaptive-sampling+MSM (~240 µs–2 ms) and most kinetics-grade approaches are **not** 4-GPU-feasible.
- **Reliable as a RANKER of ligands by active-state free energy?** Weakly. Published successes are **qualitative class separations** (agonist stabilizes active well; inverse agonist stabilizes inactive well), achieved *with controls*. Quantitative ΔΔG rank-ordering of similar ligands on an affordable budget is **not demonstrated**, convergence is an acknowledged open problem, pulling-work/short-trajectory metrics are explicitly noise-prone at small n, and results depend on starting structure, CV, and protonation.
- **Reliable as a NECESSARY-CONDITION FILTER?** This is its best-supported use: **"does the bound ligand permit/stabilize the active (TM6-out, NPxxY-active) well, or does it collapse to the inactive well?"** — run identically for apo + known agonist (positive control) + known inverse agonist (negative control) + test ligand, with well-tempered metadynamics along a validated activation CV, correct Asp2.50 protonation, demonstrated convergence, and the controls serving as a fail-loud calibration gate. Interpret as an ordinal/binary compatibility call, not a precise energy.
