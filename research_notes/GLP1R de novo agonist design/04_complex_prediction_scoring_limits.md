## Key Question 1 — Do co-folders (AF3, Boltz-1/2, Chai-1, Protenix) model the 7TM peptide insertion and the ACTIVE state, represent membrane, and place a designed binder reliably?

### Takeaway
All current co-folders default toward the INACTIVE GPCR conformation even when an agonist/peptide is present, model NO membrane/lipid/detergent, and place peptide/protein ligands markedly worse than small molecules; they can sometimes seat a ligand in the pocket but do not reliably reproduce the active-state fold or reliably rank designed binders.

### Cited Findings
- AF2/AF3 are more accurate for GPCRs in the **inactive** conformation; AF2 inactive median TM-RMSD **1.33 Å** vs active **1.86 Å**, attributed partly to training imbalance (**178 inactive vs 60 active** experimental GPCR structures), with the bias persisting after accounting for imbalance and depending on GPCR class — [Heo & Feig, Proteins / bioRxiv 2021.11.26.470086](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.11.26.470086v1.full); [arXiv:2502.17628 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.17628)
- 2026 state-specific benchmark of **AF3, Boltz-2, Chai-1, RF3**: active-state receptors were "pulled toward inactive-like geometry"; models reproduce inactive-like ICL3 "almost perfectly" while active/biased receptors gave inactive-like or intermediate ICL3; AF3 was most reliable, **Chai-1 least dependable for GPCR ligand placement**, Boltz-2 variable (best in 2 cases, worst in 2). Biased MSAs/templates improved local placement but "do not reliably enforce correct conformations." (Benchmark excludes Boltz-1 and Protenix, and excludes peptides.) — [bioRxiv 2026.05.06.723350](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.06.723350v1.full)
- A 2026 Nature cofolding-usability paper: co-folding methods "tend to predict inactive receptor structures, while most of our dataset comprises agonists (**228 out of 253 cases**)" — i.e., state mismatch is the central failure mode for agonist work — [Nature s44386-026-00066-1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44386-026-00066-1)
- AF3 vs experimental GPCRs: improved backbone over AF2 but "significant discrepancies persist in ligand-binding poses, **particularly for ions, peptides, and proteins**" — limiting functional/SBDD use — According to PubMed, [He et al., Acta Pharmacol Sin 2024, DOI 10.1038/s41401-024-01429-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41401-024-01429-y)
- Membrane representation: standard AF2/AF3 runs contain **no lipid bilayer, detergent, or nanodisc**; the reference active GLP1R structure 6X18 was solved with Gs + stabilizing Nb35 in a detergent/membrane-mimetic, none of which the co-folder represents. One workaround adds explicit lipid molecules to make an "artificial membrane," but this is non-standard — [insect-OR AF3 study (helios/PIIS2001037025003502, 2025)](https://helios.eie.gr/helios/bitstream/10442/19738/1/Thanu%20et%20al_2025_PIIS2001037025003502.pdf); [arXiv:2502.17628](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.17628)
- Placing DESIGNED binders: in the Baker de novo GPCR campaign, AF2 "initial guess" + Rosetta metrics showed **"no strong consistent trends"** separating binders from non-binders, forcing large experimental screens (binder hit rates ~**0.05–0.4%** of designs) — [bioRxiv 2025.03.23.644666v3](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.23.644666v3.full)
- GLP1R active structures used as design targets in that work: **"GLP1R (PDB IDs: 6VCB, 6X18, 7DUQ)"** — confirms 6X18 is a usable active-state template/target — [bioRxiv 2025.03.23.644666v3](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.23.644666v3.full)

### Inferences
- For GLP1R (class B1, large ECD + deep peptide-binding 7TM funnel), co-folding a de-novo binder and trusting the output state is unsafe: the model will tend to relax toward inactive/intermediate TM6/ICL3 regardless of the binder, so a "good-looking" co-fold does not evidence active-state stabilization.
- Protenix-v2 (local v2.0.0) and Boltz-1 are untested in the one rigorous 2026 GPCR state benchmark — no basis to assume they fix the inactive bias; treat them as unknowns, not solutions.
- Because the membrane is absent, any ECD-groove vs 7TM-pocket placement is in vacuo; lipid-mediated ECD restriction (e.g., GM3 at the ECD base, reported experimentally) is invisible to the model.

### Gaps
- No verifiable GPCR-**peptide**-complex active-state DockQ/success-rate broken out for AF3/Boltz-2/Chai-1/Protenix (the 2026 benchmark used ligands, not peptides).
- No published head-to-head of Protenix-v2 or Boltz-1 on GPCR state specificity.

## Key Question 2 — CAN AGONISM / active-state stabilization be PREDICTED in silico?

### Takeaway
No robust prospective in-silico agonism predictor exists for GPCRs, and none for class B/GLP1R: the SOTA de-novo GPCR design effort determined agonism EXPERIMENTALLY (no activation score), and the best dedicated predictors (DeorphaNN, Dynamic-GLEP) give only modest, retrospective, target-specific enrichment. Active-state template biasing and MD/TM6 metrics can rank efficacy classes after the fact but are not validated to prospectively call a de-novo design an agonist.

### Cited Findings
- **Baker de novo GPCR miniproteins (Nature 2026):** agonism was assessed by functional assays (Ca flux, cAMP, TRUPATH, β-arrestin), with **no in-silico efficacy/activation/Gs-recruitment score** described; the design hypothesis was that "active-state stabilization alone would be sufficient to generate agonists" (targeting active-state structures). This worked only for **class A** 7TM-pocket binders — MRGPRX1 (**7 agonists / 50 expressed ≈14%**, "partial rather than full agonists"), NK1R (**20 agonists, ~32% of expressed RFdiffusion hits**; 0 from MetaGen), CCR5 (1 agonist). For **class B (GLP1R, GIPR, GCGR, PTH1R, CGRPR, PAC1R) they obtained only ANTAGONISTS** (e.g., GLP1R antagonist mGI1_008 IC50 **39 ± 18 nM**), by ECD-blocking. Discussion: "Agonists have been particularly challenging to obtain due to the need for conformational selectivity." — [bioRxiv 2025.03.23.644666v3](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.23.644666v3.full)
- **DeorphaNN (Molecular Cell 2026):** best single in-silico discriminator of peptide agonism is weak — peptide-pLDDT mAP **0.32**, ipTM **0.26**, pocket-PAE **0.24** (random 0.035); AF-Multistate active-state templates raise peptide-pLDDT mAP **0.32→0.36** (p<0.05), more so for agonists; the GATv2 GNN reaches mAP **0.41** (retrospective C. elegans) and **0.72** on a human set (vs SpatialPPIv2 0.33). Pocket occupancy is NON-discriminative (**99.8% of agonists vs 96.6% of non-agonists** modeled in pocket). **No in-silico Gs/mini-G recruitment analysis**; the "anchor/driver toggle-switch" learning hypothesis is explicitly "untested." Uses AlphaFold-Multimer (not AF3/Boltz). — [bioRxiv 2025.03.19.644234](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.19.644234v1.full); [PubMed 42492505](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42492505/)
- **Dynamic-GLEP (ML efficacy, 2026):** MD conformational ensembles + transfer-learned equivariant GNN distinguish agonists from non-agonists: 5-HT1A AUC **0.74** CV / **0.71** external FDA set; A2A AUC **>0.85**; Holo ensembles best for scaffold optimization, Apo for chemical diversity — but small-molecule, class A, retrospective, and MD-dependent. — According to PubMed, [Chen et al., Brief Bioinform 2026, DOI 10.1093/bib/bbag049](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbag049)
- **Active- vs inactive-state TEMPLATE biasing works for structure, not efficacy:** AF-Multistate and shallow-MSA+active-template pipelines generate active-state models; PromptGPCR extends AF-Multimer/AF3 with biological "prompts" for both states and reports higher docking success — but none demonstrates prospective agonism calling. — [Biasing AF2, PMC9978208](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9978208/); According to PubMed, [Sun et al., CSBJ 2026, DOI 10.34133/csbj.0179](https://doi.org/10.34133/csbj.0179)
- **MD / TM6-outward metrics (retrospective efficacy ranking):** GaMD of M2 muscarinic (~19 µs) separated full vs partial vs inverse agonists via intracellular TM5/6/7 flexibility; β2AR smFRET shows "the extent of TM6 motions correlate with efficacy"; β2AR GaMD ties the active→inactive transition to the Arg3.50–Glu6.30 (DRY) salt bridge; an A2A study provides **p-values for agonist-dependent activation** from TM3–TM6 distance ANOVA. — [Miao & McCammon PNAS 2016](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.1614538113); [Gregorio et al. (β2AR smFRET), PMID 28607487](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28607487/); [β2AR GaMD, PMC9468641](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9468641/); [A2A activation statistics, Sci Rep 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-77072-4)
- **Mechanistic MD on AF models (examples, not predictors):** MasR partial-agonist mechanism from 1-µs MD of an AlphaFold model (TM6 stays compact ~10 Å, NPxxY activated — "decoupled" partial activation); 5-HT ligand insertion-DEPTH governs efficacy in MD. Both show efficacy is a subtle, pose-depth/microswitch phenomenon not captured by a single static co-fold. — According to PubMed, [Yasar et al. JMR 2026, DOI 10.1002/jmr.70031](https://doi.org/10.1002/jmr.70031); [Weber et al. Commun Chem 2026, DOI 10.1038/s42004-026-01936-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42004-026-01936-5)

### Inferences
- **Verdict: agonism is NOT reliably predictable in silico today, and least of all for class B/GLP1R.** The honest state of the art is: (a) binding can be designed and (weakly) scored; (b) agonism is obtained by stabilizing the active state and then measured experimentally; (c) in-silico efficacy signals (active-state-template pLDDT, DeorphaNN-type GNNs, MD/TM6 metrics) are at best modest prioritization aids, retrospective, and unvalidated for de-novo designs.
- Co-folding the design + receptor + mini-Gs to "see Gs recruitment" is NOT an established efficacy readout — no surveyed method uses it, and co-folders already fail to hold the active state; a recruited-Gs co-fold would be an artifact of templating, not evidence of efficacy.
- The most defensible in-silico efficacy proxy for GLP1R would be an active-state-templated model (6X18) scored for whether the design engages the activation-critical class B motifs (peptide N-terminus into the deep TM core, TM6 kink/PxxG, central polar network, NPxxY/HETx), then explicit-state MD (GaMD/metadynamics) to test active-state stability — but this is an expensive, unvalidated hypothesis-generator, not a predictor.

### Gaps
- No prospective, experimentally validated in-silico agonism predictor for any class B GPCR; none specific to de-novo protein (vs small-molecule/peptide) agonists.
- No surveyed study tests whether in-silico mini-Gs/Gs co-recruitment predicts efficacy (string-method/Markov-state efficacy work for GLP1R ECD exists but addresses ECD opening, not de-novo agonist efficacy).
- DeorphaNN/Dynamic-GLEP generalization to de-novo designed binders is untested.

## Key Question 3 — Interface metrics (ipTM, pLDDT, PAE, ipSAE, pDockQ, contacts): reliability for membrane interfaces, controls, defensible thresholds

### Takeaway
Confidence/interface metrics are weakly informative for GPCR complexes and essentially uncalibrated for membrane proteins; no absolute threshold is validated for GPCRs, so per-target calibration against POSITIVE (native agonist) and NEGATIVE (scramble) controls is mandatory, and metrics discriminate binding far better than agonism.

### Cited Findings
- In the 2026 GPCR/kinase benchmark, **protein pLDDT vs pose accuracy Pearson r = +0.04** (no correlation); ligand pLDDT vs ligand RMSD r = −0.46 (weak) — confidence does NOT track whether the state/pose is correct — [bioRxiv 2026.05.06.723350](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.06.723350v1.full)
- For discriminating peptide AGONISM, DeorphaNN found peptide-pLDDT (mAP 0.32) > ipTM (0.26) > pocket-PAE (0.24); all far below a useful classifier; **pocket occupancy is non-discriminative** — [bioRxiv 2025.03.19.644234](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.19.644234v1.full)
- **ipSAE** separates true from false predicted complexes more reliably than ipTM (which dilutes interface confidence over disordered/accessory regions) and is recommended for **designed binders**; ipTM "is fine" for natural complexes — [Dunbrack bioRxiv 2025.02.10.637595 (via foldmetrics/neurosnap)](https://pypi.org/project/foldmetrics/)
- Community (NON-validated, soluble-derived) thresholds: **ipSAE_min >0.61 (standard) / >0.70 (stringent); pDockQ >0.5/0.6; LIS >0.35/0.45**; PAE cutoff 10–12 Å for interface metrics; ESMFold gives no PAE so ipSAE is uncomputable there — [adaptyvbio ipsae skill](https://skills.sh/adaptyvbio/protein-design-skills/ipsae); [neurosnap ipsae docs](https://neurosnap.ai/docs/neurosnap.algos.ipsae.html)
- No surveyed source provides membrane-protein-specific validation of ipTM/pDockQ/ipSAE thresholds — all were derived on soluble complexes — [WebSearch synthesis; no membrane benchmark found](https://pypi.org/project/foldmetrics/)

### Inferences
- Defensible scoring stack for the GLP1R challenge: (1) co-fold design+GLP1R with an **active-state template (6X18)**; (2) report **ipSAE_min and interface pLDDT/PAE** but treat thresholds as relative, not absolute; (3) **calibrate per-target** by co-folding POSITIVE controls — GLP-1(7-36)NH2, exendin-4, and the known small-molecule/peptide agonists — and NEGATIVE controls (scrambled/ reversed design sequence), requiring the design to beat scramble and approach native-agonist interface scores; (4) use **sampling + ensembling** (many seeds/diffusion samples; AF3+Boltz-2+Protenix-v2 consensus) since ranking, not sampling, is the bottleneck (transfers from antibody-interface literature).
- Because metrics discriminate binding ≫ agonism, interface scores can only gate "is this a credible GLP1R binder in the active-state pocket," never "is this an agonist" — that needs experiment.

### Gaps
- No GPCR/membrane-calibrated ipSAE/pDockQ cutoff; the per-target-control approach is an informed inference, not a benchmarked protocol.
- No published ipSAE/pDockQ values for native GLP-1/exendin co-folds to anchor a positive-control band (would need to be generated locally).

## Key Question 4 — Docking into the 7TM orthosteric pocket and handling the micelle/membrane

### Takeaway
Docking small molecules into the 7TM pocket is a hypothesis-generator with many false positives even on AF models; peptide/protein placement is worse; and the membrane/micelle is simply absent from co-folders, so lipid-mediated effects and the detergent environment of 6X18 are unmodeled.

### Cited Findings
- AI GPCR models generally predict overall structure well but "essential details for computational ligand docking, such as the exact location of amino acid side chains within the binding pocket, are not predicted with the necessary accuracy"; docking is "a hypothesis generator rather than a precise predictor," yielding many false positives — According to PubMed, [Chung et al., Biomolecules 2025, DOI 10.3390/biom15030423](https://doi.org/10.3390/biom15030423)
- GLP1R activation is a two-domain class B mechanism: peptide C-terminus binds the ECD ("affinity trap") enabling the N-terminus to insert into and open the TMD; ECD closes in the bound state — the orthosteric "pocket" is a deep peptide funnel, not a small-molecule cavity — [GLP1R ECD MD/Markov study, PMID 30957116](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30957116/); [intrinsic ECD agonist mechanism, Nature celldisc201642](https://www.nature.com/articles/celldisc201642)
- Membrane/lipid handling: standard co-folds have no bilayer; adding explicit lipids to form an "artificial membrane" is a non-standard workaround; experimentally, plasma-membrane lipids (e.g., GM3 at the ECD base) modulate class B1 GPCRs including GLP1R — invisible to co-folders — [insect-OR AF3 lipid study 2025](https://helios.eie.gr/helios/bitstream/10442/19738/1/Thanu%20et%20al_2025_PIIS2001037025003502.pdf); [class B1 lipid modulation, bioRxiv 2024.12.19.629375](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.12.19.629375v2.full)
- GPR101 case: a homology model built on a **G-protein-bound template** predicted TM6 more accurately than AI methods — i.e., for the activation-defining TM6, a complex (active, Gs-bound) template beats default AI — According to PubMed, [Costanzi et al., J Mol Graph Model 2025, DOI 10.1016/j.jmgm.2025.109103](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmgm.2025.109103)

### Inferences
- For GLP1R, a de-novo AGONIST almost certainly must engage the deep TM core the way the GLP-1 N-terminus does (to open TM6), which is exactly where co-folders are least reliable and where even the Baker lab did not succeed (they went ECD → antagonist). An ECD-groove binder is far more tractable to design and score but, per Baker, yields an antagonist, not an agonist.
- If 7TM-pocket agonism is attempted, the pipeline should: co-fold on 6X18 (and the GLP1R:Gs complex) to supply the active TM6 geometry, add explicit lipids or at least acknowledge the missing membrane, and treat docking/placement as hypothesis generation to be confirmed by MD and then wet-lab function.

### Gaps
- No tool surveyed models the detergent/micelle/nanodisc of 6X18; no validated protocol for membrane-aware GPCR co-folding of a designed binder.
- No benchmark of de-novo binder placement into the GLP1R 7TM funnel specifically.

## (A) Takeaways (incl. "can we predict agonism?" verdict)

1. **Co-folders default to INACTIVE and ignore the membrane.** AF3/Boltz-2/Chai-1/RF3 pull agonist-bound GPCRs toward inactive/intermediate geometry (inactive AF2 TM-RMSD 1.33 Å vs active 1.86 Å; a 2026 Nature set is 228/253 agonists yet co-folders predict inactive), and none represent lipid/detergent. A good-looking co-fold is not evidence of active-state stabilization. [Heo & Feig; bioRxiv 2026.05.06.723350; Nature s44386-026-00066-1]
2. **CAN WE PREDICT AGONISM? No — not reliably, and not at all for class B/GLP1R.** The SOTA de-novo GPCR design effort (Baker, Nature 2026) used NO in-silico efficacy score: it stabilized the active state with active-state templates and then measured agonism experimentally; it produced class A 7TM-pocket agonists (~14–32% of expressed binders) but **only ANTAGONISTS for every class B receptor including GLP1R** (GLP1R antagonist IC50 39 nM; 6X18 was a target). In-silico agonism signals (active-state-template pLDDT, DeorphaNN GNN mAP 0.41–0.72, Dynamic-GLEP AUC 0.71–0.85, GaMD/TM6 rankings) are modest, retrospective, target-specific, and unvalidated for de-novo designs. [bioRxiv 2025.03.23.644666; Mol Cell 2026; Brief Bioinform 2026]
3. **Binding ≫ agonism for every metric.** pLDDT does not track pose/state correctness (r=+0.04); for agonism, the best single metric (peptide-pLDDT mAP 0.32) barely beats chance and pocket occupancy is non-discriminative. Interface scores can gate "credible active-state binder," never "agonist." [bioRxiv 2026.05.06.723350; Mol Cell 2026]
4. **Recommended scoring/validation stack (honest, hypothesis-generating):** co-fold design+GLP1R on an **active-state template (6X18 / GLP1R:Gs)** across AF3 + Boltz-2 (2.2.1) + Protenix-v2 (2.0.0) with many seeds; score with **ipSAE_min + interface pLDDT/PAE** (ipSAE > ipTM for designed binders); **calibrate thresholds per-target against POSITIVE controls (GLP-1(7-36)NH2, exendin-4, orforglipron) and NEGATIVE scramble controls** rather than absolute cutoffs; then test active-state stability with explicit-state **GaMD/metadynamics** (TM6-outward, NPxxY, central polar network, PxxG kink). Treat all of it as triage; functional assays (cAMP/TRUPATH/β-arrestin) are the only agonism verdict. [Dunbrack; adaptyvbio ipsae; Miao PNAS 2016]
5. **Class B/GLP1R-specific hard truth.** GLP1R activation needs a peptide-like N-terminus inserted into the deep TM core to open TM6 — precisely where co-folders are least reliable and where even Baker's pipeline failed to agonize (defaulting to ECD antagonists). A de-novo GLP1R AGONIST is at or beyond the current frontier; an ECD/7TM binder is designable but will most likely read out as an antagonist/null unless it actively opens TM6. [bioRxiv 2025.03.23.644666; GLP1R mechanism refs]

## (B) Gaps
- No prospective, experimentally validated in-silico agonism/efficacy predictor for any class B GPCR, and none for de-novo protein agonists.
- No membrane-calibrated ipSAE/pDockQ/ipTM thresholds; the positive/negative-control calibration protocol is an informed inference, not a benchmark.
- Protenix-v2 and Boltz-1 untested in the one rigorous 2026 GPCR state benchmark; no GPCR-peptide-complex DockQ for any 2026 co-folder.
- Whether in-silico Gs/mini-Gs co-recruitment indicates efficacy is unstudied (and mechanistically suspect given state-bias).
- Native GLP-1/exendin co-fold interface-score bands not yet generated locally (needed to anchor positive controls).

## (C) Confidence
- **High confidence:** co-folders default to inactive and omit membrane; pLDDT/ipTM poorly track GPCR state and agonism; binding is far more predictable than agonism; experimental functional assays remain mandatory. (Multiple independent 2024–2026 peer-reviewed + benchmark sources agree.)
- **High confidence** that the Baker effort produced class A agonists but only class B antagonists and used no in-silico efficacy score (consistent across preprint v3 and Nature 2026), though exact class-B rationale text was partly truncated in fetch.
- **Medium confidence** on specific metric numbers from 2026 preprints/benchmarks (DeorphaNN mAPs, state-benchmark r-values, Dynamic-GLEP AUCs) — single-group, not independently reproduced; treat as directional.
- **Medium-low confidence** on the exact defensible thresholds — community values are soluble-derived and uncalibrated for membrane/GPCR; per-target control calibration is a reasoned recommendation, not a validated standard.
