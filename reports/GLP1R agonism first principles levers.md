# Empirical tests sink two GLP1R agonism rankers

**Bottom line up front:** The 2026-10-13 survey (this folder's companion report, *GLP1R de novo agonist design*) concluded that class-B agonism "cannot be reliably predicted in silico" and left open whether first-principles physics could still *rank* it; this round built and tested the two most promising levers, and both failed. The **two-state binding-free-energy differential** (L1: intrinsic efficacy = ΔΔG(active−inactive) — the single most-validated structure-based efficacy proxy in the small-molecule class-A literature) ranked candidates by **binding strength, not agonism**: the orthosteric **antagonist exendin(9-39) scored HIGHEST** of eight forced co-folds and the weak-binding scramble lowest, reproducing the exact binding≠activation confound the metric exists to break [in-silico]. The **elastic-network / normal-mode allostery** lever (L2) failed for a concrete mechanistic reason — GLP1R's active−inactive transition is a **localized TM6 kink at the PxxG motif, not a soft global mode**, so an elastic-network model of the active TM domain reconstructs the activation vector with only **0.26 best-single-mode overlap** [in-silico]. The design-side levers fared no better: the de-novo **two-domain (address+message) agonist is DOWNGRADED** (geometric register control for an ECD→TM-spanning single chain is unprecedented), and the **His7-surrogate pharmacophore was DROPPED** (it converges on the native, eligibility-risky motif). No in-silico agonism oracle survives. Critically, these NO-GOs are not setbacks but **confirmations** of the project invariant: binding is computable, activation is not, and **only the cell-based cAMP/CRE-SEAP assay proves a class-B agonist.**

## Four levers tested; the scorecard shows no surviving agonism oracle

| Lever | Cross-domain root | Verdict | Why it failed (or stalled) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L1** two-state differential | Efficacy = ΔΔG(active−inactive); MWC / ternary-complex thermodynamics | **NO-GO (empirical)** | Interface-confidence differential ranks **binding**; the orthosteric antagonist scored highest |
| **L2** normal-mode / ENM allostery | Elastic-network soft modes carry the activation motion | **NO-GO (empirical)** | Class-B activation is a **localized TM6 kink**, not a soft ENM mode (overlap 0.26) |
| **L3** de-novo two-domain agonist | Message–address peptide pharmacology (Schwyzer/Portoghese) | **DOWNGRADE** | Register control for an ECD→TM single chain is unsolved/unprecedented |
| **L4** His7-surrogate pharmacophore | Minimal activating pharmacophore abstraction | **DROPPED** | Converges on the native His7 motif (eligibility risk); subtopic terminated |

What this round adds to the 2026-10-13 survey is empirical rather than bibliographic. That survey had already demoted co-folding interface scores from "selection criterion" to "binding triage with controls" on the strength of co-folder-bias arguments and the absence of any validated agonism oracle; this round **ran the controls and watched the firewall hold.** The three new contributions are (1) a documented, reproducible **two-state inactive-template recipe** and its NO-GO result, (2) an **ENM/PRS verdict** grounded in GLP1R's own measured activation geometry, and (3) a tool-provenance finding that bears on the gated submission. Each lever below keeps the project's mandatory distinction: *does the candidate bind* (computable, well-supported) is held strictly apart from *does the bound candidate drive the Gαs switch* (the part no score settles).

## The two-state differential had the best precedent — and still ranked binding

The theory is the cleanest in all of receptor pharmacology. Classical two-state / ternary-complex / MWC models converge on one identity: a ligand's **intrinsic efficacy is its differential affinity for the active (R\*) versus inactive (R) state**, `ΔΔG_eff = ΔG_bind(R*) − ΔG_bind(R)`, which a standard thermodynamic cycle (Wyman linkage) shows equals the free-energy shift the ligand imposes on the activation equilibrium — the formal bridge from a *bindable* quantity to an *activation* quantity [PR] ([MWC, Monod-Wyman-Changeux 1965](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0022-2836(65)80285-6); [Leff two-state 1995](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0165-6147(00)88989-0); [Black & Leff operational model 1983](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.1983.0093)). The sharp statement of binding≠activation falls straight out: an agonist has ΔΔG_eff ≪ 0, while a **neutral antagonist has ΔΔG_eff ≈ 0 — it binds both states equally, so it has affinity but no efficacy.** For GLP1R specifically, activation requires the peptide's N-terminal "message" to make contacts that exist only in R* (TM6 kinked out, central polar network engaged), so a rim/ECD-only binder is an antagonist by construction — matching the prior survey's "folded de-novo binder perches on the rim."

The retrospective precedent is genuinely strong, which is why the lever was worth testing. A two-state **docking** ΔScore (active-minus-inactive) separated β2-adrenergic agonists from blockers at **ROC AUC 0.98 / ≈95% accuracy** [PR] ([Costanzi & Vilar, PMC3265623](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3265623/)); conformation-selective docking to active-state β2AR "exclusively yields agonists" at a 37% hit rate [PR] ([Scharf 2019](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/output/3331208/comparative-docking-to-distinct-g-proteincoupled-receptor-conformations-exclusively-yields-ligands-with-agonist-efficacy)); A2A FEP cycles comparing ΔΔG(R\*−R) matched Δ-efficacy in **up to 70% of pair comparisons** [PR, in-silico] ([Jespers 2021](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009152)); and two-state MD free-energy simulations **prospectively designed and synthesized novel nanomolar partial β2AR agonists** [PR] ([Panel 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.202218959)). The decisive caveats, all noted before the test: every validated instance is **small-molecule, class A, with pristine active+inactive crystals, and retrospective** — transfer to de-novo *peptides* at a *class-B* 7TM core was explicitly an untested extrapolation [HYP], and DeorphaNN had already shown that raw AlphaFold-Multimer confidence gives only "partial discrimination" of agonist complexes [pre] ([bioRxiv 2025.03.19.644234](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.19.644234v1)).

The recipe used the project's existing stack: Boltz-2's documented hard template-enforcement (`force: true`, small `threshold`) to force each ligand onto the **active template 6X18** (WT-like, 2.1 Å) [PR] ([RCSB 6X18](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/6X18); [Zhang 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2020.09.020)) and onto the **inactive template 6LN2** (full-length, NAM-bound) [PR] ([PMC7062719](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7062719/)), with 5VEW/5VEX as TMD-only backups [PR] ([RCSB 5VEW](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/5VEW); [Song 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22378)). The known weak link going in: **every experimental inactive GLP1R is NAM-occluded and thermostabilized** — an engineered inactive state, not a pristine apo ground state.

**The result (2026-10-14) was a NO-GO on method-qualification** [in-silico]. Across eight forced co-folds (four controls × two states, one GPU), the interface-ipTM differential (active−inactive) was **GLP-1(7-36) +0.648, exendin-4 +0.621, exendin(9-39) antagonist +0.735, scramble +0.417**. The agonist mean (+0.634) barely edged the non-agonist mean (+0.576), a gap of **+0.058 that is noise at n=1** — and, fatally, **the orthosteric antagonist scored the HIGHEST and the weak-binding scramble the lowest.** The metric ranked affinity, not efficacy: the exact binding≠activation confound. This was a genuine method failure rather than a setup artifact, because forcing *did* produce distinct states (active-vs-inactive receptor-fold RMSD 23–28 Å). The structural causes are instructive — the only available inactive templates are NAM-occluded, so every orthosteric peptide simply fits the better-formed active pocket, and a global interface-confidence differential cannot read the TM6-drive that *defines* agonism. The decision was **not to escalate to MM/GBSA**: the same occluded-pocket, no-activation-readout causes would recur, and the result confirms rather than contradicts the agonism-unprovable prior, so the cost was unjustified.

## Normal-mode allostery misfires on a localized TM6 kink

The second lever asked whether cheap elastic-network dynamics could score the *mechanical* coupling from the peptide site to the TM6 activation lever — an orthogonal, CPU-only signal rather than a binding-confidence score. The literature is encouraging but entirely class A or non-GPCR: elastic-network low-frequency modes of M2/M3, A2A, β2 and CXCR4 recover the vestibule-contraction↔intracellular-pocket-opening collective motion that accompanies activation [PR, in-silico] ([Kolan 2014](https://doi.org/10.1002/prot.24426)); coarse-grained NMA (ENCoM) predicts μ-opioid-receptor ligand efficacy at **~3 CPU-seconds per complex and generalizes to novel chemistry where a binding-contact-only model fails** — the key binding-vs-activation separation [PR, in-silico] ([Galdino 2024](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.4c00788)); and GPCRact classifies agonist/antagonist/non-binder at **0.819 balanced accuracy** using an allosteric site defined as the top-100 apo→holo displacement residues [PR, in-silico] ([PMC12805254](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12805254/)). But three warnings were flagged in advance: rhodopsin's modes already fail to transfer; **no ENM/NMA activation study exists for GLP1R or any class B receptor**; and a magnitude-only overlap flags an inactive-stabilizing antagonist as a false positive unless the *signed* direction toward active is enforced.

**The test (CPU-only, numpy ANM) was a NO-GO** [in-silico], and it localized the failure precisely. Building the active−inactive Cα deformation vector (active 6X18 chain R vs inactive 6LN2 chain A, SVD superposition with iterative core-trim) first exposed a trap: the **naive whole-receptor vector is dominated by ECD motion (mean 23 Å, max 35 Å)** — the extracellular domain swinging between peptide-free and peptide-bound — which is *not* the activation signal. Restricting to the TM bundle (residues 136–423) and superposing on the stable TM core (core-RMSD 1.26 Å) isolated the true signature: **max displacement 18.8 Å at residue 346, the TM6 cytoplasmic tip — matching the ~18 Å TM6 swing of class-B activation** [PR] ([Zhang 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22394)). The NO-GO is then unambiguous: a Cα-ANM of the active TM domain (cutoff 15 Å; the six rigid-body modes correctly near-zero) **does not encode activation as a soft mode** — best single nontrivial mode overlap with the activation vector = **0.26**, cumulative overlap 0.10 (1 mode), 0.52 (10 modes), 0.69 (50 modes). The mechanistic reason [HYP, grounded]: class-B activation is a *localized TM6 kink at PxxG*, not a global breathing mode, so ENM/PRS — which excels at global-soft-mode allostery — is simply the wrong physics here, consistent with the literature finding that no single soft mode reproduces a GPCR inactive→active path. The lever was dropped **before** the gated ProDy install; the mandatory mode-overlap sanity check (recipe step 2) did exactly its job and saved the compute.

## Design levers narrow: two-domain downgraded, pharmacophore dropped

On the design side (how to *build* an agonist, as opposed to *rank* one), the two first-principles levers both recede, leaving the prior survey's recommendation intact. The **de-novo two-domain "address+message" agonist (L3) is DOWNGRADED** because the hardest sub-problem — holding a de-novo *message* at the correct depth under GLP1R's Trp33 cap while a de-novo *address* docks the ECD — is **genuinely unprecedented**: no published de-novo GPCR agonist has an engineered geometric register, and the only protein precedents that control an inter-module geometry (de-novo cytokine agonists tuned by dimerization geometry, e.g. G-CSFR) [PR] ([PMC12628175](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12628175/)) *cluster* receptor chains rather than *thread* a pocket. Tellingly, the one existence proof of de-novo class-B agonism — EvoBind's dual GCGR/GLP1R cyclic peptides, **18-mer EC50 ≈ 32 nM at GLP1R, G-protein biased, "no similarity to known agonists"** — reached activation with a single compact pocket-threading peptide and **no two-domain architecture at all** [pre] ([bioRxiv 2025.06.06.658268](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.06.658268)). The on-target published idea for "prefer active, reject inactive" design (AlloGen's generator-agnostic differential-state scorer; SwitchCraft's negative-allostery primitive) remains **in-silico-only, un-validated on any GPCR, with no released code or license** [pre] ([AlloGen, ICML 2026](https://openreview.net/forum?id=OOfdPJlcN1); [SwitchCraft, arXiv:2605.31236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31236)). So the design frontier is unchanged from the 2026-10-13 survey: the **compact, sequence-novel, pocket-threading peptide/mini-protein (EvoBind-style rediscovery, not motif-grafting) is the only route with a class-B agonism precedent**, and Baker's class-B binders being antagonists [PR] ([Nature 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10656-8)) still delimits the difficulty.

The **His7-surrogate pharmacophore (L4) was DROPPED** for two reasons: its research subtopic was terminated by a `[bio]` classifier, and it carries the highest eligibility risk of any lever — abstracting the minimal activating pharmacophore converges on the native His7 motif, whose cationic imidazole is indispensable for activation [PR] ([Adelhorst 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8119974/); [Endocrine J 1997](https://doi.org/10.1507/endocrj.44.701)), and recreating it risks the "modified known agonist" red line the challenge forbids.

One operational finding affects the user-gated submission: **EvoBind — the generator behind the top agonism arm — carries no LICENSE file (verified first-hand) and its published protocol is CC-BY-NC**, conflicting with the project's ODC-BY release invariant for submitted designs. Clean permissive alternatives are installed and confirmed first-hand — **DiffPepBuilder (MIT)** and **RFdiffusion (BSD)** — so a license-clean agonism arm is available as a de-risking hedge.

## Conclusion — both failures confirm the firewall

The deepest result of this round is that **the two independent rankers failed in the same way**: each measured something real but orthogonal to agonism. The two-state differential measured *binding geometry*, so the antagonist that fits both states beat the agonist; the elastic-network model measured *global mechanical coupling*, so it was blind to the localized PxxG kink that actually is the activation drive. Neither captured the *signed thermodynamic push toward R\**. This is the binding/activation firewall expressing itself twice, through completely different physics — and it converges with the prior survey's purely bibliographic verdict (Baker used no in-silico efficacy score at all; DeorphaNN only partially discriminates). The empirical NO-GOs therefore **strengthen, not weaken**, the governing thesis: for a class-B receptor, no in-silico score proves agonism; only the cAMP/CRE-SEAP assay does.

Practically, this closes a question rather than opening one. The project should **stop trying to rank agonism in silico**, use co-folds only to gate "credible active-state binder" against controls, and spend the ≤20 submission slots on *diversity within the one precedented lineage* — compact, sequence-novel, pocket-threading peptides/mini-proteins built on the active-state 6X18 template, each an independent bet, with the cAMP/CRE-SEAP assay as the sole arbiter. The frontier question is no longer "which score ranks class-B agonism" — that is answered, and the answer is *none of the cheap, first-principles ones tested here* — but "how many orthogonal, eligibility-clean pocket-threading bets can be fit into twenty slots." The CRE-SEAP amplification remains the quiet ally: a merely partial agonist that nudges TM6 can still read positive, which is the realistic shape of a first-of-its-kind result at this frontier.
